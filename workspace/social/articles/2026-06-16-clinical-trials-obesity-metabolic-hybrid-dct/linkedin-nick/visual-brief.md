## Format decision
**Recommended:** infographic (single static image, 2-pair pain-point → outcome card) — per the post's own Design tip, not overridden.
**Why:** The post's Design tip is explicit and narrower than the company version's: "compact two-column visual (**pain point → outcome**)," not a full multi-row operational schema. This profile is Nick's personal LinkedIn feed (BD, USA) and the post itself is framed as a first-person, recurring observation from his own buyer conversations ("Every clinical trial buyer conversation I have..."), not a company-level audit of trial operations. A tighter, more conversational 2-pair card fits that voice better than a dense 3-row ops table, and keeps the asset visually distinct from the company account's post on the same article.
**Dimensions:** 1200×1200 px (LinkedIn feed square — safest crop across desktop/mobile for both personal and company posts). Export a 1600×1600 master for reuse.

## Visual concept
**Big idea (1 sentence):** The line item every buyer conversation lands on — "the measurement-only visit" — resolves into two concrete shifts (visit burden and documentation), read as a personal insight, not a product comparison chart.
**Mood:** observational, first-person, still professional — closer to "an operator sharing a pattern they keep seeing" than to a corporate one-pager. Same dark teal-blue "trial infrastructure" register as the article banner (per the post's own "Keep" instruction), but the framing device (a quoted observation, not a labeled comparison table) signals personal voice rather than company messaging.

**How this differs from the linkedin-company infographic (same article):** The company asset builds a 3-row "Manual vs. FitXpress" comparison schema across Site consistency, Coordinator workload, and Remote use, with two labeled columns and left/right icon pairs per row — a compliance-audience audit graphic. This one uses only 2 rows, both intentionally different from the company version's three (Participant burden and Documentation, not Site consistency/Coordinator workload/Remote use), pulled from the parts of the article table that map directly onto Nick's own post copy rather than the general ops framing. It also opens with a quoted personal line ("The measurement-only visit") instead of a neutral title, and uses a pain→outcome arrow flow per row instead of a column-header comparison table. The multi-site dot-map motif from the OG banner is dropped entirely (Nick's post doesn't make a multi-site-distribution point) — only the guided-scan and timestamp icons carry over, per the post's "Keep" note. Net effect: same brand visual language, different rows, different structural device, different opening line — not a re-skin of the company asset.

## Composition
- Full-bleed dark teal-blue background (see Colors below) — flat or very subtle gradient within the brand palette, no purple-pink AI-style gradient. Same palette family as the article OG banner and the company LinkedIn asset, per the post's explicit "Keep" instruction — but note this is a distinct composition, not the same layout.
- Top third: a short quoted headline, set apart with a large opening quotation mark or a thin rule above/below it, reading as Nick's own observation rather than a company tagline:
  - **"The measurement-only visit."**
  - Small kicker line beneath, smaller/muted: "A recurring line in buyer conversations."
- Middle: two stacked pain-point → outcome rows, each laid out as a horizontal flow (not a two-column table grid):
  - **Row 1 — Participant burden:** left, muted/gray text + small abstract "site visit" glyph (a simple pin/location mark, dimmed) — "Measurement-only site visit" → center, a small arrow glyph (→) in 3DLOOK Blue → right, brighter text + the guided-scan phone icon (pose-validation silhouette/bounding-box guide from the OG banner, at small scale) — "Folded into a visit already scheduled"
  - **Row 2 — Documentation:** left, muted/gray text + small abstract "manual notes" glyph (a few short irregular marks, not a literal notepad icon) — "Manual notes, site by site" → center, arrow glyph in 3DLOOK Blue → right, brighter text + the timestamp/structured-record icon from the OG banner (small rows of even marks) — "Same time-stamped record, every site"
  - No vertical or horizontal ruled gridlines between the two rows — generous spacing instead, so this reads as a short list of two observations, not a data table.
- Bottom: 3DLOOK or FitXpress wordmark badge, small, bottom-right, consistent with other content-hub visuals. No logo file currently in `brand-assets/` — designer to source current lockup from the Figma website file or confirm with Vadim before placing.
- Do not add a headshot, avatar placeholder, or any photo of Nick — no such asset exists in `brand-assets/`, and inventing one would misrepresent a real person's likeness. If Vadim wants a personal-profile photo treatment in a future iteration, that requires him to supply the actual headshot; flag it as an open question rather than placeholder art.
- Generous margins around the whole block; keep it legible at LinkedIn feed thumbnail size on mobile — this needs to work as a small, quick read, more so than the company version's denser 3-row grid.

## Colors

Same background family as the linkedin-company asset is fine — it's the row content, labeling, and layout device that differentiate the two, not the base palette.
- Primary accent (arrow glyphs, outcome-side text and icons, headline): 3DLOOK Blue `#2962FF` (marked approximate in `colors.md` — confirm before final export).
- Pain-point side text/icons: Mid Gray `#6B7280` (approximate) or White at reduced opacity — deliberately more muted than the outcome side.
- Body/label text: White `#FFFFFF` at high opacity, or Light Gray `#F5F5F7` for the kicker line.
- Do not introduce Red Alert `#FF3B30` or Green Success `#34C759` — the pain-point vs. outcome contrast should be carried by the gray-vs-blue treatment above, not by red/green pass-fail coloring (which would overstate a status/verification claim the article doesn't make).

## Typography
- Headline (quoted line): Inter Bold or Black (per `fonts.md`) — large, tight tracking.
- Kicker: Inter Regular or Medium, small, muted.
- Row text (pain-point and outcome phrases): Inter Medium, consistent size on both sides — only the color/weight-of-emphasis should differ (outcome side can be Bold for slightly more visual weight; pain-point side stays Regular/Medium), not the type size.
- Max 2 font weights beyond the headline; no third typeface.
