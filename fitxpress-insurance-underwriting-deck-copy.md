# FitXpress for Insurance Underwriting — Complete Presentation Copy

---

## PRESENTATION STRATEGY

**New use case:**
Mobile 3D body scanning as a structured digital evidence layer for accelerated life insurance underwriting — supporting BMI/build verification, applicant triage, and fraud-prevention workflows without replacing underwriter judgment.

**Primary audience:**
Life insurance carriers, reinsurers, and digital distribution platforms — specifically underwriting operations leaders, chief underwriters, digital transformation teams, and product managers responsible for accelerated underwriting programs.

**Core problem:**
Accelerated underwriting has reduced friction and shortened application timelines, but it creates a practical evidentiary gap: insurers still need reliable body-related risk signals when applicants no longer complete every step in person. Self-reported height, weight, BMI, and build data can be incomplete, inconsistent, or intentionally misrepresented — directly affecting risk classification, pricing accuracy, fraud exposure, and case routing. Existing alternatives (paramedical exams, APS requests, manual cross-checks) reintroduce the very friction and delay that accelerated programs were designed to eliminate.

**Primary value proposition:**
FitXpress by 3DLOOK enables insurers to collect structured, repeatable body-related data remotely through a guided smartphone scan. Two photos and a ~45-second flow produce 80+ external body measurements, BMI insights, and weight prediction — outputs designed to serve as supporting evidence within accelerated underwriting, applicant verification, and underwriter review workflows. The technology supports faster evidence collection, better applicant triage, and stronger fraud-prevention signals without turning into a standalone underwriting decision engine.

**Key narrative:**
The deck opens by framing the evidentiary challenge that accelerated underwriting creates — faster decisions require reliable data, but traditional evidence collection methods are too slow and self-report is too unreliable. It then positions FitXpress as a practical support layer: smartphone-based body scanning that produces structured, auditable digital evidence to strengthen underwriting review. The narrative walks through the specific workflow (pre-screening → evidence collection → applicant verification → accelerated routing), demonstrates accuracy and repeatability validated for production use, explains the integration architecture that keeps the insurer in control of decisioning, and closes with deployment paths from pilot evaluation through enterprise rollout.
