## facebook-company

In some studies of glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) treatment, lean mass made up 40% to 60% of the weight lost. In others, it was approximately 15% or less.

Both ranges sit in one 2024 review, by Neeland, Linge and Birkenfeld. It adds a second point. Lean mass is not muscle: it also covers organs, bone, fluids and water in fat tissue.

These are findings across study groups. They do not establish what one member's loss was made of. If an app quotes one range to members, it should quote both.

What can the app do? A 2025 joint advisory from four obesity, nutrition and lifestyle medicine groups supports strength training, alongside adequate protein intake. An app that offers workouts can support one part of that guidance.

Our new six-minute video opens on a member's scale reading and shows the scan on real footage. Then it walks through this research, and what a progress view can show next to the training record.

## linkedin-company

The first result a body scan pilot in a fitness app should report is whether members complete their scans.

🎥 Our new six-minute video shows a FitXpress scan from the member's side, on real footage. Two phone photos go in, structured results come back in under 45 seconds, and the app can show two scans side by side.

For a member on glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) treatment, a logged weight loss does not show whether fat mass, lean mass, or fluid made up the change. A fitness app can add circumference measurements and composition estimates to the training record at each check-in.

A chapter near the end of the video covers how to test this in a pilot. Before launch, the app team defines the pilot period, eligible members and success criteria. Then it tracks five signals:

• capture completion
• retakes
• the share of members with a valid baseline and follow-up scan
• scheduled check-in completion
• use of the progress view

Where possible, check-in behavior is set against a comparable pre-launch period or cohort. Member and coach feedback is reviewed before a wider rollout.

The effect of visual progress tracking on adherence and retention requires separate evaluation. We think a pilot report should say this up front and lead with those five signals.

FitXpress is not a medical device. Treatment decisions remain with the prescribing clinician.
