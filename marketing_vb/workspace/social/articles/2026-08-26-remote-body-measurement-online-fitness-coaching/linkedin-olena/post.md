Body scans are personal data. That single fact decides how far a remote program scales.

Coaches think about the measurements. Compliance teams think about the photos. Both are right, and they need different answers.

One thing is worth settling before launch: separate the input from the output. The two smartphone photos are input. The measurements and 3D model are output. They follow different retention rules.

With FitXpress, input photos are deleted immediately after processing, or kept for up to 30 days on the client's instruction. Any retained photo is automatically blurred. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.

Consent is not the vendor's job. In most enterprise deployments, the customer acts as controller and 3DLOOK acts as processor under GDPR. The customer establishes the legal basis and obtains consent. The technology does not do that for you.

Before a pilot, I'd ask: who owns consent, and how long does each data type live? 🔒
