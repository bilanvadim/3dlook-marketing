A high scan-completion rate can still hide a program that isn't working.

Completion counts how many clients started a capture. It says nothing about whether you got a pair you can compare.

The number that decides a remote measurement program is the usable comparison rate: clients with both a valid baseline and a valid follow-up. One good scan is a record. Two, captured the same way, are a trend.

Before scaling, I'd run a pilot built around that pair rate. Track the usable comparison rate, coach review time per check-in, and retakes. Those three tell you whether the workflow survives a full roster.

FitXpress handles the standardized capture and the scan-to-scan comparison. The discipline is yours: consistent capture conditions and a defined threshold for what counts as real change.

In your last remote program, what share of enrolled clients finished with a baseline and a follow-up you could actually put side by side?
