Accuracy stopped being the hardest question in digital health procurement.

For years, the first thing a buyer asked a measurement vendor was "how accurate is it?" The serious ones now ask something else. They ask what the number is allowed to decide.

That shift matters. A body scan can show 96-97% agreement with an expert and still be the wrong input for a clinical call it was never built to make.

Here's the question I'd put to any vendor. Which decisions does your output support, and which does it explicitly not support?

A tool that answers that cleanly is easier to govern. One that blurs it moves the liability onto your team.

We built FitXpress to draw that line in the open. It captures structured body data. The coach owns the interpretation. It is not a medical device.

When you last evaluated a measurement tool, did the boundary conditions come up before the accuracy figure, or after?

**CTA:** The article walks through where that line sits across a coaching program. Worth a read if you are scoping one.
