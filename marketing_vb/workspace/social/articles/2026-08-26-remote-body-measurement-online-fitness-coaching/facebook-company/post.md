Ask any online coach how they track a client's progress from another city, and you hear the same list: a home scale, a tape measure, a few phone photos in the mirror. Each one tells part of the story. None of them quite agrees with the others.

The scale moves with hydration, food, and time of day. Tape numbers shift with placement and tension. Photos change with lighting and pose. When a client's numbers wobble, it is genuinely hard to know what changed: the body, or the way it was measured.

That is the real problem remote coaching has to solve. Not "which tool is most accurate," but "which record can I actually compare, check-in after check-in."

A mobile body scan adds one more option. Two smartphone photos, front and side, return more than 80 body measurements and a 3D model a coach can line up against the last visit. Processing typically takes under 45 seconds. It does not replace a scale or a clinic-grade body scan, and it does not make the coaching call. The coach still does that. What it adds is consistency: the same capture and the same outputs every time.

If you coach remotely, we are curious: which measurement do you trust most, and which one do you quietly ignore?
