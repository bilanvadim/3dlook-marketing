Two people at the same weight can have very different body compositions. One person can lose fat while gaining muscle, with the scale showing no net change. Weight is a composite number — it combines fat, muscle, bone, water, and waste — so it does not independently show how individual body dimensions are changing.

This is the measurement gap that structured body data fills.

Mobile body scanning produces 80+ body measurements, body-composition estimates, and a 3D model from two smartphone photos in under 45 seconds. Scan-to-scan comparison tracks circumference changes by body region independently — waist, hips, chest, arms, thighs — alongside body-fat trends over multiple scans, smoothing out daily fluctuations from hydration, food intake, and time of day.

For fitness apps, this gives the progress tab a way to show body change directly rather than relying on weight alone. For digital coaching platforms, structured measurements give coaches a consistent input for program review, distinct from a client's self-reported sense of progress.

Global fitness-app revenue was estimated at $12.1 billion in 2025 and projected to reach $33.6 billion by 2033. Yet roughly seven in ten users stop using a fitness app within three months, with lack of personalization and difficulty judging progress among the leading reasons.
