Three shifts are reshaping how fitness apps approach progress tracking.

First, smartphone cameras are capable enough to capture structured body measurements from two photos. The same device millions use for workouts can generate 80+ body measurements, body composition, and a 3D model.

Second, user expectations have moved. People accustomed to personalization from other apps expect fitness products to adapt individually, not assign a one-size-fits-all plan.

Third, retention economics reward visible progress. Giving subscribers a way to see body change a scale cannot show creates recurring check-ins that support engagement.

These three shifts are connected. Together, they are pushing more fitness platforms to add a structured body data layer — not to replace coaches or devices, but to fill the measurement gap between them.
