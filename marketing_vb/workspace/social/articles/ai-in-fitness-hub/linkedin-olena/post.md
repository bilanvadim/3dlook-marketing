Every fitness platform I speak with in Europe reaches the same question: body data would transform how we track progress — but where does it live, and under which framework?

GDPR sets the standard. For fitness apps serving users across EU member states, how body data is captured, stored, and deleted is as important as what the measurements show. Users are asking what happens to their photos, who sees their data, and how long it is kept. Clear answers at the point of capture reduce uncertainty.

Mobile body scanning can fit into this framework. FitXpress processes photos to extract measurements, then deletes them immediately or within 30 days. Faces are obfuscated at capture. No names or personal identifiers are stored alongside scan data. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.

What it produces: 80+ measurements, body composition, and a 3D model from two smartphone photos in under 45 seconds. Scan-to-scan comparison tracks regional changes — waist, hips, arms, thighs — alongside body-fat trends over time.

FitXpress does not prescribe workouts or diagnose conditions. It standardises how body data enters a fitness workflow, with GDPR-aligned implementations that give users and platforms confidence.
