I see UK digital fitness platforms scaling fast — but most are building on a data gap that affects both retention and personalisation.

The global fitness app market is projected to reach $33.6 billion by 2033, and the UK is a significant part of that growth. Yet the same retention pattern shows up everywhere: roughly seven in ten users stop using a fitness app within three months. Lack of personalisation and difficulty judging progress are consistently cited as top reasons.

The issue is not tracking activity — apps do that well. It is tracking body change. Scale weight fluctuates with hydration, food intake, time of day. Two people at the same weight can have very different body compositions. A user who loses fat while gaining muscle may see no change on the scale.

Structured body data changes what is visible. Two smartphone photos produce 80+ measurements, body composition estimates, and a 3D model in under 45 seconds. Scan-to-scan comparison tracks regional changes — waist, hips, arms, thighs — that a single weight reading would report as no change.
