The incretin market, which includes GLP-1s, is projected to reach about $200 billion by 2030, and employer coverage keeps climbing. As more programs shift to remote and hybrid care, one question keeps surfacing: how do you track real progress when the patient is at home?

Scale weight alone does not answer it. Two members can lose the same amount of weight while one preserves lean mass and the other does not. The scale reports a single number and misses that difference. Body composition adds the context a care team needs.

The harder part is that no single tool covers every check-in. Connected scales, professional BIA, DXA, mobile body scanning, manual measurement, progress photos, and wearables each trade remote capability, patient burden, and cost differently. Most programs end up combining a few.

This is where a remote capture layer earns its place. FitXpress turns two smartphone photos into 80+ structured measurements and a 3D model in under 45 seconds, with scan-to-scan repeatability under 1 cm to support consistent longitudinal comparison. It covers remote intake and between-visit check-ins, while DXA, professional BIA, and calibrated scales stay in place wherever a protocol requires them.

Our latest article compares all seven tool categories by role, patient burden, and the decision each one supports, and includes a buyer checklist for evaluating any of them. 📊
