On a GLP-1 program, the number on the scale only tells part of the story. Two people can lose the same amount of weight while one keeps muscle and the other loses it, and a scale cannot tell them apart. That is why more remote clinics are looking at body composition and progress tracking, not just weight.

So which tool actually fits a remote program? There is no single right answer. We compared 7 categories clinics can use, from connected scales and professional BIA to DXA, mobile body scanning, manual measurement, progress photos, and wearables. Each one trades remote capability, patient burden, and cost differently, and many programs end up combining a few to cover both periodic and frequent check-ins.

Mobile scanning is one piece of that mix. Two photos from a phone turn into 80+ body measurements in under 45 seconds, and scan to scan the changes hold to within less than a centimetre. It does not replace a scale, a DXA appointment, or a clinician. It helps make progress visible between visits, which is often what keeps members engaged.

If you run or work with a remote GLP-1 program, how are you tracking progress beyond the scale today?
