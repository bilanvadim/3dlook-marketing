Most operators building a remote GLP-1 program ask me the same thing: which body composition tool is best?

It's the wrong question. There's no single best tool. There are seven categories, and each one balances remote capability against patient burden and cost in a different way.

For a program serving members across a spread-out country, that trade-off is real. A DXA scan gives a strong reference read, but it needs an appointment, and appointments are exactly what remote members skip. Connected scales, wearables, and two-photo mobile scanning sit at the other end: they capture in seconds, at home, with no visit.

Members stay when progress is visible between check-ins, and data you can actually collect beats reference-grade data you can't.

This is where mobile scanning earns a place in the mix. Two photos, under 45 seconds, 80+ measurements, and scan-to-scan repeatability under 1 cm, so a small real change isn't lost in measurement noise. FitXpress produces supporting body-data estimates for clinician review. It isn't a medical device, and it doesn't replace DXA or a calibrated scale where a protocol needs them.

For AU teams, the real work is the mix and the plumbing: how the data lands in your records without new manual steps, and which categories cover baseline versus frequent check-ins. Privacy and data handling belong in that evaluation early, not after procurement.

How are you thinking about your tool mix for remote check-ins? Full breakdown of the seven categories in the comments.
