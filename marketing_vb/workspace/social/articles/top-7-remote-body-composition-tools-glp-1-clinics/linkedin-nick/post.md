US GLP-1 programs are scaling fast, but the measurement question keeps coming up the same way: which remote body composition tool should we use?

It's not a single-tool decision. There are seven categories, and each trades off remote capability, patient burden, and cost differently. Connected scales, professional BIA, DXA access, mobile body scanning, manual clinic measurement, patient photos, and wearables all have a place depending on what the program is trying to capture and how often.

For US operators running virtual-first weight loss or telehealth programs, the real constraint isn't accuracy alone. It's whether members actually complete the check-in. A DXA scan gives a strong reference read but needs an appointment — and that's exactly what remote members skip. Connected scales, wearables, and two-photo mobile scanning capture in seconds, at home, with no visit.

Data you can collect consistently beats reference-grade data you can't.

Mobile scanning earns a spot in the mix because it produces 80+ measurements in under 45 seconds with scan-to-scan repeatability under 1 cm. FitXpress provides supporting body-data estimates for clinician review. It isn't a medical device and doesn't replace DXA or a calibrated scale where a protocol needs them.

For US teams, the work is the mix and the plumbing: how the data lands in the EHR without new manual steps, and which categories cover baseline versus frequent check-ins. Privacy and data handling belong in that evaluation early, not after procurement.

What does your tool mix look like for remote check-ins? Full breakdown of the seven categories in the comments.
