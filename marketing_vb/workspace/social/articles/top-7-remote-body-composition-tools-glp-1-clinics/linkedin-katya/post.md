I've been speaking with digital health operators across Israel and the Gulf, and the same question keeps coming up: which remote body composition tool should we use for our GLP-1 and weight-loss programs?

It's the wrong starting point. There's no single best tool. There are seven categories, and each one trades off remote capability, patient burden, and cost differently.

Connected scales, professional BIA, DXA access, mobile body scanning, manual clinic measurement, patient photos, and wearables — all have a role depending on what you're trying to capture and how often.

For programs scaling in this region, the practical constraint isn't just accuracy. It's whether patients actually complete the check-in. A DXA scan gives a strong reference read, but it needs an appointment, and that's exactly what remote members skip. Connected scales, wearables, and two-photo mobile scanning capture in seconds, at home, with no visit.

Data you can collect consistently beats reference-grade data you can't.

Mobile scanning earns a place in the mix because it produces 80+ measurements in under 45 seconds with scan-to-scan repeatability under 1 cm. FitXpress provides supporting body-data estimates for clinician review. It isn't a medical device and doesn't replace DXA or a calibrated scale where a protocol needs them.

The real work is the mix and the integration: how the data lands in your workflow, which categories cover baseline versus frequent check-ins, and whether the vendor handles implementation at scale.
