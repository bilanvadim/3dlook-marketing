"Who actually makes the decision?" is a fair thing to ask about any health tool that uses AI. Here is our answer for FitXpress.

It turns two smartphone photos and a short profile into structured body data: 80+ body measurements, body composition estimates, and a 3D model, in under 45 seconds. That data then flows into a workflow the customer runs.

What FitXpress does not do is just as clear. It does not diagnose, recommend treatment, or determine insurance or clinical-trial eligibility. Those high-impact decisions stay with the clinicians, underwriters, and designated decision-makers who use the outputs. FitXpress is not a medical device.

We wrote all of this into a public FAQ, with the qualifications attached, so procurement, legal, and clinical teams can read where the boundaries sit before the first call.

Where should the line fall between what software measures and what a person decides? We would like to hear how your team draws it.
