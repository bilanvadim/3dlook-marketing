Accuracy is no longer what separates one body-scanning vendor from another.

The models are converging. Within a few years, measuring a body from two photos will be ordinary.

The questions enterprise buyers ask are changing with it.

Not "how accurate are you," but "what have you decided you are not, and who made that call?"

That is harder to answer well. It means stating your limits in writing, before a deal, with the qualifications attached.

We recently published ours for FitXpress. One line I keep coming back to: an independent regulatory assessment concluded it does not meet the definition of a medical device under the UK Medical Devices Regulations. FitXpress is not a medical device.

Boundaries like that used to surface in a sales call. Buyers now expect to read them in public.

When you last evaluated a health-data vendor, what did their written limits tell you that the demo could not?
