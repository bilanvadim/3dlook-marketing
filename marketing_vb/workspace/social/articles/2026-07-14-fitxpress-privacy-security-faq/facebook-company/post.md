When an app measures your body from two photos, it is fair to ask: who is actually making the call about your care?

We just published a plain-language answer. FitXpress turns two smartphone photos into structured body data (80+ body measurements, body composition estimates and a 3D model) in under 45 seconds. That data can support telehealth intake, weight management check-ins or a research protocol. What it does not do is decide anything on its own.

FitXpress does not independently determine a diagnosis, a treatment, or whether someone qualifies for a program. It is not a medical device. The final decision stays with the people responsible for it: the clinician, the underwriter, or whoever a program has designated to make that call.

We think that is the right line to draw. Software can gather clean, consistent information. Judgement about a person's health or eligibility belongs to a trained human who can be accountable for it.

As more health tools add AI, where do you think that line should sit?
