## linkedin-nick

HIPAA is not a certificate a vendor can hand you.

It's a regulatory framework, not a certification. No auditor stamps a scanning tool as compliant, whatever a slide claims.

When you vet a body-scanning vendor for a GLP-1 or telehealth program, a certification logo answers the wrong question. Ask whether they'll sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) and stand behind it as a business associate.

That is the line that actually protects you. HIPAA obligations follow the role each party plays. The vendor covers its systems. You cover how the workflow is configured and whether protected health information is involved.

FitXpress can support HIPAA-governed deployments where 3DLOOK acts as a business associate under an executed BAA, where applicable.

We published the rest of these answers in writing, with the qualifications attached.

## linkedin-olena

In a body-scanning deployment, the vendor rarely owns the privacy risk. You do.

Under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), in most enterprise deployments the customer acts as the data controller and the technology provider acts as the data processor. As controller, you carry obligations the vendor cannot take off your hands.

Three of them stay yours: the lawful basis for processing, the privacy notices to end users, and consent where the workflow needs it. Get any one wrong and it's your organization that answers for it.

The first thing I ask any scanning vendor: will you sign a Data Processing Agreement with Standard Contractual Clauses, and does it name who does what?

FitXpress publishes that answer in writing, with the roles and the hedge left in.

If you are scoping a scanning workflow, who on your side owns the lawful basis today?
