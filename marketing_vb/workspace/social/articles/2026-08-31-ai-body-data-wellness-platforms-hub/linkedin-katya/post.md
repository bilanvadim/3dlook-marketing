Scanning members too often is how you kill a good progress feature.

Commercially, the temptation is to scan weekly. More data, more engagement, right? Usually the opposite. If you scan faster than the body actually changes, the comparison shows noise, members stop believing it, and the feature quietly dies.

The practical starting point from the article: for body-change programs, 4-12 weeks is a reasonable range, adjusted to the goal and the expected rate of change. A waist-focused goal moves on a different clock than a broad body-composition one.

So I'd set cadence to the goal, not to the dashboard. Fewer scans that each show real change beat frequent scans that show nothing.

That's what builds trust, and trust is what earns renewals in digital health.

The article walks through how to make each comparison credible.
