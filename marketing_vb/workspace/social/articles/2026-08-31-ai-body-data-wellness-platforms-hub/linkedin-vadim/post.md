Most bad progress data comes from the capture step, not the algorithm.

Here's the failure mode I'd watch for. You roll out body scanning to thousands of members, on their own phones, in their own bedrooms. Different lighting. Different clothing. Different camera height. The baseline and the follow-up get captured under conditions that don't match.

Now your comparison is measuring the room, not the body.

The fix isn't a better model. It's a guided capture flow: the same pose each time, a pose check before the shot, and a retake that takes seconds. Boring, operational, and it's what makes a two-scan comparison mean anything.

For most evaluated FitXpress measurements, repeated scans showed typical scan-to-scan differences of less than 1 cm. That only holds if the conditions hold. So I'd treat capture guidance as a feature, not an afterthought.

The article goes deeper on what makes a comparison credible.
