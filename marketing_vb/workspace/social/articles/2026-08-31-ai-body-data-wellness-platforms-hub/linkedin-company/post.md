Wellness platforms have a retention problem hiding inside a measurement problem.

When progress is reported as a single number on a scale, members can do everything right for eight weeks and still see almost no movement. The waist may have come in while chest and shoulders held steady, but a scale cannot show that. What looks like a plateau is often real change the platform never surfaced.

Body data changes the progress view. A baseline scan establishes a starting record. A follow-up turns it into a dated comparison across the same measurements, captured under the same conditions. Members see where their body actually shifted, and coaches work from the same reference.

For a wellness product, this creates a distinct engagement point around visible progress. Its value depends on clear explanations, an appropriate cadence, member control, and relevant content. The scan supplies the record. Program design determines how well that record is used.

FitXpress provides the capture layer. From two smartphone photos, it returns 80+ body measurements, body composition estimates, and a 3D body model in under 45 seconds, through an API or mobile SDK. The platform controls where scanning appears and which outputs members see.

Accuracy matters. For longitudinal tracking, repeatability is what makes a comparison trustworthy, and it is worth evaluating separately before you build a progress view on it. 📊
