## The question this pack answers

This pack is built around ONE question, not around the article. The article
of record below is only where the facts come from.

> How can GLP-1 weight-loss clinics track body composition remotely between visits, and what tools belong in the care plan?

Source: HubSpot AEO recommendation #184342641 (LINKEDIN_POST, 2026-10-17). AI assistants cite LinkedIn posts that answer a buyer's question plainly, so every post here must leave the reader with a usable answer, not only a hook.

What the answer covers (each post picks its own part):

- Why scale weight alone says little between visits: it cannot separate a change in fat mass from a change in lean mass.
- Which methods work at home between visits (connected scales, mobile body scanning from two photos, progress photos, wearables) and which need an in-person visit (professional BIA, DXA, manual measurement).
- One possible mix from the article: a method for periodic, detailed in-clinic assessment paired with a method for frequent, low-burden remote capture.
- What to ask before choosing a remote method: what it measures versus estimates, the validation reference, repeatability at home, API export, how photos and records are retained, where clinician review sits.
- How to start: a baseline at intake, captures scheduled around the check-in protocol, and a time-boxed pilot that measures capture completion rate, record consistency across sessions and clinician review time.

Rules for this pack:

- Name the subject in the first two lines in plain words (GLP-1 program, between visits, body composition) so an assistant can match the post to the question. The hook is still a claim, not a question.
- Do not lead with 'there is no single best tool' or 'the right tool mix': the article's own pack spent that angle seven times (table below).
- FitXpress is one of seven categories in the article, never 'the solution'. DXA, professional BIA and calibrated scales keep their role wherever a protocol requires them.
- US market figures (J.P. Morgan, KFF) only in posts whose audience is US or global (linkedin-company, linkedin-nick, the two soft company accounts).
- No clinical outcome claims: nothing about preventing muscle loss, improving adherence or better results. Body data supports clinician review and does not determine eligibility, prescribing or treatment.
- No customer names. The article names none.

## Claims discipline for this article

Written 2026-10-17 against the live capture. Anything not listed here is not
available to a post.

**FitXpress, in the live wording**
- Two smartphone photos, front and side; no dedicated scanning hardware.
- Outputs: 80+ body measurements; calculated BMI and BMR; estimated body fat
  percentage, lean mass and fat mass; a 3D model. Measurements are measurements,
  BMI/BMR are calculated metrics, body composition values are estimates. Never
  "80+ body metrics".
- "processing typically takes approximately 30–45 seconds" (the article's figure;
  do not substitute "45 seconds").
- Repeatability: "For most evaluated measurements, repeated scans showed typical
  scan-to-scan differences of less than 1 cm."
- Accuracy, a SEPARATE benchmark: "compared with expert manual measurements,
  FitXpress reports 96–97% accuracy with a typical error margin of 1.5–2.0 cm."
  Never in the same sentence as repeatability; say they are two benchmarks if both
  appear.
- Predicted weight: mean absolute error of approximately 3.5%, "a software
  estimate, not a physical scale reading". Only with that qualifier.
- Delivery: white-label, through an API or SDK.
- Best role per the article: "remote intake and recurring between-visit check-ins
  that produce a structured record for review".

**Boundaries, verbatim where possible**
- "FitXpress is not a medical device." It does not diagnose conditions or
  determine treatment, prescribing or eligibility.
- Programs keep calibrated scales, professional BIA, DXA or other methods wherever
  their protocol requires them.
- Home BIA readings vary with hydration, food intake, exercise and timing (the
  article's own caveat about connected scales). Progress photos are unstructured
  and vary with pose, distance, clothing, lighting and camera angle. State these as
  the article does, without mocking other tools.

**Market and clinical context (US unless stated)**
- J.P. Morgan Research: global incretin market, which includes GLP-1s, about $200
  billion by 2030; Americans on GLP-1 treatment from roughly 10 million in 2025
  toward 25 million by 2030.
- KFF 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey: 43 percent of firms with 5,000 or more
  workers covered GLP-1 drugs for weight loss in 2025, up from 28 percent in 2024.
- Mass General Brigham clinicians: lean body mass loss is a consideration during
  rapid weight reduction, it is not unique to GLP-1 therapy, and more accurate tools
  for measuring body composition are needed. Attribute it; do not turn it into a
  claim that GLP-1 patients lose muscle.

**Not usable in this pack**
- No compliance claims. The live article's lines "supports deployments compliant
  with HIPAA", "aligned with GDPR" and "Production photos are deleted after
  processing" do NOT match the trust FAQ (CLAUDE.md §12) and must not be repeated.
  "HIPAA compliant" and "SOC 2 certified" are hard fails anywhere.
- No "tracks each patient over time" (3DLOOK does not track people; the program
  compares scans).
- No "replaces DXA" or "as accurate as DXA". No customer names, scan volumes or
  client counts.
- No pricing, trial or "free" language.
