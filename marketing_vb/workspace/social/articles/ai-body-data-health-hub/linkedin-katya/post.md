Israel's health-tech ecosystem builds fast. Digital health startups here go from concept to live clinical partnerships in months, not years. But speed creates a specific vulnerability: programs launch before the data infrastructure is ready.

I see this pattern across telehealth platforms, digital pharmacy startups, and wellness-tech companies: they build sophisticated care workflows on top of self-reported body data. A member types in their weight. The platform calculates BMI and triggers clinical workflows. The entire program runs on an input with no provenance and no repeatability.

The alternative is simpler than most teams expect. Two smartphone photos. Under 45 seconds. 80+ measurements, body composition, and a 3D model — captured the same way every time, with scan-to-scan repeatability under 1 cm.

FitXpress doesn't make clinical or eligibility decisions. It standardizes intake so the clinicians and pharmacists making those decisions have structured, repeatable input instead of a single self-reported number. It supports HIPAA-compliant and GDPR-aligned implementations.

This matters across nine health verticals — from GLP-1 programs to insurance underwriting to clinical trials. We've mapped each one with real adoption numbers: roughly 34,000 scans by one weight-loss program in 2025, about 7,500 verification scans by an online pharmacy.

Israeli health-tech moves fast. The programs that get the data layer right early are going to have a structural advantage when regulators, payers, and partners start asking for audit trails.

Curious if others in the Israeli digital health space are thinking about this data-quality question.
