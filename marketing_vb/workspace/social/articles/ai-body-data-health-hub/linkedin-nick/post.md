I've been in enough conversations with US underwriting and telehealth teams to know this pattern: the program works well until you look at what's actually feeding into it.

Self-reported build and BMI are the second-largest driver of misclassification in accelerated underwriting, after smoking non-disclosure. In telehealth and GLP-1 programs, the problem is different but the mechanism is the same — a member types in a number, and the program builds clinical and operational decisions on top of it.

The fix isn't more forms or stricter wording. It's changing the input.

Two smartphone photos. Under 45 seconds. 80+ measurements, body composition, and a 3D model — captured the same way every time, with scan-to-scan repeatability under 1 cm. That's the difference between a self-reported figure with no provenance and structured body data a reviewer can actually use.

The boundary is important: FitXpress doesn't make underwriting or eligibility decisions. It doesn't detect fraud automatically. It provides supporting evidence — timestamped, repeatable, audit-ready — that helps underwriters and clinical reviewers make better calls, faster.

We're seeing real adoption numbers that back this up. One weight-loss program ran roughly 34,000 scans last year. An online pharmacy ran about 7,500 verification scans. The volume is there because the operational need is real.

Happy to share more about how this fits into US health-tech workflows.
