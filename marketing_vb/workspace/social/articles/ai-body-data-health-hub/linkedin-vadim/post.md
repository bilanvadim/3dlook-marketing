I've been working on positioning for FitXpress and kept running into the same friction: we don't have a clean category name for what we do.

"Body scanning" is the technology. "Remote measurement" is the mechanism. Neither captures what the buyer actually needs — trustworthy body data collected remotely, at scale, that a workflow can act on.

The phrase that's been landing in conversations: *verified body data.*

It draws a clear line against what health programs use today — a self-reported number in a form field. Weight typed in by a member. BMI stated on an application. No provenance, no consistency, no audit trail. That's not data a program can build decisions on.

Verified body data means something different: structured, repeatable measurements captured the same way each time. 80+ outputs from two smartphone photos in under 45 seconds. Scan-to-scan repeatability under 1 cm. Timestamped records that support reviewer decisions without making those decisions.

The category framing matters because it changes the buyer conversation from "how accurate is your scanner?" to "what decision do you need this data to support?" That second question is the one that leads to a real evaluation.

We just published a piece that walks through this across nine health verticals — telehealth, GLP-1 programs, insurance underwriting, BMI verification, wellness, bariatrics, occupational health, clinical trials, and connected fitness. Each one has the same underlying body-data problem; each one needs the same thing: structured, repeatable input before review.

Link in the comments. Would be interested to hear how others in B2B health-tech are framing this category.
