One of our UK pharmacy customers ran about 7,500 BMI verification scans in 2025. That number isn't vanity — it's a signal about where remote prescribing is heading and what regulators are going to ask for next.

When I talk to health-tech founders and clinical operations leads in the UK, the conversation around body data is shifting. A year ago, the question was "can we capture measurements remotely?" Now it's "can we trust the input enough to base a clinical or compliance decision on it?"

The difference is between a self-reported weight — one figure with no provenance — and a structured, repeatable scan that produces 80+ measurements, body composition, and a 3D model from two smartphone photos. The scan doesn't make the decision. It gives the clinician or pharmacist better input before they do.

What's driving this in the UK specifically: tightening scrutiny of remote GLP-1 prescribing, MHRA guidance evolving, and NHS digital transformation putting more workflows online. Programs that still run on self-reported BMI are going to face harder questions about auditability and defensibility.

FitXpress supports HIPAA-compliant and GDPR-aligned implementations. It doesn't diagnose, doesn't make eligibility or prescribing decisions, and doesn't replace clinician review. It's an intake and documentation layer — one that a growing number of UK health programs are building on.

Curious what others in UK health-tech are seeing on this front.
