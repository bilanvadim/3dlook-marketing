Here's something we noticed while researching how body data gets used across health programs: the same problem shows up in nine different places.

A telehealth platform asks members for their weight. An insurance application relies on self-reported build. A clinical trial site measures waist circumference with a tape measure that varies between coordinators. A wellness program accepts a number typed into an app.

Each workflow looks different on the surface. Underneath, they all share the same failure: body data that's self-reported, measured manually at inconsistent points, or captured once and never repeated.

FitXpress approaches this differently — not by adding more steps, but by standardizing how body data enters a workflow in the first place. Two smartphone photos. Under 45 seconds. 80+ measurements, body composition, and a 3D model, with scan-to-scan repeatability under 1 cm.

The result isn't an automated decision. FitXpress doesn't diagnose conditions or make treatment, underwriting, or eligibility calls. What it does is give the people who make those decisions — clinicians, underwriters, reviewers — structured, repeatable data they can actually work with.

We've seen this approach in action: one GLP-1 program ran roughly 34,000 scans in 2025. An online pharmacy ran about 7,500 verification scans. The pattern holds across telehealth, insurance, wellness, occupational health, bariatrics, and clinical trials.
