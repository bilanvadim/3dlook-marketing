Most conversations about AI in healthcare start too broadly. The more useful question for a program owner is narrower: where does the body data a program already relies on come from, and can it be trusted for the decision it feeds?

In practice, that data is self-reported, measured manually at inconsistent points in time, or captured once and never repeated. Each workflow — telehealth, GLP-1 programs, insurance underwriting, wellness rewards, clinical trials — exposes the same underlying body-data problem in a different operational context.

FitXpress exists as an operational layer between self-report and clinical or underwriting decision. It standardizes how body data enters a workflow and how it's documented. Two smartphone photos produce 80+ measurements, body composition (BMI, body fat percentage, lean and fat mass), and a 3D model in under 45 seconds. Scan-to-scan repeatability is under 1 cm — the property that makes longitudinal tracking reliable.

We're deliberate about where FitXpress ends: it does not diagnose conditions or make treatment, underwriting, eligibility, hiring, or clearance decisions. It does not replace a clinician, a DEXA scan, a calibrated scale, or a protocol-defined reference method. It supports review. It provides the structured, repeatable data that a human reviewer still depends on — with HIPAA-compliant and GDPR-aligned implementations.

We've mapped nine health verticals where verified body data changes the workflow: connected fitness, telehealth, GLP-1 and weight-loss programs, BMI verification, insurance underwriting, wellness rewards, bariatric pre-qualification, occupational health, and clinical trials. For each, we cover the operational context, the self-report failure mode, and how structured capture fits into the review process.
