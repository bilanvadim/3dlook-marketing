Every conversation I have with European health programs eventually reaches the same junction: "This data would be useful — but where does it live, and under which framework?"

Cross-border health data in the EU isn't a checkbox. It's a structural question that shapes how programs can operate. A telehealth platform serving patients in Germany and France. A clinical trial running sites across multiple member states. An occupational health provider screening a distributed workforce. Each one needs body data that's not just accurate — it needs to be captured and stored in a way that stands up to GDPR scrutiny.

FitXpress approaches this by being an intake and documentation layer, not a decisioning system. It produces structured, repeatable body measurements from two smartphone photos — 80+ measurements, body composition, and a 3D model in under 45 seconds — and supports GDPR-aligned implementations with encryption in transit and at rest, configurable retention, photo-handling controls, and deletion workflows.

Critically, it does not make clinical, eligibility, or clearance decisions. It gives the reviewer better input. The decision stays human. That boundary matters for compliance just as much as the technical safeguards.

The programs that are adopting this — across telehealth, GLP-1 weight loss, bariatric pre-qualification, and clinical trials — aren't replacing their existing compliance frameworks. They're adding structured, repeatable data to workflows that currently run on self-report.

Open to connecting with others working on the GDPR and health-data intersection in Europe.
