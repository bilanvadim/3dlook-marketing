I keep noticing the same pattern in conversations with European sponsors and CROs running multi-site obesity and metabolic trials: the protocol reads identical on paper at every site, and the execution doesn't.

A trial running across several European countries carries more variability than a single-site study. Each site team develops its own habits in how a coordinator takes a waist or hip measurement, and that gap compounds over a year-long study. A hybrid or decentralized design with remote check-ins raises the stakes further. This is the gap FitXpress closes: a guided two-photo scan producing the same structured, time-stamped record at a site visit in one country or a remote check-in in another.

There's a second layer worth naming for European trial teams. Any remote capture workflow needs to be GDPR-aligned from the start. FitXpress follows GDPR principles, deletes photos immediately or within 30 days per client policy, and does not process personal identifiers. That matters once measurement moves outside the clinic.

None of this replaces the reference methods a protocol already defines. It's the operational layer underneath: the part deciding whether data from a multi-country trial is actually comparable a year in.

Open to connect if you're weighing this for a European trial program. Article in comments.
