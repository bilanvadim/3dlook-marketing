I keep having a version of the same conversation with people building health-tech in Israel: everyone wants the flashy AI layer, and almost no one wants the unglamorous operational problem underneath it.

Clinical trials are a good example. Obesity and metabolic studies run on repeated anthropometric measurement: BMI, waist and hip circumference, body composition, captured at baseline, follow-up, and across a monitoring window that can run a year or more. The protocol looks identical on paper across every site. In practice, a coordinator at one site measures waist circumference slightly differently from a coordinator at another, and that gap compounds across a multi-site, twelve-month study. Hybrid and decentralized designs add remote check-ins on top, raising the pressure on the measurement step to hold up on its own.

I think this is the kind of infrastructure problem Israel's health-tech scene is well suited to take seriously — the ecosystem here is built for operational rigor, not just for a good demo.

FitXpress's role here is narrow: a guided two-photo scan producing structured, time-stamped records, usable at site or remotely, standardizing capture around the protocol, not replacing the reference methods it already defines.

Curious how others here see it: bigger opportunity in the trial tech itself, or in the operational layer that makes multi-site trials scalable?
