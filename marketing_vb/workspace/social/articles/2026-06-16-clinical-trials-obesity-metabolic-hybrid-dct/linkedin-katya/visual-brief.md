## Format decision
**Recommended:** single photo/illustration accompanying the text post ("text + photo") — per the post's own Design tip, not overridden.
**Why:** `linkedin-katya` is a personal profile (Katya Boychuk, BD, Israel) posting in first person about an operational observation, not a company content-hub asset — per `social-profiles-config.md`, the tone is "BD professional... direct, confident" and the content type is industry-insight/perspective, not a data infographic. A single tightly cropped image reads as authentic personal commentary rather than a marketing graphic, which matches how personal-profile posts perform on LinkedIn (a supporting visual, not a lecture-style schema like the company profile's 3-row comparison). The post's own Design tip is explicit and not contradicted: crop tight on the guided-scan smartphone element, keep the multi-site dot motif small/secondary, square or portrait.
**Dimensions:** 1080×1080 px (LinkedIn feed square) as primary. Portrait alternative: 1080×1350 px (4:5) if Katya prefers more vertical feed real estate on mobile — either works per the post's "square/portrait" instruction; default to square unless Katya has a stated preference.

## Visual concept
**Big idea (1 sentence):** One phone, mid-scan, doing the unglamorous measurement work — the "guided capture" moment, not the company-level trial-infrastructure argument.
**Mood:** direct, grounded, human-scale — this is Katya's personal observation illustrated with the one concrete detail her post anchors on (the guided two-photo scan), not a dense operational schema. Same "trial infrastructure" register as the article and sibling company posts, but personal in scale, not corporate.

## Composition
- Full-bleed dark teal-blue background (see Colors below) — flat or very subtle gradient within the brand palette, no purple-pink AI-style gradient.
- Center, dominant, tightly cropped: the guided two-photo scan-capture element from the article's OG brief — a smartphone in guided-scan capture mode showing a pose-validation silhouette or bounding-box guide. This is the single hero element; crop in close enough that the phone/silhouette guide fills most of the frame (per the post's Design tip — "tight crop," not the full scene).
- Background, small and secondary, low-opacity or pushed to a corner/edge: the multi-site dot-map motif (3-4 abstract pin/dot marks, no real map, no country outlines) — present just enough to visually tie this image back to the article banner and the company-profile posts, but clearly subordinate to the phone element.
- Drop the timestamp/structured-record icon from this crop. The post's Design tip names only two elements to carry into the personal-profile adaptation (guided-scan phone as tight-crop hero, dot motif small/secondary) — the record-icon is part of the fuller article-banner composition, not this crop. Do not add it back in; it would compete with the tight-crop framing this format calls for.
- No 3DLOOK/FitXpress wordmark badge required — this is a personal-profile post, not a company asset, and the post's Design tip doesn't call for a logo badge. If Katya/designer wants a very small, unobtrusive mark for brand consistency, keep it corner-sized and secondary to the phone element — optional, not required.
- Keep generous negative space around the phone element even at a tight crop — avoid the guide/silhouette touching the frame edges.
