I keep coming back to the same pattern across health-tech verticals: the measurement technology commoditizes, the workflow around it does not.

Obesity and GLP-1 trials are increasingly hybrid and decentralized, with remote check-ins and follow-up windows that run a year or longer. Every one depends on repeated anthropometric measurement (BMI, waist and hip circumference, body composition). Variability in that data rarely comes from a flawed measurement method. It comes from staff technique, tools, and documentation habits that differ site to site, and that is an operational problem before it is a data problem.

Frameworks like ICH E6(R3), the international Good Clinical Practice standard, already put real weight on source data, audit trails, and traceability, not on the measurement step alone. That is where I think this is heading: as scan-based capture becomes table stakes, the differentiator moves to the standardization and audit trail wrapped around it, not the accuracy number on a spec sheet. I think the UK's depth in clinical research and life sciences more broadly is exactly why UK-based CROs and research networks have reason to treat this layer as infrastructure, not an afterthought.

That is the layer we built FitXpress around: the same guided capture at every site, structured and time-stamped records, sitting alongside the protocol-defined reference methods it does not replace.

Curious how others in clinical operations see the value shifting once measurement stops being the differentiator.
