Manual anthropometric measurement is one of the more overlooked variability risks in multi-site obesity and metabolic trials.

The site, the staff member, the tool and technique used all shift the result. Even when a protocol specifies exactly how to measure waist circumference, real-world execution drifts, and that drift compounds across a study window that can run 12 months or longer.

FitXpress standardizes the capture step: a guided two-photo flow returning 80+ body measurements and body composition estimates in about 45 seconds, run the same way at every site and timepoint, whether that's a scheduled visit or a remote check-in where the protocol allows it. Every scan produces a time-stamped, structured record, the kind of source data and traceability the FDA's Digital Health Technologies guidance and ICH E6(R3) both describe.

That's also where the scope ends. FitXpress supports the recordkeeping and consistency layer around a trial's measurement workflow. Where a protocol calls for DEXA or trained-anthropometrist measurement, that reference method stays in place, and endpoint validation and eligibility determinations stay with the sponsor, CRO, and investigator team. Compliance is a programmatic outcome a study achieves through process design, not a vendor deliverable, and a structured record is one input into that, not a substitute for it.

For sponsors and CROs running hybrid or decentralized obesity and GLP-1 trials, the practical gain is fewer measurement-only site visits, less coordinator time per visit, and records that trace cleanly back to a defined capture workflow when monitoring or audit review comes around.
