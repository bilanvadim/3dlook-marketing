How much would you trust a health number that someone typed in themselves?

For online pharmacies and weight-loss programs, that question is getting harder. Many still approve eligibility from a self-reported weight and a photo pulled from the camera roll.

Population research backs this up. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) researchers found that self-reported BMI underestimated the prevalence of severe obesity by 40% compared with bias-corrected estimates, 5.3% versus 8.8% in 2020 data. That is a population-level pattern. It still shows why a typed-in number and a loose photo are thin evidence when someone's health decision depends on them.

A connected scale, a video-observed measurement, and a guided live smartphone scan each fit a different moment in the workflow. FitXpress covers the scan: two guided photos taken live in the app, no camera roll access, and processing typically takes under 45 seconds.

The judgment call is the hard part. When BMI decides eligibility or safety, how much verification is enough, and at which step?
