Every US telehealth and RPM conversation I have eventually reaches the same question: once you scale past a pilot, how do you know the body data you are capturing between visits is actually comparable over time?

Self-reported weight and measurements are the usual answer, and they are also the usual problem. The equipment changes visit to visit, the technique varies, and recall fills in what the patient does not remember exactly. A care team ends up reviewing a record that looks like data but was not captured the same way twice.

The fix most US operators land on is not a bigger form. It is standardizing the capture step itself. Two smartphone photos, a guided flow, results in under 45 seconds, with repeated scans showing typical differences of less than 1 cm.

Privacy is the second question every buyer asks, and it should be. FitXpress supports HIPAA-compliant implementations, including a Business Associate Agreement on request, with data encrypted in transit and at rest, and photos deleted after processing by default.

None of this replaces clinical judgment. FitXpress standardizes the intake step. The care team still owns the decision.
