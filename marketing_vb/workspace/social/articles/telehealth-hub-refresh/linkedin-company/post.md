Telehealth programs are scaling remote check-ins faster than most teams are standardizing what gets captured during them.

Body measurements are a clear example. A patient might use a bathroom scale one visit, a cloth tape the next, and an estimate after that. The equipment, technique, and format all shift, and the record stops comparing cleanly over time. Longitudinal programs need the opposite: the same measurement, captured the same way, so a provider reviews real change and not noise from how the number was taken.

That is the operational role structured body-data capture plays inside a telehealth workflow. It standardizes intake. It does not replace clinical judgment. FitXpress captures 80+ measurements and estimated composition from two smartphone photos in under 45 seconds, with repeated scans showing typical differences of less than 1 cm.

The more useful procurement question for any AI tool in this space is not how accurate is it. It is: accurate enough for which decision? A tool supporting progress tracking faces a different bar than one feeding a clinical determination.

Privacy is the other half of that question. FitXpress supports HIPAA-compliant implementations, including a Business Associate Agreement on request, alongside GDPR-aligned workflows, with data encrypted in transit and at rest.
