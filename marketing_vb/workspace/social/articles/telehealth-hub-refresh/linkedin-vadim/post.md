Something I keep running into with Australian telehealth and RPM operators: the check-in workflow is solid, but the body-data step underneath it is still built on whatever the patient reports.

That gap gets more expensive as a program scales. At a few hundred patients, inconsistent self-reported measurements are annoying. At a few thousand, they make it hard to tell whether a change over time is real or just a different scale, a different tape, a different visit.

What I like about structured capture is that it is boring in the right way. Two smartphone photos, a guided flow, results back in under 45 seconds, and the same measurement method every time. That consistency is what makes a scan-to-scan comparison useful for a care team instead of noise.

The implementation questions I get asked most from AU operators are not about the model. They are about deployment: does it drop into an existing intake flow, what happens to the photos afterward, and can procurement sign off on the privacy posture. FitXpress is built as a capture layer for exactly that review, not a decision-maker.

If you are running a remote program at scale in Australia, I would like to hear how you are handling the body-data step today. I go into more detail in the full piece.
