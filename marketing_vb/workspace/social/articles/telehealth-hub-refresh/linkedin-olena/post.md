A question I hear constantly from European telehealth and digital-health teams: once remote check-ins scale across several countries, how do you keep body-data capture consistent and still meet a GDPR-aligned standard everywhere you operate?

Those are usually treated as two separate problems. They are actually one. Self-reported weight and measurements vary by equipment, technique, and recall, which already makes longitudinal comparison difficult. Add different consent and retention expectations across markets, and the intake step becomes the hardest part of the workflow to standardize.

Structured mobile capture addresses both at once. Two smartphone photos, a guided flow, results in under 45 seconds, with the same measurement method applied everywhere. On the privacy side, FitXpress supports GDPR-aligned workflows, does not require names or direct personal identifiers to process a scan, and deletes photos after processing by default. Data stays encrypted in transit and at rest.

None of this replaces clinical judgment. The care team still reviews and decides. What changes is the consistency and the governance of the record they are reviewing.

For teams running remote programs across several European markets: how are you handling consent and data minimization today? I cover the workflow and governance side in the full piece.
