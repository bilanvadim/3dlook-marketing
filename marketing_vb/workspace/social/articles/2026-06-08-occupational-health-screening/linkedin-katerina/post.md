I keep hearing the same thing from occupational health providers in the UK: they're not short on clinicians. They're short on intake capacity.

Pre-employment and return-to-work screening still depends on someone standing in a clinic with a tape measure and a paper form. The medical examination isn't the bottleneck — it's everything that happens before it.

That's a workflow problem, not a clinical one. And it's the kind of problem that gets interesting when you stop asking "how do we add more clinic slots" and start asking "what if the intake data arrived before the appointment?"

Our team just published a deep dive on this — how digital intake supports occupational health screening without crossing into clinical decision-making. The boundary matters: capture the data remotely, structure it, timestamp it, but leave the clearance determination exactly where it belongs — with the licensed clinician.

For UK operators, this sits at an interesting intersection. MHRA and CQC frameworks set clear expectations around what constitutes a medical device and what doesn't. A remote intake layer that provides structured body measurements before the appointment, without making fitness-for-duty calls, occupies a defined space — one that procurement teams can evaluate without regulatory ambiguity.

The real question isn't whether body scanning belongs in occupational health. It's whether the intake workflow feeding your screening programme is built for the volume you're planning to handle.
