Working in Israel's health-tech ecosystem, I see the same pattern across occupational health: clinics buried in intake paperwork while the medical exam takes 15 minutes.

The bottleneck isn't clinical. It's manual data collection — tape measurements that vary between nurses, forms that arrive incomplete, candidates called back because a field was missed.

Our team just published a piece on digital intake for occupational health screening. The core argument: capture body measurements remotely before the appointment — structure them, timestamp them — but leave the clearance decision exactly where it belongs, with the clinician.

The workflow: two smartphone photos, no app install, 80+ body measurements and BMI in ~45 seconds. The record arrives before the candidate walks through the door.

What I find compelling from a BD perspective: this isn't about replacing clinicians. It's about stripping out repetitive intake steps so clinical teams focus on review — the work only they can do.

In Israel, where employer screening programs span multiple sites and contractors, consistent intake data is an operational multiplier: fewer rescreens, standardized documentation, higher throughput without adding capacity.
