One pattern I keep hearing from occupational health providers across Europe: the intake workflow, not the medical examination, is the throughput bottleneck.

Pre-employment and return-to-work screening still runs on paper forms and manual measurements. When intake data is inconsistent across sites, candidates get called back for a second appointment. For multi-site employers operating across borders, the documentation friction compounds.

What makes this a European conversation is the regulatory layer. Employee health data under GDPR demands a clear legal basis, purpose limitation, and audit trail for every intake record. National frameworks and EU workplace safety directives aren't getting lighter on documentation.

I've been exploring where digital intake fits: remote body measurement capture before the appointment, with structured, time-stamped records that support clinician review without replacing it. Compliance posture — data residency, access controls, retention — matters as much as measurement accuracy.

The team published a guide on standardizing occupational health screening intake with FitXpress, covering pre-employment, return-to-work, and fit-for-duty workflows with clear compliance boundaries. If you work in European occupational health or workforce screening, I'd be curious how your intake workflows hold up at scale.
