Occupational health screening still runs on manual intake. Paper forms. Tape measurements that vary by site. When intake data is missing or inconsistent, the candidate is called back for a second appointment.

The bottleneck in high-volume screening is not the medical examination. It is the unstructured intake step that feeds it.

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics recorded 2.5 million nonfatal workplace injuries and illnesses in 2024. Many generate return-to-work documentation. When each intake step depends on a separate clinic visit, the throughput cost compounds.

FitXpress shifts intake before the appointment. Two smartphone photos, captured remotely, produce 80+ body measurements and BMI inputs in roughly 45 seconds. The time-stamped record arrives ahead of the visit, so the appointment opens with intake data already attached.

What improves:

Fewer rescreens. Structured intake reduces missing and inconsistent measurement data — the primary driver of repeat appointments.

Standardized documentation. One guided workflow across all sites produces comparable, audit-ready records.

Higher throughput. Removing manual measurement from the appointment lets the same team process more candidates per day.

FitXpress operates as an intake and documentation layer. It does not perform medical examinations, make fitness-for-duty determinations, or replace clinician review. Clearance stays with the licensed provider.

For occupational health providers, workforce screening vendors, and multi-site employers, the question is not whether body data can be captured remotely. It is whether consistent intake reduces the rescreens that manual workflows create at scale.
