Just published our first FitXpress article in the occupational health vertical — digital intake for pre-employment and return-to-work screening. Hardest content piece I've positioned this year.

In occupational health, what you DON'T say matters more than what you do. The space sits under EEOC guidance, OSHA recordkeeping, and ACOEM standards. Every sentence about body measurements and employment screening had to survive the "what could this be misread as implying?" test.

The positioning anchor: FitXpress is an intake and documentation layer. It supports clinician review — it doesn't make fitness-for-duty determinations. Body measurements are intake data points, not hiring criteria. That took four rounds of refinement. The disclaimer alone went through three iterations.

Why does this vertical matter? BLS reports 2.5 million nonfatal workplace injuries in 2024. Each can trigger return-to-work workflows that still run on paper forms and manual tape measurements. The bottleneck is intake, not clinical capacity.

One experiment worth noting: this is our first article with inline citations to government and professional-body sources (BLS, EEOC, OSHA, ACOEM). We usually keep articles citation-clean. This vertical demanded it — the buyers read with a diligence lens.

Question for B2B marketers: how do you handle positioning when your product lives inside a regulated framework where clarifying what you explicitly don't do is half the job?
