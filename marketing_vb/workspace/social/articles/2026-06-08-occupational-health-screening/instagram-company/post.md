Returning to work shouldn't depend on a tape measure.

In most occupational health programs, a worker shows up for clearance and a medical assistant takes measurements by hand. If a number is off or missing — rescreen. Second appointment.

Scale that across thousands of cases and you see the real cost. It's not clinical. It's operational.

2.5 million workplace injuries were reported in the US last year. Every one can trigger a clearance workflow that still depends on manual intake.

The step that doesn't need to be manual? Body measurement capture.

Two smartphone photos. 45 seconds. Structured body data arrives before the appointment — not during it. The clinic team opens the visit with records already attached.

Fewer rescreens. Faster return-to-work. Consistent documentation across sites.

FitXpress doesn't replace the clinician. It removes the repetitive intake so clinical teams can focus on what they're trained to do: the review.

### Design tip

**Visual direction:** Split-frame or before/after composition. Left side: a clean smartphone screen showing the guided scan capture interface (pose outline overlay, no face visible — just the body silhouette with measurement zones marked). Right side: a structured intake dashboard with time-stamped records and measurement fields populated, conveying "data ready for review." No safety vests, no construction sites, no patient faces — keep it about the workflow shift, not the workplace.

**Text overlay:** Keep it minimal. One line, bottom-third: "Intake before the appointment. Clearance at the visit." Or use the hook line: "Returning to work shouldn't depend on a tape measure." Match the brand's Satoshi typeface in electric blue (#143DFF) on a dark overlay gradient.

**Mood:** Clean, calm, professional. Not urgent or alarming. The story is about removing friction — the visual should feel like relief, not drama.

**Brand badge:** FitXpress wordmark bottom-right corner if space allows; otherwise, 3DLOOK logo mark only. Consistent with sibling content-hub banners.
