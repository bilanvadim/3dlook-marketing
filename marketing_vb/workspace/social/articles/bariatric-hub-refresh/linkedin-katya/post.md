Accuracy specs rarely close a digital health deal. A pilot does.

I've watched obesity-care programs nod at an internal 96-97% accuracy figure and still stall. The specs aren't the blocker. The open question is whether a new body-data record creates rework instead of removing it.

That's why the pilot matters more than the demo. And most pilots I see skip the two decisions that make the result readable.

First: set your baseline and evaluation period before launch, not after. Second: separate what the capture produces from what your review team decides. A flagged BMI discrepancy is a signal for human review, never an automated eligibility conclusion.

Get those two right and a FitXpress pilot tells you something real. Get them wrong and you're comparing a demo to a feeling.

If you ran a scan pilot next quarter, which single number would tell you it worked?
