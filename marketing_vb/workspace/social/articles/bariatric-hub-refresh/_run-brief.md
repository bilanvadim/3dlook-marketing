## Claims discipline for this article

Numbers and boundaries a post may state, in the article's live wording only.
Anything not listed here is not available to a post. This is a bariatric / obesity-care
piece: eligibility, payer and clinical language is boundary-sensitive. `brand-assets/
product-info/compliance.md` carries the say / never-say table; when a post touches
privacy or regulatory status beyond the lines below, link the trust FAQ instead of
restating it.

**Product and capture**
- Guided front and side photos on the patient's smartphone; no specialized hardware;
  results typically return **in under 45 seconds**.
- Outputs: a 3D model; 80+ body measurements; predicted weight from Smart Scales;
  calculated BMI and basal metabolic rate (BMR); body composition estimates (body fat
  percentage, lean mass, fat mass).
- Sessions use live capture, real-time pose validation and clothing detection; liveness
  prompts add capture-integrity signals **when enabled**.
- The BMI comparison mechanic, exactly as the article has it: BMI from self-reported
  height and weight vs BMI from the same height and Smart Scales' predicted weight. A
  difference above the **configured threshold is routed for human review**. The software
  flags the discrepancy; the program decides (retake, in-clinic measurement, or review).
  Never write that the flag is an eligibility conclusion.

**Accuracy (internal validation; always say internal)**
- Approximately 96-97% across the evaluated body measurements vs expert pattern-maker
  manual measurements; typical absolute error 1.5-2.0 cm per measurement, varying by
  body part.
- Scan-to-scan differences of less than 1 cm for most evaluated measurements.
- Smart Scales predicted weight: average prediction error of approximately 3.5% relative
  to scale weight. It is **an average across evaluated captures, not the maximum error
  for an individual result** — keep that qualifier if the number is used.
- Validation population: ages 16-78, heights 150-220 cm, weights 38-210 kg, US and
  Europe. Performance outside this scope has not been characterized.
- Do NOT combine the accuracy benchmark and the repeatability figure into one claim.

**External numbers — only with their named source, as the article cites them**
- CDC (NHANES Aug 2021-Aug 2023): 40.3% of US adults had obesity (BMI 30+), 9.4% severe
  obesity (BMI 40+).
- ASMBS: less than 1% of eligible patients undergo bariatric surgery in a given year.
- 2026 narrative review: preoperative attrition reported as high as 60%, other cohorts
  differ substantially.
- CDC (Preventing Chronic Disease): self-reported BMI underestimated severe-obesity
  prevalence by 40% (5.3% self-reported vs 8.8% bias-corrected, 2020 data). This is a
  population-level result, not a claim about any individual patient.
- JAMA Surgery (May 13, 2026; 11.7M insured adults): 2022-2024, bariatric surgery use
  fell 34.1% while GLP-1 receptor agonist use rose 140.4%.
- ASMBS release (May 5, 2026): conference study of 6,700+ patients who used GLP-1 before
  surgery lost approximately 8% of total body weight before the procedure.
- Johns Hopkins on a JAMA Surgery analysis: roughly one in seven bariatric surgery
  patients used new weight-loss drugs after surgery.
- CMS-0057-F, from January 1, 2026: standard non-drug prior-auth decisions within 7
  calendar days, expedited within 72 hours, extensions up to 14 additional days; covers
  Medicare Advantage and specified Medicaid/CHIP payers; does NOT cover Medicare
  fee-for-service, and did not change QHP requirements on Federally Facilitated
  Exchanges. Do not shorten this into "payers must now decide in 7 days".

**Boundaries (non-negotiable, article wording)**
- FitXpress does not diagnose conditions, make clinical decisions, or determine
  treatment eligibility. Prior-authorization decisions remain with payers.
- It does not guarantee regulatory compliance, payer acceptance, or approval.
- It does not replace DXA, bioelectrical impedance analysis, or a calibrated scale where
  those methods are required. **"FitXpress is not a medical device."**
- Remote capture supplies inputs, not conclusions; clinical teams and payers retain
  responsibility for their decisions.
- "Pre-qualification" here = intake review of preliminary alignment with program
  criteria and payer requirements BEFORE full clinical evaluation — not an approval.

**Privacy (this article's own lines; link the trust FAQ for anything more)**
- 3DLOOK signs BAAs with customers covered by HIPAA. Never "HIPAA compliant".
- "In most enterprise deployments, the customer acts as controller and 3DLOOK acts as
  processor under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)."
- Encrypted at rest in Amazon S3 and in transit using TLS.
- Photos deleted immediately after processing or within 30 days, per the customer's
  policy.

**Gate behaviour learned on the fitxpress pack (2026-09-20).** The linter is
negation-aware but not attribution-aware: naming a banned compliance string as something
other vendors claim still hard-fails, quotation marks included. A question in quotation
marks is not read as a question anywhere in a line: avoid `makes the decision` entirely —
write "who signs off", "who decides", "where the call sits".
