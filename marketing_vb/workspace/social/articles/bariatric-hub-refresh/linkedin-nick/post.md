The weakest part of post-op progress tracking is usually the baseline.

Where the record starts decides how much of the patient's story you can actually see. If the first structured body-data capture happens at the surgical consultation, the pre-treatment window is already gone.

That window matters more now. The American Society for Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery reported a study of more than 6,700 patients. On GLP-1 medications, they lost about 8% of total body weight before surgery. The consultation-day number understates where the patient really began.

What I'd do: start the structured record at intake, then run the same guided capture after surgery. FitXpress returns the same field structure at both points, which keeps serial scans aligned with medication history. Johns Hopkins reported roughly one in seven bariatric patients use new weight-loss drugs post-op.

If your baseline starts at the consultation, how much of the pre-surgery change are you not measuring?
