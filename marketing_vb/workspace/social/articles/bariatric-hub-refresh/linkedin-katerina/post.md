A body measurement is only as good as its source.

For years, obesity care has run on self-reported height and weight. Researchers at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) found that self-reported BMI underestimated the population prevalence of severe obesity by 40%. That is not a story about patients being dishonest. It is a story about numbers with no provenance.

The shift I'm watching is quiet but real. Buyers no longer ask only for a measurement. They ask where it came from, when it was captured, and how a discrepancy gets reviewed.

That changes what intake tooling has to do. A structured scan can attach a timestamp and a recorded source to each value, then route anything above a threshold for human review. The clinical decision still sits with the care team.

Worth asking your own team: when they open a patient record today, what share of the values carry a source and a date?
