Here is a quiet bottleneck in bariatric care: the first usable body-data record often does not exist until the patient is already in the consultation. That is late. Staff may need extra outreach before they can finish pre-qualification or start a prior-authorization packet, and the appointment gets spent on measuring instead of on the patient.

Mobile body scanning moves that step earlier. With FitXpress, a patient completes guided front and side photos on their own smartphone before the visit, and the program gets a dated, consistently formatted body-data record back in under 45 seconds. No specialized hardware, no measurement-only appointment.

One thing worth saying plainly: this supplies inputs, not conclusions. Clinical eligibility stays with the care team, and each payer decides whether the documentation meets its requirements. FitXpress is not a medical device.

For teams running bariatric intake, where does the delay actually sit for you right now: getting the record, or getting it in a usable form?
