## linkedin-company

A new prior-authorization clock is running for US bariatric programs. 🗓️

Under CMS-0057-F, from January 1, 2026, Medicare Advantage organizations and specified Medicaid and Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) payers must issue standard non-drug decisions within 7 calendar days and expedited decisions within 72 hours, subject to applicable extensions. The rule does not cover Medicare fee-for-service, and it did not change the requirements for Qualified Health Plans on Federally Facilitated Exchanges.

Faster decisions on the payer side raise the value of a clean submission. The practical question for intake teams is whether the first prior-authorization packet holds a complete, traceable body-data record, or whether it comes back for corrections.

That is harder than it sounds when the first usable body-data record often appears only at the consultation. Body metrics vary in format and traceability, and self-reported figures do not always hold up.

This is where a structured capture earns its place. With FitXpress, a guided two-photo scan completed before the consultation returns a dated record: 80+ body measurements, a BMI comparison, and capture-quality flags, with the source of each value recorded.

It does not shorten a payer's deadline or assure acceptance, and FitXpress is not a medical device. Clinical eligibility stays with the care team, and each payer decides whether documentation meets its requirements.

The value here is operational: a more complete first packet, and fewer round-trips.

Governance stalls remote-scan pilots in obesity care more often than accuracy does.

The technology returns a body-data record in seconds. But the harder question comes first: who owns that record, and how long does it live?

Bariatric programs handle sensitive data across multiple sites. Before any pilot, settle the roles. In most enterprise deployments, the customer acts as controller and 3DLOOK acts as processor under the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).

Then settle deletion. With FitXpress, photos are deleted immediately after processing or within 30 days, depending on your policy. Measurements and the structured record are what the review team keeps.

My rule of thumb: write the data-governance answer into the pilot plan before the first scan. Retention, access control, and photo deletion are questions you answer once, up front.

## linkedin-vadim

Remote body capture looks simple until you run it on a bariatric population.

Mobility, standing position, available space and smartphone access all affect whether someone finishes a scan. In a general wellness app you rarely notice this. In an obesity-care program you will.

The number I'd watch in a pilot isn't accuracy. It's completion rate, split two ways: patients who finish alone, and patients who need staff or caregiver help.

That split tells you how much support the workflow really needs before you scale it. It also tells you when to route someone to an in-clinic measurement instead.

Build the fallback path before you send the first scan link. Not after the first patient can't stand long enough for the capture.

FitXpress handles retake logic and quality flags. The operational design around them is still yours.
