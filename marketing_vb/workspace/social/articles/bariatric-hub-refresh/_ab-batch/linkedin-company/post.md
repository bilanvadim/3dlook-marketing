Obesity care has a sequencing problem, and it rarely gets named.

The most recent clinical measurement cycle from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), conducted from August 2021 to August 2023, found that 40.3% of US adults had obesity and 9.4% had severe obesity. The American Society for Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery states that less than 1% of eligible patients undergo surgery in a given year. A 2026 narrative review identified preoperative attrition reported as high as 60%, while other cohorts in that literature reported substantially different rates.

Earlier body data does not resolve those gaps. What it changes is where a program can measure.

Bariatric programs need body metrics at two points: before clinical review begins, and during follow-up after treatment. When the first usable record appears only at the consultation, staff may need additional outreach before they can complete pre-qualification or prepare a prior-authorization packet.

A guided two-photo scan completed before that appointment returns a structured record: a capture timestamp, selected body measurements, two BMI values for comparison, and capture-quality flags. FitXpress supplies those inputs. Clinical eligibility remains with the care team, and each payer decides whether submitted documentation meets its requirements.

The business value sits in what a program can then measure: intake completion, time to a completed record, prior-authorization rework, measurement-only appointments, and follow-up completion.
