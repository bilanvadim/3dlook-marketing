Bariatric care does not end at the procedure, and neither does the measuring. A scan taken before surgery can become the baseline for every scan that follows, as long as the later ones use the same guided sequence and return the same fields.

That matters more than it used to. Johns Hopkins researchers, reporting on a JAMA Surgery analysis, found that roughly one in seven bariatric surgery patients used new weight-loss drugs after surgery. Follow-up is rarely one straight line any more.

Weight, circumference measurements and body composition estimates each describe a different part of postoperative change. When the care team deems them appropriate, these values can be reviewed alongside medication history.

The record itself stays modest. It is a dated series of selected metrics. The program sets the cadence and decides which fields to keep.

Here is the question we keep coming back to. If a patient's body-data record started at the first intake instead of the surgical consultation, how much more of their trajectory would your team see?
