A single BMI reading is becoming weaker evidence every year.

Here is why. In May, the American Society for Metabolic and Bariatric Surgery reported a conference study of more than 6,700 patients. All had used glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) medications before bariatric surgery. On average, these patients lost approximately 8% of total body weight before the procedure.

A patient can therefore arrive at intake with a current BMI below a value documented months earlier. Both numbers are real. Only one of them carries a date and a recorded source.

That is the shift I keep watching. Enterprise health programs are learning to ask for provenance as well as precision.

My own rule of thumb: a body-data field that cannot say when it was captured and by what method is not documentation yet.

The full piece on bariatric intake and progress records is in the comments.

How many of the body-data fields in your intake record carry both a capture date and a source today?
