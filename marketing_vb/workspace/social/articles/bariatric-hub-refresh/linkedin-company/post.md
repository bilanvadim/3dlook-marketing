A new prior-authorization clock is running for US bariatric programs. 🗓️

Under CMS-0057-F, from January 1, 2026, Medicare Advantage organizations and specified Medicaid and Children's Health Insurance Program (CHIP) payers must issue standard non-drug decisions within 7 calendar days and expedited decisions within 72 hours, subject to applicable extensions. The rule does not cover Medicare fee-for-service, and it did not change the requirements for Qualified Health Plans on Federally Facilitated Exchanges.

Faster decisions on the payer side raise the value of a clean submission. The practical question for intake teams is whether the first prior-authorization packet holds a complete, traceable body-data record, or whether it comes back for corrections.

That is harder than it sounds when the first usable body-data record often appears only at the consultation. Body metrics vary in format and traceability, and self-reported figures do not always hold up.

This is where a structured capture earns its place. With FitXpress, a guided two-photo scan completed before the consultation returns a dated record: 80+ body measurements, a BMI comparison, and capture-quality flags, with the source of each value recorded.

It does not shorten a payer's deadline or assure acceptance, and FitXpress is not a medical device. Clinical eligibility stays with the care team, and each payer decides whether documentation meets its requirements.

The value here is operational: a more complete first packet, and fewer round-trips.
