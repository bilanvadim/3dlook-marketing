## Claims discipline for this article

Written 2026-11-06 against the live capture (`published-live-2026-11-06.md`,
published 2026-10-29). Anything not listed here is not available to a post.

**Who the article is for.** Fitness and coaching apps (connected and digital
fitness), including apps run by GLP-1 programs. The member is on GLP-1 treatment
and may also follow a strength program in the app. The app team owns the progress
view: what it displays, how each value is labeled, which conclusions it avoids.

**The core idea, in the live wording**
- A logged weight loss "shows the change in weight. It does not show whether fat
  mass, lean mass, or fluid made up the change."
- "A weight chart alone leaves that training record disconnected from
  body-change data." The app can add circumference measurements and composition
  estimates to the training record at each check-in.
- Progress view rows (the article's table): weight trend (member-entered or synced
  scale reading), waist and other circumferences (guided scans, subject to capture
  variation), fat and lean mass estimates ("lean mass is not muscle mass"),
  completed strength sessions (fitness app). Each value carries its source label;
  results from different methods stay separate trends.
- When trends disagree (weight falls, waist stays the same): review capture
  conditions and earlier readings first; never assign a cause from one scan,
  including "the training program caused this".
- Comparable check-ins have five parts: baseline, cadence, capture conditions,
  review of differences, an alternative path.
- Pilot metrics the article names: capture completion, retakes, share of members
  with a valid baseline and follow-up scan, scheduled check-in completion, use of
  the progress view; plus member and coach feedback before wider rollout.

**FitXpress, in the live wording**
- Guided front-and-side photo capture embedded through a web or mobile SDK; an API
  submits scans and retrieves results; the app shows selected outputs in its own
  progress view.
- "processes the captured photos into structured results in under 45 seconds"
  (use this phrasing, not "45 seconds" bare).
- Outputs: 80+ body measurements, body composition estimates (body fat percentage,
  fat mass, lean mass), a 3D model. BMI and BMR are calculated metrics. Never
  "80+ body metrics".
- Repeatability: "For most evaluated measurements, repeated scans showed typical
  scan-to-scan differences of less than 1 cm." (internal testing, real-world
  customer dataset, five repeated scans per participant).
- Accuracy is a SEPARATE benchmark: approximately 96-97% against expert
  pattern-maker tape measurements, "with a typical absolute error of 1.5-2.0 cm
  depending on the body part". Never in the same sentence as repeatability.
- Both figures "describe body measurements. They are not validated for accuracy in
  measuring lean mass, detecting muscle loss, or detecting the smallest meaningful
  change during GLP-1 treatment." Any post quoting a figure keeps this boundary.

**Research context (attribute every time)**
- Neeland, Linge and Birkenfeld, 2024 review in *Diabetes, Obesity and
  Metabolism*: in some studies, reductions in lean mass accounted for 40% to 60%
  of total weight loss; other studies put the share at approximately 15% or less.
  Quote BOTH ranges or neither. Lean mass is not muscle: the review's lean mass
  covers organs, bone, fluids and water in fat tissue, and changes in skeletal
  muscle volume looked broadly consistent with what age, disease status and weight
  loss predict. Never write "GLP-1 users lose up to 60% muscle" or any variant.
- 2025 joint advisory (American College of Lifestyle Medicine, American Society
  for Nutrition, Obesity Medicine Association, The Obesity Society): names muscle
  and bone loss among the challenges of GLP-1 therapy; supports strength training,
  alongside adequate protein intake, as part of broader nutritional and medical
  management. "An app that offers workouts can support one part of that guidance."

**Boundaries, verbatim where possible**
- "FitXpress is not a medical device and does not evaluate medication, dosing, or
  side effects. Treatment decisions remain with the prescribing clinician."
- "A mobile body scan cannot determine whether a member has lost muscle or
  establish the cause of a change in lean mass."
- "FitXpress is not equivalent to DXA, BIA, or a calibrated scale when the
  workflow, protocol, or regulatory standard requires those methods."
- "3DLOOK does not track individuals or determine whether two scans belong to the
  same person." The app links scan IDs to its own member records and picks which
  two scans to compare.
- The app gives no medication or clinical advice; a clinical concern goes to the
  prescribing clinician.
- FitXpress "was not specifically trained on data representing people with
  physical disabilities"; the app offers manual measurements or a weight-only view.

**Not usable in this pack**
- The 34,000-scan weight-loss platform example: the editors cut it from the live
  page. No customer names, scan volumes or client counts.
- No adherence or retention claims: the live article says that effect "requires
  separate evaluation".
- No compliance claims. The article only links to the privacy FAQ on lawful basis,
  notice and consent; do not add HIPAA, GDPR or SOC 2 lines. "HIPAA compliant" and
  "SOC 2 certified" are hard fails anywhere.
- No "detects muscle loss", "measures muscle", "protects muscle", "replaces DXA" or
  "as accurate as DXA".
- The numbers inside the article images (148.2 lb, 13.4 lb, 1.8 lb, 33.1 in and
  the rest) are labeled "Illustrative example". They are not data and never go
  into post text.
- No pricing, trial or "free" language.
