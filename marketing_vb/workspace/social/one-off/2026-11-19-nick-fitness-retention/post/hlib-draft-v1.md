Most fitness apps spend heavily on acquiring users, then measure their progress with a single number from a bathroom scale.

That’s a retention blind spot.

Someone can lose inches around the waist and shift body composition over months of training while the scale barely moves. When members don’t see that progress clearly, motivation drops fast.

Retention benchmarks show how steep the early drop-off is: active usage in health and fitness apps drops from 24% on day one to 7% by day 30, and around 70% of users quit lifestyle apps within 100 days. Losing users early means eating acquisition costs without getting the return.

Putting body progress tracking directly inside the app changes how check-ins feel.

With FitXpress, members take two guided smartphone photos and get 80+ measurements, body composition estimates, and 3D progress tracking in under 45 seconds - without needing a club visit or extra hardware.

FitXpress isn’t a medical device and doesn’t replace coaching. It gives members a clearer view of real changes between check-ins - and gives fitness teams structured data they can build around, whether for coaching reviews or premium features.

Happy to share how teams test this in a cohort pilot before rolling it out broadly.
