A flat line on the scale costs fitness apps members.

Someone trains for three months. Their waist drops an inch. Body composition shifts. The scale barely moves.

If weight is the only progress they see, it looks like nothing worked. That's often when people stop opening the app.

The drop-off is already steep. In health and fitness apps, 24% of users are active on day one. By day 30 it's 7%.

So the check-in screen matters more than most roadmaps admit. I'd show members the changes a scale can't: circumferences, body composition, a 3D view next to their last scan.

That's what we build with FitXpress. Two guided phone photos, 80+ measurements and body composition estimates in under 45 seconds. No club visit, no extra hardware. It isn't a medical device and doesn't replace a coach.

What share of your members come back for a second progress check-in?
