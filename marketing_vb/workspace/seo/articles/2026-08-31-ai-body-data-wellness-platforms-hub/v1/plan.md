## Recommended Title (Phase 2)

**H1:** AI Body Data for Wellness Platforms: Progress Tracking, Personalization, and Engagement

**Why:** it is the approved hub name from the content plan, word-for-word, and a hub's H1 should
match the architecture label the whole cluster links to. Optimizing this H1 around the head term
buys little: the exact-match phrase has no demand either way, and a hub earns its position from
cluster links, coverage, and citations rather than from an exact-match H1. `corporate wellness
platform` is placed in H2.7 and in the meta description instead, where it can carry the query
without bending the hub's name toward the employer side — which is precisely the drift the
strategy row warns against.

### Other options

1. **AI Body Data for Corporate Wellness Platforms: Progress Tracking, Personalization, and
   Engagement** — the SEO-maximizing variant; puts the 500/mo head term in the H1. Not chosen
   because "Corporate" narrows the hub toward employers, which is the sub-hub's territory, and
   the wellness-app gap is the exact reason this page exists. *Pick this one if Vadim wants the
   head term in the H1 and accepts the employer tilt.*
2. **How Wellness Platforms Use AI Body Data for Personalization and Engagement** — reads well and
   is answer-engine friendly, but "How…" framing sounds like a supporting article, not a hub.
3. **Body Data for Wellness Platforms: A Guide to Progress Tracking, Personalization, and
   Rewards** — "A Guide to" mirrors the Main Health hub's published H1 pattern; drops "AI," which
   costs the `ai body` (400/mo, KD 8) association.
4. **Wellness Platforms and Body Data: Making Member Progress Visible** — sharpest single-benefit
   framing, but too narrow for a page that must also cover personalization, coaching, rewards,
   and integration.

## Article Outline (Phase 3)

Structure follows §12 (12-part strategic structure). Wellness is **not** on the sensitive-vertical
list, so no heavy clinical scope note — but H2.8 carries an explicit "what this does not do"
section, and the soft-claims boundary is restated per-section below where a writer could drift.

### H2.1 — Wellness platforms lose members before the program works
- **Goal:** reader recognizes their own retention curve in the first 200 words.
- **Word count target:** 300-400
- **Must-cover:**
  - Engagement plateaus after roughly the first 90 days; churn arrives before results do.
  - Self-reported check-ins and weight are the default progress signal, and both are weak.
  - Personalization in most wellness products stops at an onboarding survey and a stated goal.
  - Programs are asked to prove outcomes to employers, insurers, or their own board.
- **Keywords to weave:** wellness platform, wellness app, engagement
- **Sources:** `use-cases/fx-digital-fitness.md` (pain + KPIs); `audience.md` layers 3 and 4
- **Approved claims:** none needed — this is the buyer's problem, not our proof
- **Boundary:** describe *program* dropout, never *health* risk. No "members at risk."

### H2.2 — What "body data" means for a wellness platform
- **Goal:** short-answer/definition block, extractable by answer engines.
- **Word count target:** 300-400
- **Must-cover:**
  - Definition: structured body measurements plus body composition, captured remotely and
    repeatably, timestamped so two check-ins are comparable.
  - What is actually produced: 80+ measurements, BMI, BMR, fat %, lean mass, fat mass, plus a 3D
    model (FX-008, FX-009).
  - How it is captured: two photos, front and side, results in under 45 seconds (FX-006, FX-007).
  - Explicit contrast: this is a *data input to a program*, not an assessment of a person.
  - Why weight and BMI alone under-describe change → 2-3 sentences + link to Beyond BMI.
- **Keywords to weave:** ai body data, body composition tracking, body composition tracking app
- **Sources:** `proof-points.md`; `use-cases/fx-digital-fitness.md`; `/content-hub/beyond-bmi-business/`
- **Approved claims:** FX-006, FX-007, FX-008, FX-009
- **Boundary:** "body composition" is a set of outputs, never a health verdict. No "body fat is a
  risk marker for…" — that is clinical framing.

### H2.3 — Why this matters now for wellness platforms
- **Goal:** the "why now," grounded in market conditions rather than trend language.
- **Word count target:** 250-350
- **Must-cover:**
  - Hybrid and distributed workforces made onsite measurement impractical; remote capture is the
    only version that scales.
  - Wellness budgets are under pressure to show outcomes, and self-report does not survive that.
  - Smartphone capture removed the hardware barrier that used to keep body data in a facility.
  - Members now expect visual, app-native progress, not a number in a form.
- **Keywords to weave:** corporate wellness, workplace wellness app
- **Sources:** `use-cases/fx-wellness-rewards.md` ("Why now"); `audience.md` shared spine
- **Approved claims:** none required
- **Boundary:** no "AI-powered future," no revolution language (§13). Cite conditions, not destiny.

### H2.4 — Where body data fits in a wellness program workflow
- **Goal:** concrete workflow the reader can map onto their own product.
- **Word count target:** 400-500
- **Must-cover:**
  - Baseline scan at onboarding → program personalization → repeat check-ins at program-defined
    intervals → visual comparison surfaced to the member → structured record for reporting.
  - Who does what: the platform owns the program logic and the member relationship; the body-data
    layer owns capture, measurement output, and the comparable record.
  - Where a coach or program administrator stays in the loop.
  - Cadence realism: check-in frequency should match the pace at which body change is measurable.
- **Keywords to weave:** wellness program software, corporate wellness tracking platform
- **Sources:** `use-cases/fx-digital-fitness.md`; `use-cases/fx-wellness-rewards.md`; `how-it-works.md`
- **Approved claims:** FX-006, FX-007
- **Boundary:** the workflow *supports* a coach or administrator. It never *triggers* an outcome.

### H2.5 — Personalization: from stated goals to measured starting points
- **Goal:** the personalization half of the hub thesis.
- **Word count target:** 350-450
- **Must-cover:**
  - What a measured baseline lets a program do that a survey cannot: set realistic targets, tailor
    content, segment cohorts by starting point rather than by stated intent.
  - Re-personalization: the program adapts as the body record changes across check-ins.
  - Honest limit: body data personalizes *around* a program; it does not design the program, and
    it says nothing about a member's motivation, schedule, or preferences.
  - One paragraph on where nutrition/lifestyle coaching picks this up → forward-link to the
    planned P1 "Body Data for Nutrition and Lifestyle Coaching Platforms."
- **Keywords to weave:** wellness coach app, personalization, wellness tracker app
- **Sources:** `use-cases/fx-digital-fitness.md`; `messaging.md`
- **Approved claims:** FX-008, FX-009
- **Boundary:** no prescriptive advice, no calorie/macro/training *recommendations* attributed to
  the scan. Personalization means better inputs, not automated instructions.

### H2.6 — Progress visibility: what changes when members can see change
- **Goal:** the engagement half of the hub thesis — the strongest wellness-safe argument we have.
- **Word count target:** 400-500
- **Must-cover:**
  - The core mechanic: side-by-side 3D comparison and measurement deltas make small real change
    legible when the scale is flat or moving the wrong way.
  - Why repeatability is the property that makes this work at all: if scan-to-scan noise exceeds
    real change, the comparison misleads. Scan-to-scan variance < 1 cm; 95%+ repeatability
    consistency (FX-003, FX-004).
  - Which numbers a wellness product should surface to members, and which it should keep internal.
  - Honest limit: progress visibility supports engagement; it does not by itself retain anyone.
- **Keywords to weave:** wellness app, app engagement, progress tracking
- **Sources:** `proof-points.md` (Repeatability); `/content-hub/mobile-body-scanning-accuracy/`;
  `/content-hub/ai-in-fitness-industry/` (sideways, for the fitness statement of the same idea)
- **Approved claims:** FX-003, FX-004
- **Boundary:** **no retention or engagement uplift number.** None exists for a wellness
  deployment (see `claim_gaps` in the context pack). Write the mechanism, not a percentage.

### H2.7 — Employer and insurer wellness programs: participation, rewards, and reporting
- **Goal:** cover the second buyer, then hand off to the sub-hub. This is the section that carries
  the primary keyword.
- **Word count target:** 300-400
- **Must-cover:**
  - How the employer/insurer case differs: incentives, fairness across a distributed population,
    disputes, auditability, program reporting.
  - Remote standardized check-ins reduce administrative burden versus onsite screening events.
  - Explicit handoff: "rewards verification is covered in depth in [Wellness Rewards
    Verification]" — 2-3 paragraphs maximum here, then link.
  - Fraud framing must be *support*: structured, timestamped, consistently captured records
    support review. Never automatic detection.
- **Keywords to weave:** **corporate wellness platform** (primary — exact match, twice max),
  employee wellness app, wellness program software, employee wellness program
- **Sources:** `use-cases/fx-wellness-rewards.md`;
  `/content-hub/wellness-rewards-verification-employers-insurers-using-ai-3d-body-scanning/`
- **Approved claims:** FX-014 (HIPAA/GDPR posture) — only as a one-line procurement note
- **Boundary:** hardest section in the article. Never imply the scan decides a reward, a tier, an
  incentive, or eligibility. Never "detects fraud." Never publish the internal TAM/SAM figures
  from the use-case file. Keep claims soft.

### H2.8 — Where FitXpress fits, and what it does not do
- **Goal:** product placement plus the honesty section that keeps this hub credible.
- **Word count target:** 350-450
- **Must-cover:**
  - What FitXpress is here: a capture and structured-data layer inside the platform's own product
    — two photos, 80+ measurements, body composition, 3D model, under 45 seconds (FX-006-FX-009).
  - The "we provide / you build" boundary: API/SDK, the platform keeps the member experience.
  - Grounding for the model: 9+ years of training data, 150,000+ photographs, 30,000+ 3D scans,
    430,000+ measurements, ages 16-78, 38-210 kg, 150-205 cm (FX-010, FX-011).
  - **What it does not do** (its own sub-list): does not diagnose or screen; does not decide
    rewards, eligibility, or program tiers; does not replace a clinician, a DEXA scan, a BIA
    device, or a calibrated scale; does not detect fraud automatically; does not make a program
    compliant.
- **Keywords to weave:** 3d body scanning app, body scanning app
- **Sources:** `about-me.md` (claims discipline); `proof-points.md`; `tech-spec.md`
- **Approved claims:** FX-006, FX-007, FX-008, FX-009, FX-010, FX-011
- **Boundary:** this section *is* the boundary. Write it in the same breath as the capability, not
  as a disclaimer footer.

### H2.9 — What wellness platforms should evaluate in a body-data provider
- **Goal:** decision framework — the MOFU/BOFU value of the hub, and the trust-asset anchor.
- **Word count target:** 400-500
- **Must-cover:**
  - Accuracy asked properly: accurate for which decision, against which reference method, under
    which capture protocol, for which population, at what tolerance. Link to the accuracy
    framework as the canonical source rather than restating numbers.
  - Repeatability as the property that actually matters for progress tracking (distinct from
    accuracy) — this is the reframe move for a wellness buyer.
  - Capture reliability across a real distributed population: lighting, phones, clothing, pose.
  - Privacy and data handling as a procurement gate: what is stored, photo retention and deletion,
    server geography, member deletion requests, HIPAA and GDPR posture → link to the central
    Data/Privacy/Security & Regulatory FAQ. **(Placeholder — that FAQ is still unpublished; see
    open item 5.)**
  - Integration effort: API/SDK, time to a working check-in flow, who owns the UI.
  - A short decision framework or checklist (no vendor comparison table — that is the P1 listicle).
- **Keywords to weave:** corporate wellness platform requirements, corporate wellness platform
