Priority corrections

0. Follow the General Approach & Language Guardrails for Corporate Content - 3DLOOK (asseyla-style-guide). On surface: Too casual language for a healthcare-enterprise tone. No em dashes; use explicit anchor text for internal links; no "Plus" connector; no banned words (leverage/utilize/harness/robust/seamless/comprehensive/delve/navigate/tapestry/realm/unlock/unleash/game-changing/cutting-edge/revolutionary/disrupt/objective/reader); no "this article"; not "by hand".

1. Correct the connected-scale table entry
"One aggregate figure" is inaccurate because these scales usually return weight plus several estimated outputs.
Replace with: "Structured weight record plus device-generated composition estimates"

2. Remove the blanket restriction on clinical use
This scope note is too broad:
"none of these tools should be treated as a way to…make eligibility, prescribing, or treatment decisions."
DXA, professional BIA, calibrated scales, and other regulated tools may inform clinical decisions under applicable protocols.
Replace with:
This article is a tools-evaluation resource, not medical advice. The role any output plays in eligibility, prescribing, or treatment decisions depends on the tool, its regulatory status, the program protocol, and clinician review.
The FAQ also needs correction:
Old Q&A: "Is remote body composition data used to be used in eligibility or treatment decisions?" / "No."
Replace with:
Q: "Can remote body-composition data inform clinical decisions?"
A: "Its role depends on the tool, its regulatory status, and the program protocol. FitXpress provides supporting body-data estimates for clinician review and does not independently determine eligibility, prescribing, or treatment."

3. Soften the clinical claim in the market section
Too absolute: "Composition, not just weight, is the clinical detail that matters most…"
Replace with:
Body composition can add important context that scale weight alone does not provide.
Also change:
"GLP-1 delivery has grown into a largely remote, telehealth-first category."
to:
Many GLP-1 programs now operate through remote or hybrid care models.
(Current sentence needs evidence to establish that the entire category is "largely" telehealth-first.)

4. Add genuine examples of tools or services
"Consumer product lines," "InBody-class devices," and "imaging centers" are too vague. Add two or three representative examples for relevant categories, clearly labelled as examples rather than endorsements:
* Connected scales
* Professional BIA systems
* DXA access services
* Wearables with composition estimates
(Use well-known real product/company names as EXAMPLES, clearly labelled as examples, not endorsements. Do not invent vendors. If unsure of a real vendor name, keep the category generic rather than fabricating.)

5. Remove the remaining prescribed hybrid model
This FitXpress entry says: "alongside a reference method at milestones" - still presented as a required workflow. Replace with:
Remote intake and recurring between-visit check-ins that produce a structured record for review
Similarly, the conclusion still uses: "periodic reference-grade assessment"
Use: "periodic detailed or in-clinic assessment"
("Reference-grade" is undefined and overgeneralizes professional BIA; do not use it.)

6. Correct the progress-photo cost
"None" ignores storage, workflow, platform, and review costs.
Replace: "None" with "No dedicated hardware; potentially low incremental cost"

7. Distinguish direct measurements from estimates more carefully
The article does this well in the buyer checklist, but not consistently in the category descriptions.
- For DXA "Data captured: fat mass, lean mass…" → use: "Outputs: estimated fat mass, lean mass, and regional breakdowns"
- For manual measurement: "Data captured: directly measured circumferences and skinfolds; body-composition values may then be estimated using formulas"
- For FitXpress, separate:
   - Body measurements generated from image analysis
   - Estimated composition outputs
   - Calculated outputs such as BMI and BMR
   - 3D model

* "members enrolled for 30, 60, or 90 days"
* "A device that lives in a clinic room…reaches almost no one between appointments"
* "FitXpress handles the part…reference methods cannot reach affordably"
* "FitXpress is used in production by…Yazen" (KEEP as in v5-revision1: name Yazen as a customer WITHOUT the scan-count figure; this is the approved, qualified form.)

"In-clinic equipment cannot support at-home capture between appointments."
and:
"FitXpress supports remote intake and recurring between-visit capture without dedicated scanning hardware."
