**Working title:** Manual vs Digital Intake in Occupational Health Screening: Which Method Fits Which
Workflow

**What is being reviewed:** the outline of an unwritten article. Nothing has been drafted. Structural
objections cost one line of edit at this stage. The same objection after a draft exists costs a

## Part 1. The outline

Twelve sections. Section titles below are final as planned and are quoted exactly. Two tables are
required: the method comparison in Section 5 and the does-and-does-not table in Section 8.

The primary keyword `manual vs digital intake` has to appear as an exact string in the H1, in the
first paragraph, and in at least one H2. Section 5 carries the H2 occurrence. That placement is a
mechanical gate in the publishing pipeline, and it is the reason the H1 uses the short form rather
than the full topic phrase.

### Section 1. The intake step, not the examination, is where screening programs lose time

**Goal:** the reader recognises the problem as their own within two sentences, and knows the scope of
the page before reading further. **Words: 200.**

Must cover:

- Open on the operational fact with no runway: the measurement and questionnaire step sits inside a
  capacity-constrained appointment slot.
- Name the four costs the strategy row owns: throughput, missing or incomplete data, rescreens,
  multi-site inconsistency.
- State what the page decides: which intake method fits which program, and on what dimensions the two
  methods actually differ.
- A scope note early, with an italic disclaimer. Non-negotiable for this vertical.
- An up-link to the hub on a meaningful anchor phrase, for readers who want the category rather than
  the comparison.

Boundary: the scope note states intake and documentation only. It says that FitXpress does not perform
examinations, does not make fitness-for-duty or clearance determinations, and is not a basis for hiring
or employment decisions.

### Section 2. Short answer: what each intake method actually covers

**Goal:** a summary an answer engine can extract, and clean definitions of both methods, before any
argument. **Words: 240.**

Must cover:

- A quick answer block of four to five bullets. Each bullet is one method or one verdict.
- Manual intake defined: written or paper health questionnaire, staff-administered tape measurement,
  transcription into the screening record, all at or around the appointment.
- Digital intake defined: the same information collected through a structured remote channel before the
  appointment, with body measurement captured by guided smartphone scan.
- One honest verdict bullet: the two are not substitutes across the whole of intake. Digital intake
  covers a subset of what manual intake covers, and that subset is where the operational cost sits.

Boundary: define the two **methods**, not the category. The hub owns the definition of occupational
health screening intake and answers it in its own FAQ.

### Section 3. Why the intake bottleneck is getting more expensive

**Goal:** establish the "why now" on a fresh figure rather than repeating the hub's injury statistic.
**Words: 170.**

Must cover:

- Hiring volume drives pre-employment screening intake and draws on a fixed supply of appointment
  slots. Cite the US Bureau of Labor Statistics Job Openings and Labor Turnover Summary, July 2026:
  hires 5.1 million, hires rate 3.2 percent, described in the release as little changed over the
  month.
- Multi-site and multi-vendor programs raise the cost of inconsistency, because records have to be
  comparable across locations before they are reviewable.
- Documentation expectations have moved upstream: review teams expect structured records.
- Adding clinic capacity does not address an intake problem.

Boundary: hiring volume is context for screening demand. Nothing may connect body data to hiring
volume in a way that implies body data informs hiring.

### Section 4. Occupational health intake, component by component

**Goal:** the article's central mechanism. Split intake into its parts and mark which parts a digital
channel can carry and which stay manual or clinician-led. This is what makes the comparison honest
instead of a sweep. **Words: 260.**

Must cover:

- Four components: health history and symptom questionnaire; body measurement and BMI inputs;
  modality-specific testing (drug, vision, hearing, functional); the examination and clinician review
  itself.
- Only two of the four are candidates for a remote digital channel, the questionnaire and the body
  measurement. Say so plainly.
- Why manual body measurement varies: it is technique-, landmark- and team-dependent, which is why
  national measurement protocols specify posture, tape position, tension and a recorder role in
  detail. Ground this in the CDC/NCHS NHANES Anthropometry Procedures Manual, quoting the protocol
  text directly from the PDF.
- The regulator already treats the questionnaire as a routed document rather than a clinic activity.
  OSHA's mandatory respirator medical evaluation questionnaire (Appendix C to 29 CFR 1910.134) must be
  answerable during working hours or at a time and place convenient to the employee, the employer must
  not review the answers, and the employer must tell the employee how to route it to the reviewing
  health care professional. This is the freshest point in the article. A digital intake channel is one
  way to meet that routing and confidentiality posture.

Boundary: naming the OSHA respirator questionnaire is a fact about intake mechanics. It is not a claim
that FitXpress administers that questionnaire, satisfies 1910.134, or supports respirator clearance.
State the routing principle, then state that the medical evaluation and the clearance determination
stay with the reviewing health care professional.

### Section 5. Manual and digital intake compared, dimension by dimension

**Goal:** the comparison table, and a reading of it that names where each method wins. Core of the
article. **Words: 280.**

Must cover:

- A clean table, two method columns, one dimension per row. Dimensions: where the step happens; who
  performs the body measurement; consistency across staff, sites and vendors; record format and time
  stamping; confidentiality routing of the questionnaire; time consumed inside the appointment slot;
  what the method can capture; access requirement for the candidate or employee; setup and
  change-management cost.
- **Digital does not win every row, and the table has to show that.** Manual intake wins on scope (it
  carries the whole of intake, including the parts no remote channel can), on access (no smartphone or
  network dependency), and on setup cost (no integration, no configuration, no change management).
- A short prose reading of the table: the dimensions digital improves are the ones that produce
  rescreens and multi-site inconsistency; the dimensions manual holds are scope, access and cost of
  change.
- One sentence stating that these row-by-row differences are what the decision in Section 9 turns on.

Boundary: no row and no sentence may claim digital intake is more accurate than manual measurement.
The company's own accuracy figure is measured **against** expert manual measurement as the reference,
so "more accurate than manual" is not a claim available here. The consistency row stays qualitative;
no accuracy or repeatability figure appears in this section. It belongs in Section 7 with its
conditions and its link.

### Section 6. Where FitXpress fits in a digital intake workflow

**Goal:** place the product against the two components identified in Section 4, briefly, without
rebuilding the hub's workflow walk-through. **Words: 190.**

Must cover:

- FitXpress covers one component, remote pre-appointment body measurement capture. Name that
  component, and say that the questionnaire, the testing and the examination sit elsewhere in the
  program's stack.
- The three properties that matter here: capture happens before the appointment on the candidate's or
  employee's own device; outputs are structured and time-stamped; the compliance posture holds at
  procurement (HIPAA maintained for US healthcare contexts, GDPR principles for EU processing,
  encryption at rest and in transit, no personal identifiers processed, photos deleted immediately or
  within 30 days per client policy).
- A down-link to the FitXpress parent page on a descriptive anchor.

Boundary: FitXpress supports clinician review as a remote intake and documentation layer, never as a
clearance, eligibility or fitness-for-duty input. The phrase "speeds clearance decisions" is banned as
a capability claim. If time-to-decision is mentioned, the sentence says the path to the determination
is shorter and the determination itself stays with the licensed provider and employer policy. Do not
restate the hub's five-step implementation sequence.

### Section 7. What improves operationally, and what the evidence behind it actually says

**Goal:** the genuinely open ground. The hub states no accuracy or repeatability figure anywhere. This
section introduces repeatability as the property that matters for a manual-versus-digital comparison,
with its conditions attached. **Words: 220.**

Must cover:

- The reframe: the buyer's question is not "how accurate is it" but "accurate enough for which
  decision", and for intake documentation the decision is whether records are comparable across staff,
  sites and time points.
- Repeatability, quoted in the approved words, with its conditions: internal repeatability testing on
  a real-world customer dataset, five repeated scans per participant.
- Accuracy, quoted in the approved words, with its reference stated in the same breath: measured
  against expert manual measurement, over a defined population scope, and varying by body part.
- What that combination means for this comparison, stated carefully: expert manual measurement is the
  reference the figure is measured against, so the case for digital intake rests on consistency across
  repeats and across sites, not on beating a tape measure.
- The operational outcomes, hedged: measurement time moves out of the appointment slot; records arrive
  before review; missing and inconsistent fields fall, which is the common cause of rescreens.
- The link to the accuracy framework page goes **in the paragraph carrying the figure**, not in a
  further-reading list.

Boundary: three hard constraints, all gated. The company has two separate accuracy benchmarks measured
against different references, and they are never combined in one paragraph. The second benchmark
appears nowhere in this article at all. An internal repeatability percentage exists and is not
publishable, because the published framework page describes the same study and gives no such figure.
No per-measurement figures: "varying by body part" is the published level of detail, and detailed
methodology is available under NDA. Outcomes are stated as "can reduce" and "supports", never as
guarantees.

### Section 8. What FitXpress does not do

**Goal:** the boundary, stated once and cleanly, in the section the house structure reserves for it.
**Words: 130.**

Must cover:

- A two-column table: what the intake layer supports, and what it does not do.
- Does not: diagnose; perform or replace the occupational health examination; make fitness-for-duty or
  clearance determinations; inform hiring or employment decisions; replace clinician review or employer
  policy; guarantee compliance.
- One closing sentence on compliance framing: evaluation runs on data-privacy and recordkeeping
  frameworks, and the regulatory classification of any deployment depends on intended use, deployment
  context and jurisdiction.

Boundary: this is where the sentence "It is not positioned as a medical device." belongs if Section 1
has not already used it. Once in the article, not twice. State each boundary once and do not chain
negatives inside a sentence. The hub has its own does-and-does-not table; this one is shorter and
pointed at the comparison rather than copied.

### Section 9. A decision framework: which intake method fits which program

**Goal:** the second core block. Tell a buyer when manual intake is still the right answer. This is
the section that earns the article's credibility. **Words: 260.**

Must cover:

- **When manual intake remains the right choice:** single-site or low-volume programs where the slot is
  not the constraint; populations without reliable smartphone or network access; intake dominated by
  history, symptom and functional content; workflows where the measurement is part of the examination
  itself; programs with no downstream system able to receive structured records.
- **When a digital intake channel earns its cost:** high candidate or employee volume against fixed
  appointment capacity; multiple sites, clinics or vendor partners needing comparable records;
  documented rescreen and rework volume traceable to missing or inconsistent intake data;
  documentation that has to hold up across reporting periods.
- **The realistic answer is hybrid, stated as a positive:** the questionnaire and the body measurement
  move to a remote channel, the testing and the examination stay in the clinic, and the program decides
  the split by component rather than wholesale.
- Frame the choice as a set of conditions on the program, using "depends on" and "varies by".
- Cite US EEOC enforcement guidance on pre-employment disability-related questions and medical
  examinations for one sentence placing pre-employment medical examination content after a conditional
  offer.

Boundary: the framework decides an **intake method**. It never decides a candidate, an employee, a
clearance or an eligibility. The EEOC citation marks the post-offer boundary the workflow sits inside.
Do not restate the hub's fuller EEOC passage, and do not frame anything as a pre-offer medical
examination.

### Section 10. Buyer fit and the questions to ask before switching

**Goal:** who gets the most value from the switch, and what an evaluation should establish.
**Words: 190.**

Must cover:

- Which buyer profile the switch pays back fastest for, one or two sentences each, then route readers
  to the hub for the full roster: occupational health providers and clinic networks; workforce
  screening vendors running multi-employer contracts; multi-site employers. Workers' compensation and
  absence administrators get a single clause only.
- Evaluation questions worth answering before a pilot: which intake components move; what the current
  rescreen rate is and what causes it; whether the downstream system can receive structured records;
  what happens for a candidate who cannot complete a remote capture; how repeatability was measured and
  over how many repeated scans.
- An up-link to the hub on the buyer-profile phrase.

Boundary: keep workers' compensation and return-to-work thin. Two unwritten sibling articles own that
ground (see Part 4). No return-to-work delay framing, no workers' compensation program-ops detail, no
baseline-before-injury argument. One clause and a link up.

### Section 11. Frequently asked questions

**Goal:** answer-engine coverage of questions the live hub does not answer. Answers of 2 to 5
sentences. **Words: 300.**

Six questions. None restates any of the hub's twelve distinct questions, checked one by one (Part 4).

1. **Is digital intake more accurate than manual tape measurement in occupational health screening?**
   The honest answer, and the reframe. The accuracy figure is measured against expert manual
   measurement as the reference, so the useful question is whether the expected error is acceptable for
   the decision the record supports, and whether repeated measurements are comparable. Carries the
   repeatability claim with its condition and the link to the framework page.
2. **Which parts of occupational health intake cannot be moved to a digital channel?** History and
   symptom review that needs clinical follow-up, modality-specific testing, functional assessment, and
   the examination itself.
3. **What happens if a candidate cannot complete a remote scan?** The program keeps the manual path as
   the fallback, and the answer says plainly that a digital intake channel does not remove the need for
   one. Access varies by workforce and by role.
4. **Does moving intake to a digital channel change the post-offer boundary for pre-employment
   screening?** No. The boundary is set by the timing and content of the medical examination, not by
   the channel the intake data arrives through. Cites EEOC guidance.
5. **Can digital intake records be compared against measurements taken manually at an earlier
   appointment?** Carefully, and only where the program documents both. The two references differ, so a
   mixed-method series is weaker than a consistent one, which is the argument for standardising the
   method rather than the record.
6. **What should a program measure to know whether digital intake worked?** Rescreen rate and its
   causes, intake completeness at the point of review, measurement time inside the appointment slot,
   and record comparability across sites.

Boundary: Q1 must not turn into an accuracy pitch and must not combine the two benchmarks. Q4 must not
read as legal advice: state what the boundary is set by, and leave the determination with the
employer's counsel and the licensed provider. No question and no answer may touch clearance
decisioning.

## Part 2. The keyword, and the alternatives that were priced and rejected

### The finding, stated first

**There is no measured, in-vertical, un-owned head term available to this page.** Every candidate is
one of four things: owned by the live hub, reserved for a planned sibling article, off-vertical, or
unmeasured. The chosen primary keyword has no measured search volume at all.

**This page is therefore a GEO and answer-engine play plus a long-tail procurement-question play, with
no measured head demand.** Its classical-search upside is close to zero by design. The return it is
being bought for is answer-engine coverage of the "which intake method fits which workflow" question,
and the cluster authority it feeds back to the hub.

Do not read this section as "keywords verified, volume looks fine". It is the opposite finding, and it
is the first thing worth a reviewer's judgment. If a P0 slot should not be spent on a page with no
measured search demand, that decision is available now at no cost.

### How to read the data

The figures come from three Ahrefs API v3 keyword pulls run on **2026-09-03**, US market, 2,366 API
units spent in total:

1. the topic seed, `manual intake vs digital intake in occupational health screening`

Two data states are different facts and are written differently below:

- **"no data"** means Ahrefs returns no measurement for the phrase. It is an absence of measurement,

- **volume 0** means measured, with no demand found.

`manual intake` on its own is volume **0**. The other fragments of the topic phrase are "no data".
