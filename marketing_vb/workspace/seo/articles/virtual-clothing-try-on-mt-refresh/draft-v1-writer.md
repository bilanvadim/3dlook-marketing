# Virtual Try-On Clothes in 2026: How It Works and Where Fit Comes From

By Assel Sekerova

(Cover) - Concept: two panels. Left, a phone showing a try-on image of a jacket on a person's photo (the look). Right, a size chart with waist, hip, and inseam callouts (the fit). No 3DLOOK or Mobile Tailor interface in the try-on panel, and no numbers.

Virtual try-on for clothes has moved from augmented reality (AR) filters and in-store mirrors into search engines and AI assistants. Google brought AI try-on to Search in the US in July 2025. OpenAI added a Try On button to ChatGPT on October 1, 2026. <!-- ext-claim: GOOG-TRYON-US-2025 --> <!-- ext-claim: TC-CHATGPT-2026 -->

Virtual clothing try-on shows a garment on an image of the shopper. That image comes from a live camera view, a 3D avatar, or a photo processed by an AI model.

The three approaches differ in how they work, where shoppers find them, and what they do for brands. They also differ in how much they reveal about fit. Try-on shows how a garment looks. Size and fit come from body measurements.

## Virtual try-on for clothes: the short answer

- **What it is.** Software that shows a garment on an image of the shopper.
- **How it works.** Shoppers try on clothes virtually through AR overlays on a live camera, 3D avatars with simulated garments, or generative AI images made from a photo.
- **Where to find it.** Search engines, AI assistants, retailer websites and apps, and some in-store screens.
- **What it helps with.** Judging color, style, and proportions on the shopper's own photo before buying.
- **What it leaves open.** Size and fit. Both depend on body measurements, compared with the garment's size chart or pattern.

## How virtual try-on clothes tools work: AR overlays, 3D avatars, and generative AI

Each approach needs different input from the shopper and the brand, and each returns a different kind of image.

### AR overlays on a live camera

The camera on a phone or an in-store screen tracks the shopper's body or face. Software draws the garment over the live image in real time, and the overlay moves with the shopper. AR works best for shoes, eyewear, accessories, and tops, where the item sits on a clear point of the body. Drape and size are approximate. A separate guide to [AR clothing try-on tools](https://3dlook.ai/content-hub/ar-clothing-try-on-tools/) covers the history of the approach and its early adopters.

### 3D avatars and simulated garments

A 3D avatar is built from the shopper's height, weight, and body measurements, or from a body scan. Each garment needs a 3D digital twin that carries its fabric properties. The software simulates the garment on the avatar, taking fabric behavior and the shopper's fit preference into account. Shoppers can turn the avatar and see the garment from several angles.

Of the three approaches, 3D virtual try-on is the nearest thing to a fit preview. It is also the most expensive to run, because every product needs its own 3D asset. How the body side of a 3D try-on is captured is explained in the overview of [body scanning technology for apparel](https://3dlook.ai/content-hub/body-scanning-technology-for-apparel/).

### Generative AI from a single photo

The shopper uploads a full-length photo or a selfie. An image model then generates a picture of the shopper wearing the item. AI virtual try-on for clothes covers a whole catalog without 3D assets, because the model works from ordinary product images. Google's try-on and ChatGPT's Try On both work this way. ChatGPT's feature runs on the ChatGPT Images 2.5 model, according to TechCrunch. <!-- ext-claim: TC-CHATGPT-2026 --> The result is a generated image of how the item could look on the shopper.

## Virtual fitting rooms in stores and online

Live video try-on, where the garment follows the shopper in real time, runs mainly on AR. Shoppers find it as lenses in camera and retailer apps and on in-store screens.

In stores, a virtual fitting room is usually a smart mirror or a screen in or near the fitting area. Tesco began testing one in the F&F clothing department of its Sandhurst Extra store in September 2026, [Retail Gazette reported](https://retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/09/tesco-trials-virtual-try-on). Shoppers take a photo on the spot or upload one, then try on about 1,500 F&F menswear and womenswear items. Tesco said the trial is "designed to understand how Tesco can help customers browsing in store to discover new styles." <!-- ext-claim: RG-TESCO-2026 -->

Online, the fitting room is usually a try-on button or widget on the product page. It is built on an AR overlay, a 3D avatar, or a generated image. Some retailers call it a virtual dressing room. The guide to the [virtual fitting room for ecommerce](https://3dlook.ai/content-hub/virtual-fitting-room-for-ecommerce/) covers the software types and their benefits in more depth.

- **ChatGPT (OpenAI).** OpenAI launched a Try On button in ChatGPT's shopping results globally on October 1, 2026, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/01/chatgpt-can-now-virtually-try-on-clothes-for-you/). Each of these shows how an item looks, and the shopper still picks the size separately.

## How to try on clothes virtually before you buy

To try on clothes virtually, choose a tool that supports try-on for the item and add a full-length photo or a selfie. Then review the generated image before you buy.

You have five main routes:

1. **Google Search or Shopping.** Tap the "try it on" icon on a supported apparel listing and upload a full-length photo. Shoppers in the US can also start from a selfie. <!-- ext-claim: GOOG-HOWTO --> <!-- ext-claim: GOOG-SELFIE-2025 -->
2. **ChatGPT.** Select Try On in the shopping results, or upload a screenshot of an item and ask ChatGPT to try it on. <!-- ext-claim: TC-CHATGPT-2026 -->
3. **A retailer's own website or app.** Look for a try-on option on the product page, where the retailer offers one.
4. **An in-store screen.** Some stores now test try-on screens in the clothing department. <!-- ext-claim: RG-TESCO-2026 -->
5. **AR lenses.** Camera and social apps show some items on you in a live camera view.

AR virtual try-on apps for clothes fall into two groups: camera and social apps with lenses, and retailers' own apps with a live camera view. Coverage depends on which items each brand has prepared for AR.

The widely available platforms for trying on clothes virtually run on phones, laptops, and in-store screens. None of them needs a virtual reality (VR) headset.

A few habits improve the result. [Google recommends](https://blog.google/products/shopping/how-to-use-google-shopping-try-it-on) a full-body shot, good lighting, and fitted clothing for the photo. <!-- ext-claim: GOOG-HOWTO --> Before uploading, check what the tool does with the photo. Then choose the size from the brand's size chart, because the image shows the look and leaves the size to you.

## What virtual try-on does for fashion brands

**Confidence before purchase.** Shoppers see the item on their own photo before they order. Retailers are still testing what that changes, and Tesco's Sandhurst trial is one such test. <!-- ext-claim: RG-TESCO-2026 -->

**Engagement and sharing.** Try-on images are made to be kept and shown to others. Google's try-on and Doppl allow shoppers to save and share looks. ChatGPT keeps saved products alongside try-on images, TechCrunch reported. <!-- ext-claim: GOOG-HOWTO --> <!-- ext-claim: GOOG-DOPPL-2025 --> <!-- ext-claim: TC-CHATGPT-2026 --> For a brand, a saved or shared look can keep a product in view after the session ends.

**Returns.** Returns carry a large cost. Retailers estimated that 15.8% of their annual sales would be returned in 2025, and 19.3% of online sales, according to the [National Retail Federation](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/consumers-expected-to-return-nearly-850-billion-in-merchandise-in-2025). <!-- ext-claim: NRF-RETURNS-2025 --> The estimate covers every retail category, apparel among them. A garment can come back because it looks different than expected, or because the size or cut is wrong. Try-on addresses the first cause. The second needs the right size, and the right size depends on body measurements.

## Can virtual try-on show which size will fit?

In any virtual fitting room, realistic sizing for jeans starts with the shopper's waist, hip, thigh, rise, and inseam. Those measurements are compared with the brand's size chart or the garment's own measurements. A try-on image shows the cut and color on the shopper's photo.

Google's selfie try-on asks shoppers to [select their usual clothing size](https://blog.google/products/shopping/studio-quality-digital-try-on/) (December 2025). Google's [Doppl app](https://blog.google/technology/google-labs/doppl/) states: "Fit, appearance and clothing details may not always be accurate." <!-- ext-claim: GOOG-SELFIE-2025 --> <!-- ext-claim: GOOG-DOPPL-2025 -->

A try-on image helps answer how clothes will look in person. Product photos on models of a similar build add a second view. Fit is a separate question, answered by the shopper's own measurements against the size chart. The guide to [taking body measurements at home](https://3dlook.ai/content-hub/how-to-take-your-body-measurements-at-home/) covers the method.

A true fit preview would show how long a garment runs, how it sits on the hips, and whether it pulls at the waist. That takes two sets of data: the body's measurements and the garment's pattern measurements. 3D avatar tools come closest, for the products a brand has digitized. For clothing cut to the body or allocated by size, the process therefore starts from measurements.

## Frequently asked questions about virtual try-on

### Which website can I use to try on clothes with my own 3D model?

The widely available tools, from Google and ChatGPT, work from a photo of you. 3D avatar try-on appears on individual brand sites, for the products each brand has digitized. On Zara.com, Zara Try-On builds a synthetic avatar from your own photos. In custom clothing, a 3D body model from a phone scan serves another purpose: the brand needs the measurements to make the garment.

### Can I try on clothes online for free?

Usually, yes. Google's try-on sits on apparel listings in Search and Shopping, which are free to use. Retailer features such as Zara Try-On are part of the store itself. Availability varies by country, product, and service.

### What happens to the photo I upload for virtual try-on?

It depends on the tool. Before uploading a full-length photo, read the tool's privacy policy and check its settings. Look for whether the photo is stored, for how long, and whether you can delete it.

Try-on images will likely keep improving and spreading as more search engines and AI assistants add the feature. The size decision will still rest on body measurements.
