### Part I — Data lifecycle

### H2.1 — What data does FitXpress process and generate?
- **Goal:** Enumerate and *categorize* the data, then give the lifecycle table. Establish that outputs are not all "measurements."
- **Words:** 300–360 (incl. table).
- **Must-cover:** Submitted data (front/side photos, height, **optional** weight, sex/gender input where the model requires it, profile info, customer/scan identifiers); Generated data (80+ measurements; body composition — BMI, BMR [Mifflin-St Jeor], body fat % [US Navy], lean mass, fat mass, Smart Scales weight estimate ±2.1 kg MAE; 3D model; progress-tracking data); Technical data (capture-quality/pose/clothing flags, face-obfuscation confirmation, logs/timestamps/request metadata). **Lifecycle table** rows: Photos · Measurements & body metrics · Body composition data · 3D model/mesh · Progress-tracking data · Identifiers & logs — columns: Purpose · Stored by 3DLOOK? · Retention · Deletion method.
- **Keywords:** body measurement data storage, body composition data privacy, 3D body model data.
- **Boundary:** Do not describe all outputs as measurements (brief §5.3). Body composition = *estimates*, not measurements. Do not overstate weight as a scale reading — it is a software estimate (±2.1 kg MAE). Keep optional weight labeled optional.

### H2.2 — How are data storage, retention, and deletion handled?
- **Goal:** Answer photo/output/progress retention, hosting, deletion, backups, and exceptions together, with exact periods.
- **Words:** 280–340.
- **Must-cover:** Photos deleted **immediately after processing** by default; where a client-specific policy retains them (up to 30 days), retained photos are **auto-blurred**; face obfuscation applied at capture. SDK distinction: when the customer collects photos via the FitXpress SDK and sends them to the 3DLOOK API, the customer decides its own-system retention (separate from 3DLOOK). Measurements/body composition/3D models retained for the active contract term; enterprise-configurable windows. Hosting: AWS, primarily us-east-1; regional options for enterprise under contract. Deletion API: user-level + individual-scan; backups purged within a 30-day cycle; deletion may be deferred for legal hold or active security investigation. Link to technical docs / retention schedule / subprocessor list / deletion API docs.
- **Keywords:** AI body scan photo retention, AI body scan data deletion, body measurement data storage.
- **Boundary:** No vague timing ("stored temporarily", "shortly after processing", "as needed") — brief §4/§15. Use exact periods or state "implementation-dependent." Encryption keys (TLS/SSE-S3) → covered in §6, don't duplicate here.

### H2.3 — How does body and 3D model progress tracking work?
- **Goal:** Explain scan linkage, what can be compared, required historical data, who performs tracking, configurability, and deletion impact.
- **Words:** 240–300.
- **Must-cover:** Scans linked to one profile via customer-assigned identifier; comparable outputs = measurements, body composition (BMI/BMR/body fat %/lean/fat mass), weight estimates incl. Smart Scales, 3D models (side-by-side/overlay). Historical records required *scale to what is compared* (overlay needs both 3D models retained, etc.). Performed by 3DLOOK (API/dashboard), customer, or both. **Not enabled by default**; enterprise-configurable; not present in every deployment. Deleting a scan/user record removes it from progress history (subject to backup cycle in §2).
- **Keywords:** 3D body model data, body composition data privacy.
- **Boundary:** Not diagnostic; a visible 3D change ≠ a clinically meaningful change (brief §5.5). Do not imply every deployment retains full historical 3D models.

### Part II — Data rights and permitted use

### H2.4 — Who controls and owns FitXpress data?
- **Goal:** Split control/ownership across three roles and separate five distinct rights.
- **Words:** 260–320.
- **Must-cover:** *End users* — data-subject rights (access, correction, portability, deletion, restriction, objection), routed through the customer as controller; 3DLOOK supports fulfillment for data it processes. *Enterprise customers* — own submitted data + generated outputs (measurements/body composition/3D models); responsible for privacy notices, lawful basis, consent, retention policy, downstream use, securing their apps/integrations/API credentials, and deciding SDK-side photo retention. *3DLOOK* — limited processing rights to deliver the service; owns software/algorithms/models; **does not sell customer data or use it for advertising**; may use aggregated/anonymized data for internal analytics where the contract permits.
- **Keywords:** FitXpress data privacy security, body composition data privacy.
- **Boundary:** Do not say a party "owns the data" without separating personal-data rights vs contractual rights vs processing rights vs IP rights vs rights in generated outputs (brief §6). No "you/your/we/our."

### H2.5 — Does 3DLOOK use customer data to train AI models?
- **Goal:** Direct standalone answer; distinguish five data categories.
- **Words:** 180–230.
- **Must-cover:** **No** — 3DLOOK does not use production customer data (photos, measurements, body composition, 3D models) to train models **without the customer's explicit, contractually documented authorization**. Model development uses separately collected research/validation datasets under their own consent, distinct from production data. Customers can contractually prohibit training use via the DPA. Aggregated/anonymized analytics may be used internally where permitted. Technical service logs are a separate category, not repurposed for training.
- **Keywords:** AI body scanning privacy, FitXpress data privacy security.
- **Boundary:** Use "anonymized" only where the legal/technical standard is met (brief §7). Keep this a standalone question — do not fold into §4.

### Part IV — Privacy compliance

### H2.8 — How does FitXpress support HIPAA, GDPR, and CCPA/CPRA?
- **Goal:** Three clearly separated framework subsections. (Runs long — this single H2 legitimately exceeds the 400-word band because it carries three regulatory frameworks.)
- **Words:** 340–420.
- **Must-cover:** *HIPAA* — may apply when a covered entity / business associate handles PHI; 3DLOOK can act as a business associate under an executed **BAA** (enterprise, qualifying plans); supported safeguards (encryption in transit/at rest, access controls, audit logging); HIPAA is a **framework, not a certification**; customer keeps its own obligations incl. determining whether its use involves PHI. *GDPR & UK GDPR* — customer generally = controller, 3DLOOK = processor; Article 28 DPA with SCCs + UK Addendum; supports data-subject rights via retrieve/correct/delete mechanisms; special-category data (Art. 9) → customer identifies lawful basis + reflects in its notice/consent. *CCPA/CPRA* — 3DLOOK generally = service provider/contractor; no sale, no sharing for cross-context behavioral advertising; supports access/deletion/correction/opt-out; use limited to the agreement's business purposes.
- **Keywords:** HIPAA compliant body scanning, GDPR body scanning, CCPA body scanning.
- **Boundary:** Never "HIPAA certified/approved/automatically compliant", "GDPR certified", "fully compliant", "guaranteed compliance" (brief §10, §13). Keep every qualification next to its claim, not paragraphs later.

### H2.9 — Is FitXpress data biometric or health data?
- **Goal:** Qualified classification answer — no single label fits all outputs.
- **Words:** 220–280.
- **Must-cover:** Photos, measurements, 3D models = personal data when tied to an identifiable person. Body composition / weight metrics *may* be health or sensitive data depending on use (clinical/weight-management workflow vs apparel sizing). Under GDPR, data is **biometric** only when technical processing is used to **uniquely identify** a person — FitXpress does not perform unique identification, so a scan is not automatically biometric data. PHI status under HIPAA depends on who holds it + its relation to health/care/payment, not the data type. Customer, as the party setting purpose/means, classifies its own data; 3DLOOK supplies supporting documentation.
- **Keywords:** body scanning biometric data, body composition data privacy.
- **Boundary:** Do not call all measurements biometric data or all body composition outputs PHI (brief §11). Briefly define personal data / health data / biometric data where each first becomes relevant (brief §9) — no glossary dump.

### Part V — Certifications and regulatory status

### H2.10 — Is 3DLOOK SOC 2 certified?
- **Goal:** State the exact current position and offer alternative evidence.
- **Words:** 160–210.
- **Must-cover:** 3DLOOK has completed a **SOC 2 readiness assessment** and aligned controls to SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria; a **formal SOC 2 examination has not yet been conducted.** SOC 2 = an attestation examination by an independent auditor, not a product certification; the phrase "SOC 2 certified" is not used. Alternative evidence (pen-test summaries, security overview, questionnaire responses) available under NDA — overlapping but **not equivalent** to a completed examination.
- **Keywords:** SOC 2 body scanning software, SOC 2 body scanning.
- **Boundary:** Never "SOC 2 certified" or "SOC 2 compliant" (brief §12, §13). Do not imply readiness = examination.

### H2.11 — Is FitXpress FDA approved or regulated as a medical device?
- **Goal:** Five-beat regulatory answer (position → depends on intended use → category distinctions → does FitXpress decide → customer must assess whole workflow).
- **Words:** 280–340.
- **Must-cover:** FitXpress is **not cleared, authorized, or approved** by the FDA and is **not positioned as a medical device.** FDA treatment depends on intended use + claims. Distinguish general wellness (low-risk, generally outside premarket authorization) · administrative intake/documentation · progress tracking for engagement/monitoring · clinical support/diagnosis/treatment (may trigger requirements). FitXpress does not make medical/diagnostic/treatment decisions on its own — outputs need human interpretation / clinical judgment / customer-defined rules. Customer must assess whether its complete integrated workflow + marketed claims trigger FDA requirements. Clarify clearance (510(k)/Class II) vs approval (PMA/Class III) vs authorization are **not interchangeable**; some software falls outside both by intended use; FitXpress has pursued none.
- **Keywords:** FDA body scanning software.
- **Boundary:** Never "FDA approved" as a quality signal (brief §13). No diagnosis/treatment-decision claims. Do not turn into an FDA regulatory guide for unrelated software (brief §11).

### H2.12 — What uses are supported, and what decisions should not rely on FitXpress alone?
- **Goal:** Draw the supported-vs-standalone line explicitly.
- **Words:** 180–230.
- **Must-cover:** *Supports* — remote intake / measurement capture, body composition tracking, progress tracking, clinical research data collection (as a capture tool), eligibility documentation, patient/member engagement. *Should not independently determine* — diagnosis, treatment, fitness for duty, employment eligibility, insurance eligibility, clinical-trial eligibility, other high-impact individual decisions. Such workflows require validation, human review, customer-defined decision rules, legal compliance, alignment with approved intended use. FitXpress = a **supporting data layer**, not the decisioning system.
- **Keywords:** healthcare body scanning security (light), FitXpress data privacy security.
- **Boundary:** No language implying FitXpress independently makes clinical/employment/insurance/eligibility decisions (about-me claims discipline; brief §14). Use "supports review" workhorse framing.
