## 3. Unsupported or stale numbers: do not carry them over without a source

- "global fitness app market growing over 10% annually" (no source)
- "28% of gym members used AI apps daily in 2023" (gitnux, an aggregator)
- "53% after the first month" (exercise.com blog), "71% of users abandon their apps in 3 months" (dr-muscle.com)
- Stanford 2021 tracking study; 2017 AI health coach study (PMC6238835): old, and tangential to body scanning

**Use instead (already verified at primary sources for the fitness landing, 2026-10-02, see
`workspace/pages/for-connected-and-digital-fitness/fact-sheet.md`):**
- Adjust, *Mobile app retention benchmarks for 2023*: health & fitness apps, 24% of users active on day 1 and
  7% on day 30 (global, 2022 data).
- Kidman PG et al., J Med Internet Res 2024;26:e56897: a **median** of 70% of users stopped using lifestyle
  apps within the first 100 days; "lack of personalization" is among the reasons.

Any other fresh number or example must be checked at its primary source, with the date, before it goes in.

## 4. Angle for the refreshed page

- **Reader:** product, growth and founder roles at connected and digital fitness companies (coaching apps,
  connected-equipment platforms, workout apps, gym chains with an app). ICP segment "Connected & digital
  fitness", $1M+ revenue; buyers Founder/CEO, CPO, Head of Growth.
- **Owns:** the commercial case for adding a phone-based body scan to a fitness app. Where a scan sits in the
  member journey (baseline at onboarding, scheduled check-ins, progress review with a coach, a premium-tier
  feature), what it produces and what it does not, why visible progress matters for retention (the two
  sources above), the choice between in-club hardware, progress photos and an in-app scan, and how to test
  it with a member cohort before rolling it out.
- **Bridge, not a copy of the landing:** the landing (`/fitxpress/for-connected-and-digital-fitness/`) sells
  FitXpress. The article answers the category questions a product team asks first and sends them to the
  landing. **First CTA inside the first 30% of the text**, a mid CTA and a closing CTA, all to the landing
  (the one-CTA rule is overridden for this article, same as ROI and try-on).
- **Fresh examples:** the row asks for them. Category-level, verifiable, dated. No competitors by name
  (`blog-style-guide.md` §Competitors). Non-overlapping brands only, verified at a primary source; if a named
  app's scan is supplied by a competitor, describe the category instead of naming the app. Clients are not
  named in the blog unless a published case study exists.
- **AEO:** answer the "alternative to hardware 3D body scanners for fitness studios" and "body composition
  tracking for a fitness app with smartphone scans" questions directly, in plain question-shaped H2s or FAQ.
- **Keep the URL words in the H1/title:** "AI body scanning" and "fitness". The `fitness apps` framing from the
  Sheet row is the natural H1 angle. The planner decides and tests length.
- **Length:** a MOFU use-case article, 1,600–2,200 words. Not a hub.
