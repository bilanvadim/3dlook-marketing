## 2. Recommended changes before publication
### P1. Strengthen the early CTA
Now: The first commercial link appears in the short-answer section:
3DLOOK's FitXpress provides body scanning for fitness apps under the app's own brand.
This meets the location requirement, but the text behaves like a reference link rather than encouraging a prospective buyer to explore the solution.
How to change: Keep the existing paragraph, then add a short standalone CTA immediately after it.
See how FitXpress works for fitness apps. Explore the guided scan experience, member progress features, and integration options for connected and digital fitness platforms.
Explore FitXpress for Fitness Apps
Why: The refresh is partly intended to recover commercial performance. With the reported decline from 410 to 29 GA4 sessions year over year, the article should provide a clear conversion path for every qualified visitor, without requiring them to reach the conclusion.
The early CTA should lead to the landing page rather than introducing another competing conversion destination.
### P1. Reduce overlap with the fitness landing page
Now: The section titled:
What is the alternative to a hardware 3D body scanner for a fitness studio?
is highly relevant to buyer intent, but the live landing page already targets the same topic with the H1:
A mobile gym body scanner alternative for fitness apps.
It also includes a comparison between FitXpress, progress photos, standalone scanning apps, and in-gym hardware.
3DLOOK+1
How to change: Keep a shorter comparison section, but change its purpose from describing a product alternative to helping teams select a measurement workflow.
Suggested heading:
When should fitness programs use phone scanning instead of in-club hardware?
Keep the three contexts, but shorten the comparison. Conclude with a contextual link to the product LP for technical and commercial details.
Why: The article should capture readers researching how to implement body scanning in their fitness business. The landing page should remain the strongest destination for buyers comparing solutions.
The difference matters because creating two nearly identical answers on adjacent URLs makes it harder to establish which page should rank for which query.
### P1. Make the examples more concrete
Now: The member-journey section describes four useful placements: onboarding, scheduled check-ins, coach review, and premium tiers.
The logic is good, but much of the content explains possibilities rather than demonstrating what the experience looks like.
How to change: Add one short, clearly illustrative example after the scheduled check-ins paragraph.
Example: A six-week fitness program. A member completes a baseline scan at enrollment and another scan at the end of the program. The app displays the change in waist and hip measurements alongside the member's weight log. If a coach reviews the program, the two scan records provide additional context for the next training block. The app determines which results to show and how to use them.
Why: This makes the benefit immediately understandable to a product manager or fitness platform operator, without inventing customer results or implying a demonstrated retention improvement.
One practical example is sufficient. The article does not need multiple fictional scenarios.
### P2. Reduce repetition of retention statistics
Now: The article uses Adjust's retention benchmarks and the Kidman et al. review to introduce the retention problem.
Both sources are relevant. The Kidman review's median 70% abandonment figure is supported by the research, with the important qualification that it covers lifestyle behavior and mental health apps rather than body-scanning interventions.
JMIR
However, both statistics also appear on the fitness landing page, while the broader hub discusses the same retention problem.
3DLOOK+1
How to change: Retain the statistics, but shorten the supporting discussion and put more emphasis on the hypothesis being tested:
Does showing structured body progress increase the likelihood that a member returns for another check-in?
Why: A little repetition is useful for standalone readers, but this article's unique value should be how to evaluate the scan inside a fitness app, not another extended explanation of industry churn.
### P2. Clarify processing time
Now: The short answer says:
Structured results come back in under 45 seconds.
How to change:
The member takes two guided smartphone photos, front and side. Processing typically completes in under 45 seconds after capture.
Why: This is more precise and consistent with the live product landing page.
3DLOOK
It avoids suggesting that the complete member interaction, including following instructions and taking photos, always finishes in that time.
### P2. Improve the pilot evaluation section
Now: The proposed cohort test primarily focuses on the share of members returning for the next check-in.
That is a good primary outcome, but it does not fully address the possible negative effect of introducing another onboarding step.
How to change: Retain return-to-check-in rate as the main outcome, then briefly identify secondary metrics: scan completion rate, retake rate, onboarding completion, and premium conversion if the feature is offered in a paid tier.
Why: This gives buyers a more balanced evaluation framework, measuring both engagement and friction. It also aligns with the pilot metrics already described on the fitness landing page.
3DLOOK

## 5. What I would keep unchanged
The following sections are worth preserving as drafted:
- The short answer. Strong standalone definition that works for search and AI-driven discovery.
- The member journey. The article's most valuable differentiator.
- Accuracy and repeatability. Appropriate detail without overstating the evidence.
- SDK and API considerations. Useful for MOFU buyers evaluating implementation.
- Disability/accessibility FAQ. Important limitation presented transparently.
- Privacy FAQ. Accurately describes the distinction between scan IDs and member identity.
The body of the article should not be expanded significantly. Its problem is not missing breadth. It needs a slightly stronger commercial path and tighter differentiation from other pages.
