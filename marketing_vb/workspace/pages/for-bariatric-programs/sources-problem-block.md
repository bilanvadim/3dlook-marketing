# Problem-block sources: bariatric landing (verified at primary sources, 2026-10-16)

Read in full text unless marked **[abstract only]**. cdc.gov blocks curl and r.jina.ai; the PCD paper
was read on PMC and the key sentences confirmed on cdc.gov.

## The two statistics on Nika's draft

**44.6%: number right, reason overstated.** Krietenstein et al., "Characteristics of Patients Lost to
Follow-up after Bariatric Surgery", Nutrients (MDPI) 2024, PMC11357598. Prospective single-centre study,
University Hospital Würzburg, Germany; 591 operated 2008-2017 (sleeve, RYGB, sleeve→RYGB), 573 analysed;
lost to follow-up = no visit in 18 months. "Our follow-up rate of 90.3% after one year, 44.6% after five
and 15.0% after ten years". Reasons from a structured phone interview of 219 of 290 lost patients:
"Travel issues were mentioned in 29.7% (long distance) and 6.4% (costs) of patients." Abstract: "main
reason"; conclusion: "Though not frequently mentioned, travel issues were the most common reasons." 25%
thought follow-up unnecessary; weight regain named by 9 of 219.
→ Page: "44.6% of patients attended follow-up five years after surgery at one German centre; long
distance was the most cited reason for dropping out." Not "Distance to the clinic is the main reason
they stop."

**40%: number right, wrong job for this page.** Zhao et al., "State-Specific Prevalence of Severe Obesity
Among Adults in the US Using Bias Correction of Self-Reported BMI", Preventing Chronic Disease 2023
(PMC10364830). "Self-reported BRFSS data underestimated the overall prevalence of severe obesity by 40%
(5.3% vs 8.8%)." BMI ≥40, 2020 BRFSS, corrected against NHANES 2013-2018; relative difference in
population prevalence. Two problems: it is not about individual error, and FitXpress computes BMI from
entered height and weight, which is itself self-report (only Smart Scales adds an estimate). The one
bariatric-candidate study found (White et al., Obesity 2007, 179 women, [abstract only]): "obese
bariatric surgery candidates were accurate in self-report of weight." → Drop from the problem block.

## Post-op follow-up (the page's primary job since 2026-10-16)

1. **MBSAQIP Standards (ACS, 2026 edition), Standard 6.2:** "It is mandatory that all patients be
   followed through the 30-day, 6-month, and 1-year follow-up time frames. Centers must attempt to follow
   patients annually until they go two consecutive follow-up time frames without completing an
   assessment." Lost patients need "a minimum of two attempts to contact the patient for each follow-up
   time frame", documented in the MBSAQIP Registry. 6.3: risk-adjusted reports only for centres with a
   30-day follow-up rate ≥ 80%. **No 1-year follow-up-rate threshold exists; never claim one.** Strong.
2. **NICE NG246 (Jan 2025, updated Jan 2026; replaced CG189):** 1.18.17 "Offer people who have had
   bariatric surgery a follow-up care package for a minimum of 2 years within the bariatric service";
   1.18.18 annual nutritional monitoring in shared care; 1.18.1 patients "agree to the necessary
   long-term follow up after surgery (for example, lifelong annual reviews)". Strong (UK).
3. **SOReg, Sweden:** Dreber et al., SOARD 2018 [abstract only], RYGB: 37.0% of young and 46.1% of older
   eligible adults attended the 5-year follow-up. Strong; with Würzburg, two countries land near half.
4. **Lean mass:** Nuijten et al., Obesity Reviews 2022, 59 studies: −8.13 kg lean body mass at 12 months
   (~21-22% of weight lost); "About 55% of 12-month LBM loss occurred within 3-month postsurgery". DXA /
   BIA studies; no guideline mandates routine body-composition monitoring. Usable with caveat, never imply
   FitXpress estimates equal DXA.
5. **Follow-up and outcomes:** Reiber et al., Obesity Surgery 2022 meta-analysis: adherence linked to
   weight loss 0.5-3 years, not beyond. Do not write "follow-up improves long-term weight loss".
6. **Remote follow-up:** comparable, not better (de Oliveira 2025 meta-analysis [abstract only], OR 1.17,
   p = 0.44; BELLA pilot, Germany; Toronto matched study [abstract only]). Never "remote follow-up raises
   attendance".

## Pre-qualification (secondary job)

7. **Love et al., SOARD 2017 (University of Virginia, 2010-2015):** 827 of 1,475 evaluated patients had
   surgery (648, 44%, did not). Insurer-required diet: median 3 months (had surgery) vs 6 months (dropped
   out); PCP letter, cardiology / pulmonology / advanced labs predicted dropout. Strong.
8. **ASMBS/IFSO 2022 indications** (Eisenberg et al., Obesity Surgery 2023, PMC9834364): BMI > 35
   regardless of comorbidities; consider 30-34.9 with metabolic disease. Insurance-mandated preoperative
   weight loss is "discriminatory, arbitrary, and scientifically unfounded, contributing to patient
   attrition". Strong.
9. **Aetna CPB 0157 (reviewed 2026-07-21):** BMI "documented in contemporaneous clinical records…
   measured prior to preoperative preparatory program"; behavioural programme "may be in-person or
   remote". A phone-entered BMI does not meet "measured"; FitXpress is triage, not the documentation.
10. Sala et al., Obesity Surgery 2017 (Mount Sinai St. Luke's): 397 of 779 who began evaluation had
    surgery; 26.2% of them attended the 2-year follow-up. Old, single centre. Usable with caveat.

## Not supported (do not write)

"Patients drop out because they can't see progress" · "follow-up improves long-term weight loss" ·
"remote follow-up raises attendance" · "MBSAQIP requires X% at 1 year" · "self-reported BMI is
unreliable in bariatric candidates" · the "90% → 30% at 2 years → <10% at 10 years" line (secondary
citation in the Nutrients introduction, source not opened).
