How it works

How 3D body scanning works on a smartphone

Mobile 3D body scanning estimates body shape and body measurements from photos taken on an ordinary smartphone camera, with no booth-style 3D body scanner or wearable. Each 3DLOOK 3D body scan needs two photos: one from the front and one from the side.

Read how the body scanning technology works
  1. Guided captureThe app guides the person through two photos and checks position and framing before each one.
  2. Body modelA patented statistical body model, trained on 9+ years of data, reconstructs the body from the two photos.
  3. Structured resultsMeasurements and the other outputs reach the business's own system through an API, with web and mobile SDKs, including supported iOS and Android integrations.

Accuracy and privacy

Accuracy and data handling, documented

Accuracy

96-97%
measurement accuracy across body metrics, internal validation

Internal validation across multiple real-world scan events with five repeated scans per person against expert pattern-maker manual measurements shows 3DLOOK's measurement accuracy of approximately 96-97% across body metrics, with a typical absolute error of 1.5-2.0 cm per measurement, varying by body part.

For most evaluated measurements, repeated scans showed typical scan-to-scan differences of less than 1 cm.

Privacy and security

FitXpress can support HIPAA-governed deployments where 3DLOOK acts as a business associate under an executed business associate agreement (BAA), where applicable. In most enterprise deployments, the customer acts as the data controller and 3DLOOK acts as the data processor under GDPR.

FitXpress scan records are associated with anonymized, randomly generated IDs, and 3DLOOK cannot identify a specific individual from the stored data. 3DLOOK does not use production customer data to train its models.

Frequently asked questions

What does 3DLOOK do?

3DLOOK builds mobile body-scanning technology that turns two smartphone photos into body measurements, body composition estimates and 3D body models for businesses, delivered through an API and SDKs. FitXpress serves health and fitness programs, Mobile Tailor serves apparel and uniform companies, and Wrist Measurement serves jewelry, watch and wearable brands.

Which 3DLOOK product fits my business?

Health programs, insurers, online pharmacies and fitness apps use FitXpress for body measurements, BMI and body composition estimates. Made-to-measure brands, manufacturers and uniform programs use Mobile Tailor for garment measurements. Jewelry, watch and wearable brands use Wrist Measurement for wrist sizing. The use-case pages above show each workflow in detail.

Can the scan run inside our own app?

Yes. The web and mobile SDKs place the guided capture inside the business's own app or web flow, and the results return through the API. Onboarding, consent wording, loading screens and the results screen follow the business's own design. The photo capture layer stays fixed, because its pose and phone-angle checks protect measurement accuracy.

Is FitXpress a medical device?

FitXpress is not a medical device. An independent regulatory assessment concluded that FitXpress does not meet the definition of a medical device under the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002 (UK MDR) or the EU Medical Devices Regulation (EU MDR). FitXpress is not cleared, authorized, or approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA).

How do I get access and pricing?

FitXpress and Mobile Tailor plans are listed on the pricing page. Access to the API and SDKs starts with a demo call, where the team confirms the workflow and the integration route.
