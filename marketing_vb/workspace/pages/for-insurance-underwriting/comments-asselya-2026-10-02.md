# Asselya's edits to v2, with her reasons (verbatim)

Her summary (translated from Russian): the current version is clearly stronger and reads more easily.
What still bothered her: repetition, and very frequent direct address ("you / your / yours"), which
feels like too much for an enterprise reader. She ran a deduplication pass and applied the language
guardrails file.

What the builder took from this is in `review-2026-10-02-final-vs-v2.md` §3.

1. **Evidence-gap introduction.** Before: "The accelerated path removed the one step where someone measured the applicant. The build field kept its weight in the risk class." After: "The evidence gap becomes material when build affects risk classification but the application relies on self-reported height and weight." Reason: the original repeated the explanation already provided in the hero and used the figurative phrase "kept its weight." The new version defines the relationship directly: build affects classification, while the application depends on self-reported data.
2. **Sources for the three underwriting statistics.** Before: the source names appeared as unlinked text (Gen Re, 2025 Next-Gen Underwriting Survey · Munich Re · LIMRA). After: the source labels link directly to the supporting primary sources (Gen Re, 2025 Next Gen Underwriting Survey · Munich Re, misclassification analysis · LIMRA, automated and accelerated underwriting study). Reason: the content guardrails require source links to be integrated directly into meaningful text anchors. The links also allow the 59%, Build/BMI, and 9-versus-27-day claims to be verified from their original sources.
3. **Workflow introduction and first use of SDK.** Before: "No new app for the applicant and no new queue for your team. FitXpress runs inside your e-application through a web or mobile SDK." After: "FitXpress is embedded in the carrier's e-application through a web or mobile software development kit (SDK). Results can return to the existing review workflow." Reason: the original relied on corrective negation and did not expand SDK on first use. The new version explains the integration and result-delivery relationship directly.
4. **Threshold and disclosure-flag relationship.** Before: "A gap above your threshold comes back flagged." After: "The carrier-defined threshold determines whether the response includes a disclosure flag." Reason: the original used compressed wording. The new sentence explicitly states that the carrier-defined threshold determines whether the disclosure flag is returned.
5. **Case-routing step heading.** Before: "Your rules route the case". After: "The carrier's rules determine case routing". Reason: the new heading identifies the responsible actor and states the relationship between the carrier's rules and the routing outcome more precisely.
6. **Case-record delivery and first use of API.** Before: "One timestamped, machine-readable record per applicant. It returns over the API to your underwriting workbench, case-management system or policy administration system." After: "FitXpress returns one timestamped, machine-readable record per applicant through its application programming interface (API). The record can be delivered to the carrier's underwriting workbench, case-management system or policy administration system." Reason: the original used "it" without a clear actor and did not expand API on first use. The new version identifies FitXpress as the source of the record and clarifies where the record can be delivered.
7. **Case-record table heading.** Before: "What your underwriter gets". After: "Returned output". Reason: the new heading is shorter and describes the table contents without unnecessary direct address.
8. **Availability of additional outputs.** Before: "80+ body measurements, body composition estimates and a 3D model are available if you need them." After: "… are available when enabled for the deployment." Reason: availability depends on the deployment configuration. The new version states that dependency directly instead of presenting the outputs as generally available on request.
9. **Comparison-section H2.** Before: "FitXpress vs self-report vs the paramedical exam". After: "FitXpress compared with self-report and a paramedical examination". Reason: the new heading removes compressed "vs" phrasing and gives the comparison a more formal enterprise tone.
10. **Comparison-section conclusion.** Before: "Keep the paramedical exam where your guideline calls for one. FitXpress covers the build field on the cases your guidelines already route away from it." After: "A paramedical examination remains part of cases for which the carrier's guidelines require one. FitXpress adds structured build evidence to cases that remain on the accelerated path." Reason: the original used an instructive command and defined FitXpress through contrast with the examination. The revised version states the role of each method directly and preserves the product boundary.
11. **Accuracy and data-handling H2.** Before: "Accuracy and data handling on one screen". After: "How accurate is FitXpress, and how is underwriting data handled?" Reason: the new version is a question-type H2 aligned with search and GEO intent. It also identifies the two questions answered by the section more clearly.
12. **First use of NDA.** Before: "The full methodology is available under NDA." After: "… under a non-disclosure agreement (NDA)." Reason: NDA is expanded on first use in accordance with the abbreviation guardrail.
13. **Scan-record description.** Before: "Tied to anonymized, randomly generated IDs." After: "Scan records use anonymized, randomly generated identifiers." Reason: the original was a sentence fragment and did not identify what was tied to the identifiers. The new version names the subject directly and replaces the unexplained abbreviation "IDs."
14. **First use of BAA.** Before: "… under an executed BAA." After: "… under an executed Business Associate Agreement (BAA)." Reason: BAA is expanded on first use. HIPAA remains abbreviated because it is included among the approved commonly known abbreviations.
15. **Link to the Data, Privacy, Security & Regulatory FAQ.** Before: "Storage, retention, SOC 2 and FDA: Data, Privacy, Security & Regulatory FAQ →". After: "See the Data, Privacy, Security & Regulatory FAQ for storage, retention, System and Organization Controls 2 (SOC 2), and U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) information." Reason: the original was a compressed label rather than a complete relationship. The new version integrates the link into a sentence, explains what the linked FAQ covers, and expands SOC 2 and FDA on first use.
16. **Pilot-section H2.** Before: "Prove it before it touches a live decision". After: "Evaluate FitXpress before using its outputs in underwriting decisions". Reason: "Prove it" and "touches a live decision" were more promotional and conversational. The revised heading describes the evaluation boundary in formal underwriting language.
17. **Timing of pilot success criteria.** Before: "… on success criteria agreed before day one." After: "… on success criteria agreed before the evaluation begins." Reason: "Before day one" is conversational and can be ambiguous. The new version identifies the relevant event precisely.
18. **Pilot-results introduction.** Before: "You finish with numbers:". After: "The evaluation should quantify:". Reason: the original focused on the audience and used conversational language. The new version focuses on the evaluation and introduces the listed metrics directly.
19. **Duplicate FAQ about shadow evaluation.** Removed: "What does a shadow evaluation include?" Reason: the pilot section already explains the cohort, separation from underwriting decisions, evidence comparison, success criteria, candidate thresholds, and review-volume effects. Repeating the same explanation in the FAQ added length without adding a new decision-stage answer.
20. **Duplicate FAQ about internal-only results.** Removed: "Can results stay internal to the carrier?" Reason: the recommended setup in the workflow section already explains that the applicant sees confirmation of submission while the structured results are sent to the carrier's systems. The FAQ repeated the same deployment pattern.
21. **Failed-scan answer.** Before: "Applicable capture-failure reasons return with the record." After: "FitXpress returns applicable capture-failure reasons with the record." Reason: the original attributed the action to the failure reasons themselves. The new version identifies FitXpress as the actor.
