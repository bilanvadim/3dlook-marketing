The problem

Why does declared weight put online pharmacies at risk?

Since February 2025, General Pharmaceutical Council (GPhC) guidance has said that, when supplying weight management medicines such as glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) treatments, the prescriber verifies "the person's weight, height and/or body mass index" using "a different way to verify the information provided to them by the person". An order supplied on declared weight alone is therefore a prescribing decision the pharmacy may have to defend at inspection or in answer to a concern.

1,307

concerns about weight management medicines and services reached the GPhC between January 2024 and December 2025

11%

of concerns were about inadequate BMI verification, the third most reported area; public examples included photographs that could be easily manipulated

Source for both: General Pharmaceutical Council, "Weight management medicines and services: a review of GPhC inspections and concerns"

FitXpress adds one input to that check: a weight estimate from photos taken live in the order flow, calculated with the height the patient enters. Whether that estimate, alone or with other checks, meets the pharmacy's process is the pharmacy's decision.

The scan record

Each scan adds a second weight source to the order record

FitXpress returns one timestamped record per scan; the pharmacy chooses which fields the prescriber sees.

  • Weight estimate

    FitXpress Smart Scales estimates weight from the photos and the entered height. The prescriber reads it beside the declared weight.

  • Mismatch flag

    The clinical team sets a threshold. A difference beyond it flags the record, and prescribers start there; the cause is theirs to judge.

  • Calculated BMI

    FitXpress returns BMI already calculated from the estimate and the entered height.

  • Status and timestamps

    Every record carries processing status and timestamps, which show when the check ran if an inspector asks.

  • Admin Panel

    Failed-scan messages and photo-deletion dates sit in the optional FitXpress Admin Panel, ready for compliance staff to look up.

FitXpress is not a medical device. It adds evidence to the order; the prescriber decides whether to supply.

How it works

The check runs inside the order flow the pharmacy already has

Pharmacies embed FitXpress through web and mobile software development kits (SDKs), including supported iOS and Android integrations.

  1. The patient scans where the pharmacy places the check.

    That can be checkout, a re-order, or during or straight after an online consultation, with consent and instruction screens in the pharmacy's own wording.

  2. Real-Time Pose Validation (RTPV) guides the capture.

    Patients follow on-screen pose and framing guidance while they take both photos live.

  3. The result reaches the prescriber with the order.

    The pharmacy's system pulls the record through the FitXpress application programming interface (API) into its patient medication record (PMR), prescriber dashboard or order management, linked by scan identifier to the patient and the order.

Recommended setup: the SDK together with the API, because pose and framing validation runs in the SDK's capture layer.

Proof and data handling

How accurate is the weight estimate, and how is patient data handled?

Smart Scales estimates weight with approximately 3.5% mean absolute error under evaluated conditions. The clinical team sets the mismatch threshold with that error in mind. FitXpress is not equivalent to a calibrated scale when the prescribing protocol requires one.

3.5%approximate mean absolute error of the Smart Scales weight estimate, under evaluated conditions

The figure comes from an internal study. Peer review and third-party clinical certification are not part of that record, and the full methodology is available under a non-disclosure agreement (NDA). FitXpress was not specifically trained on data representing people with physical disabilities, and its measurement performance has not been established for this population.   • Photos. FitXpress deletes photos immediately after processing or within 30 days under a customer-specific policy. Retained photos are blurred, and face obfuscation is applied during capture.
  • Identifiers. Scan records use anonymized, randomly generated identifiers.
  • Data protection. Where the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) applies, 3DLOOK provides a data processing agreement (DPA) that includes Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) and the UK Addendum. In most enterprise deployments, the customer acts as the data controller and 3DLOOK acts as the data processor under GDPR.
  • Encryption. Data is encrypted in transit and at rest.
  • Model training. 3DLOOK does not use production customer data to train its models without the customer's explicit, documented authorization.

FAQ

Questions online pharmacy teams ask

Does FitXpress verify the patient's height?

No. The patient enters height, which FitXpress uses with the two photos to estimate weight and calculate BMI. Where the prescribing protocol needs more than the patient's own figure, that check sits outside FitXpress.

Can the scan run during an online consultation?

Yes, as one placement the pharmacy can choose. The patient takes the two photos on their own phone during or straight after the consultation, and the prescriber can see the result in the same session. The prescriber still decides, and the pharmacy judges whether the combined check meets its process.

How does FitXpress connect to the pharmacy's checkout?

The pharmacy's developers add the SDK capture step to the checkout and call the FitXpress API from their own back end. Integration details are covered in the FitXpress API documentation.

Where is patient data hosted, and how long are results kept?

FitXpress data is hosted on Amazon Web Services (AWS), primarily in the US-West-2 region and partially in US-East-1. Scan outputs are retained on an ongoing basis unless the agreement says otherwise, and the compliance team can request deletion by scan identifier.
