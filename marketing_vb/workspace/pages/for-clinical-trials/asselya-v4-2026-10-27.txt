The problem

## Collect body measurements between scheduled study visits

Obesity and metabolic trials rely on repeated measurements to understand changes over time. Scheduled site visits and imaging assessments may leave gaps between measurement points. FitXpress enables additional remote anthropometric data collection through guided smartphone capture. Study teams determine how these results fit into their research workflows.

Between visits
Participants can complete protocol-scheduled measurements at home using a compatible smartphone, adding observations between study visits.
Inside existing apps
FitXpress supplies guided capture and structured outputs for integration into the study’s participant app or trial platform.

White-label integration

## Add body measurement capture to a DCT platform or participant app

For decentralized clinical trial (DCT) platforms, FitXpress provides a measurement component that teams can embed through web and mobile software development kits (SDKs). The existing platform manages branding, consent language, scheduling, and the participant experience.

FitXpress returns results through its application programming interface (API). The platform team determines where results appear and how fields are mapped into an electronic data capture (EDC) system. FitXpress does not provide a prebuilt EDC integration.

Supported integration options include native iOS and Android SDKs, React Native, and web capture. Device compatibility, connectivity requirements, and study-specific behavior are reviewed during implementation.

Between visits

## Add body measurements between site and imaging visits

Participants can complete additional, protocol-scheduled scans at home on their own compatible phone or a study-provided device. Each scan supplies consistent output fields for longitudinal review alongside site-collected measurements.

The sponsor decides whether and how these additional measurements are used in participant follow-up, exploratory analyses, or a substudy. Suitability for any research endpoint requires study-specific evaluation.

Results visibility, set by the protocol. Outputs return to the study’s backend through the API. The study team decides which results, if any, participants and site staff see.

Outputs and capabilities

## Body measurements and structured outputs for study workflows

FitXpress provides body measurements and related body metrics through the API. The study’s technology team maps the relevant results into its research systems; the fields available depend on the specific implementation.

Body measurements

### 80+ body measurements
Waist, hip, chest, thigh and other dimensions in centimeters, produced by the same processing at every scan.
Body composition

### Body composition estimates
Body-fat percentage, fat mass and lean mass estimates. BMI and basal metabolic rate (BMR) are calculated metrics.
3D model

### 3D body model and 3D Body Progress
A 3D body model reconstructed from each scan, with comparison capabilities subject to the selected configuration.
Capture integrity

### Quality checks and flags
Real-Time Pose Validation (RTPV) and clothing guidance support capture quality. The study team sets retake rules according to the indicators available in its configuration.
Traceability

### Timestamp and scan identifier
Available scan identifiers and timestamps support record mapping. The available fields depend on the configuration.
Data delivery

### Formats for the study database
Structured results through the API and available administrative export options. Mapping and validation within the study database are managed by the sponsor or platform vendor.

FitXpress provides supplementary study data. The sponsor and investigator determine endpoint selection, validation requirements, and protocol compliance. Regulatory classification must be evaluated for the proposed intended use.

## Where FitXpress fits alongside existing measurement methods

FitXpress can provide additional smartphone-derived anthropometric measurements between visits. Tape measurements, calibrated scales, bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA), dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA), and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) provide different information. The protocol determines which methods are required.

Validation and data handling

## Measurement performance and data handling

FitXpress has undergone internal accuracy and repeatability testing. Sponsors should evaluate the available evidence against their study population, required precision, reference methods, and intended use.

< 1 cmTypical scan-to-scan difference for most evaluated body measurements.

96 to 97%Body-measurement accuracy against expert manual measurement, with typical absolute error of 1.5 to 2.0 cm.

Internal testing reports typical scan-to-scan differences below 1 cm for most evaluated body measurements, and approximately 96–97% measurement accuracy against the stated manual reference, with typical absolute errors of 1.5–2.0 cm. These findings are not clinical-trial endpoint validation. Performance depends on the tested population and capture conditions; supporting methodology is available for evaluation.

How can clinical trials collect body measurements between site visits?
Clinical trials can incorporate remote body measurement tools into protocol-defined workflows. FitXpress uses guided smartphone capture to generate body measurements between scheduled visits where the study permits.Can FitXpress integrate with decentralized clinical trial platforms?
