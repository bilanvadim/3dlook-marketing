The problem

What do remote check-ins miss in obesity and metabolic trials?

Remote check-ins already return body weight. Waist and hip still need a site visit and a trained measurer, so between visits the study has no body measurements.

Secondary endpoint

The European Medicines Agency expects waist circumference as a secondary endpoint in weight-management trials.

Guideline on clinical evaluation of medicinal products used in weight management.
1 to 6 cm

Self-measured waist misses a trained measurer’s reading by 1 to 6 cm on average, in either direction.

Waist circumference as a vital sign in clinical practice: a Consensus Statement from the IAS and ICCR Working Group on Visceral Obesity; Validity of self-measured waist circumference in adults at risk of type 2 diabetes and cardiovascular disease; Accuracy of self-reported body weight, height and waist circumference in a Dutch overweight working population.

White-label integration

Run body scans inside the participant app the study already uses

Participants scan in the same app they use for diaries and questionnaires, under the platform’s branding and consent flow. There is no separate app to install.

Results return to the platform’s backend through the application programming interface (API). The platform team maps them to the electronic data capture (EDC) system. FitXpress does not provide a prebuilt EDC integration.

Outputs and capabilities

One record format at baseline and at every later scan

FitXpress returns each scan as a timestamped record through the API, with the same fields at every scan. The study’s data management team or platform vendor maps these fields to the study database.

Body measurements

80+ body measurements

Waist, hip, chest, thigh and other dimensions in centimeters, produced by the same processing at every scan.

The study gets waist and hip data at home check-ins, not only at site visits.

Body composition

Body composition estimates

Body-fat percentage, fat mass and lean mass estimates. BMI and basal metabolic rate (BMR) are calculated metrics.

The study sees composition estimates between imaging visits. Imaging stays the reference.

Traceability

Timestamp and scan identifier

Each record shows when the values were produced. Scan records use randomly generated identifiers, which the study maps to its own participant numbers.

No personal identifiers are processed on the 3DLOOK side.

Data delivery

Formats for the study database

JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) through the API, comma-separated values (CSV) export from the FitXpress Admin Panel and 3D model export.

The field mapping is built once and covers every later scan.

FitXpress is not a medical device. It captures and documents body measurements as additional study data; endpoint selection, endpoint validation and protocol compliance remain with the sponsor and the investigator.

How it works

Embed body scans in the trial technology the study already runs

The platform team embeds FitXpress through the SDKs and retrieves results through the API. The FitXpress Admin Panel gives a central view of scan results with CSV export, for teams that do not build their own dashboard.

  1. Embed guided capture

    Add the scan as a task in the participant app, with the platform’s branding, consent language and schedule. The coordinator runs the baseline scan at the site.

  2. Participants scan on schedule

    Participants complete front and side capture at home on the schedule the protocol sets. Real-Time Pose Validation guides pose and framing during each scan.

  3. Results to the platform backend

    Results return through the API. The data management team maps each field and timestamp to the EDC system.

Validation and data handling

How repeatable is FitXpress, and how is study data handled?

For a measurement repeated between visits, repeatability is the deciding property. It determines whether a baseline scan and a week 12 scan can be compared. FitXpress’s internal validation evaluates repeated scans and comparisons with expert manual measurements.

< 1 cmTypical scan-to-scan difference for most evaluated body measurements.
96 to 97%Body-measurement accuracy against expert manual measurement, with typical absolute error of 1.5 to 2.0 cm.

These figures come from internal validation and have not been peer-reviewed. The validation population covered ages 16 to 78, weights of 38 to 210 kg and heights of 150 to 220 cm, from the US and Europe; performance outside this range has not been characterized. Accuracy is not published by body shape or skin tone. Body composition values are estimates. Supporting methodology is available under a non-disclosure agreement (NDA).

Frequently asked questions

Questions from clinical operations, data management and platform teams

Is FitXpress a medical device?

No. An independent regulatory assessment concluded that FitXpress does not meet the definition of a medical device under UK MDR or EU MDR for its current intended purpose. FitXpress is not cleared, authorized or approved by the FDA. The sponsor assesses the regulatory status of the complete study workflow, including how the outputs are used.

How can obesity trials collect body measurements between site visits?

Participants complete a guided two-photo scan at home in the study’s participant app, and FitXpress returns waist, hip and 80+ body measurements to the study’s backend through the API. The sponsor decides how the data is used, for example as an exploratory endpoint, in a substudy or for participant follow-up between visits. The FDA guidance on digital health technologies for remote data acquisition (December 2023) recommends that sponsors use fit-for-purpose technologies and address verification and validation, whether or not the technology is a device. Protocol-defined methods, such as a calibrated scale, tape measurement, DXA or MRI, keep their role.

Can FitXpress run white-label inside a DCT platform?

Yes. The platform team embeds guided capture through web and mobile SDKs, including supported iOS and Android integrations, and configures the interface, flow, branding, consent text and privacy links. Participants stay inside the platform’s app throughout the scan.

Can scan results stay hidden from participants and site staff?

Yes. Capture runs in the participant app, and results go to the study’s backend through the API. The study team decides which results, if any, participants and site staff see.

Does FitXpress measure muscle?

FitXpress reports fat mass and lean mass estimates. Lean mass includes organs, bone and water along with muscle; it is not a direct measurement of skeletal muscle. Trials that assess muscle preservation use DXA, MRI or another method the protocol defines.
