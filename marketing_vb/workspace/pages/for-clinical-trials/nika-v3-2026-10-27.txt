The problem

## In multi-site and hybrid obesity trials, the harder problem is the workflow around each body measurement

Who performs the measurement, how the participant is prepared, how the value is recorded and how consistently the process repeats all vary by site, staff member and visit. Visits that exist only to capture body measurements add burden over a study window of 12 months or longer.

“Not standardized”
The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) does not consider waist circumference a surrogate endpoint, in part because “the procedure is not standardized.” It may be evaluated as a secondary endpoint, and acceptability for labeling depends on data quality. With FitXpress, every site and check-in follows the same guided capture sequence and produces the same record.FDA draft guidance on obesity drug development, January 2025.

30 to 45%
Attrition reported across obesity pharmacotherapy trials in a network meta-analysis. Long studies with repeated visits add participant burden. With FitXpress, measurement-only time points can move to remote check-ins where the protocol allows.JAMA, 2016. Network meta-analysis of weight-loss drug trials.

For sponsors, contract research organizations and research networks

## Standardize the measurement step and reduce visit burden across the study

One guided scan runs at the site and at home, inside the protocol’s existing workflow.

- 

### One capture workflow at every site
Every coordinator follows the same guided sequence, and Real-Time Pose Validation (RTPV) checks pose and framing during each scan. Cross-site review compares records produced the same way.

- 

### Less measuring and documentation per visit
A guided scan replaces separate measuring and recording steps where the protocol permits, and returns a structured record directly.

- 

### Fewer measurement-only visits
Where the protocol allows, participants complete check-ins at home. Fewer trips for a single data point can lower the friction that contributes to dropout.

- 

### Audit-ready records
Each scan carries a timestamp, a scan identifier and capture-quality checks, supporting monitoring, quality assurance and audit review.

Site visits

## Run the same capture sequence at every site and every visit

Coordinators follow the same guided front and side capture at every site, and each scan returns the same structured record. The baseline scan and the month 12 follow-up share one data structure, which supports longitudinal comparison and cross-site review.

Scans arrive as complete or flagged records. Pose validation and the Clothing Detector record capture-quality checks with each scan, and flagged scans go to a retake or to the measurement the protocol defines.

Protocol rules, set by the study team. The study team sets the scan schedule, the retake rule and the in-clinic fallback for every site.

Remote check-ins and pre-checks

## Move measurement-only time points to remote check-ins where the protocol allows

Participants complete the same guided scan at home on their own phone or a study-provided device. Remote scans return the same fields as site scans, keeping the longitudinal record continuous between visits and through the post-treatment follow-up window.

Where the protocol allows a remote pre-check, the same scan surfaces BMI-based screening data before the first site visit. Eligibility remains the investigator team’s determination under the approved protocol.

Results visibility, set by the protocol. Outputs return to the study’s backend through the FitXpress application programming interface (API). The study team decides which results, if any, participants and site staff see.

Platform integration

Decentralized clinical trial (DCT) platforms and participant-app vendors embed FitXpress through web and mobile software development kits (SDKs), under their own branding, consent language and flow. Outputs and capabilities

## One record format for baseline, follow-up and remote check-ins

FitXpress returns each scan as a timestamped, machine-readable record through the API, with the same fields at every site and time point. The study’s data management team or platform vendor maps these fields to the study database.

 Body measurements

### 80+ body measurements
Waist, hip, chest, thigh and other dimensions in centimeters, produced by the same processing at every scan.
 Body composition

### Body composition estimates
Body-fat percentage, fat mass and lean mass estimates. BMI and basal metabolic rate (BMR) are calculated metrics.
 3D model

### 3D body model and 3D Body Progress
A 3D model reconstructed from each scan. On Pro, compare two scans in a 3D view.
 Capture integrity

### Quality checks and flags
Pose validation results and Clothing Detector flags are stored with each scan. The protocol defines when a participant retakes the scan.
 Traceability

### Timestamp and scan identifier
Each record shows when the values were produced and how they were captured. Scan records use randomly generated identifiers, which the study maps to its own participant numbers.
 Data delivery

### Formats for the study database
JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) through the API, comma-separated values (CSV) export from the FitXpress Admin Panel and 3D model export.

FitXpress is not a medical device. It standardizes and documents anthropometric capture; protocol design, endpoint validation and Good Clinical Practice (GCP) compliance remain with the sponsor, the contract research organization and the investigator team.

Validation and data handling

## Understand measurement performance and data handling before designing the study

For a measurement repeated across visits, repeatability determines whether a baseline scan and a month 12 scan can be compared. FitXpress’s internal validation evaluates repeated scans and comparisons with expert manual measurements.

< 1 cmTypical scan-to-scan difference for most evaluated body measurements.

96 to 97%Body-measurement accuracy against expert manual measurement, with typical absolute error of 1.5 to 2.0 cm.

These figures come from internal validation and have not been peer-reviewed. The validation population covered ages 16 to 78, weights of 38 to 210 kg and heights of 150 to 220 cm, from the US and Europe; performance outside this range has not been characterized. Accuracy is not published by body shape or skin tone. Body composition values are estimates. Supporting methodology is available under a non-disclosure agreement (NDA).

Body scanning accuracy, repeatability and validation methodology

- Photo retentionFitXpress deletes photos immediately after processing or retains them for up to 30 days under a contract-specific policy. Retained photos are blurred, and face obfuscation is applied during capture.

- Participant identifiersScan records use randomly generated identifiers, and 3DLOOK cannot identify an individual from stored records. The study maps them to its own participant numbers.

- HostingFitXpress data is hosted on Amazon Web Services (AWS), primarily in the US-West-2 region and partly in the US-East-1 region.

- General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)In most enterprise deployments, the contracting organization acts as data controller and 3DLOOK acts as data processor. A data processing agreement (DPA) with Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) covers international transfers, with a UK Addendum where UK GDPR applies.

- Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)Where HIPAA applies, FitXpress can support the deployment with 3DLOOK acting as a business associate under an executed Business Associate Agreement (BAA).

- Regulatory statusAn independent regulatory assessment concluded that FitXpress does not meet the medical-device definition under the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002 (UK MDR) or the EU Medical Devices Regulation (EU MDR) for its current intended purpose. FitXpress is not cleared, authorized or approved by the FDA.

- Vendor qualification documentsA security overview, penetration-test summary, architecture and data-flow diagrams and an incident-response overview are available under NDA. 3DLOOK is working toward a System and Organization Controls (SOC) 2 attestation report.

- Encryption and model trainingData is encrypted in transit and at rest. 3DLOOK does not use production customer data to train its models without the customer’s explicit, documented authorization.

Frequently asked questions

## Questions from clinical operations, data management and platform teams
Is FitXpress a medical device?
No. An independent regulatory assessment concluded that FitXpress does not meet the medical-device definition under UK MDR or EU MDR for its current intended purpose. FitXpress is not cleared, authorized or approved by the FDA. The sponsor assesses the regulatory status of the complete study workflow, including how the outputs are used.
What causes measurement variability in multi-site obesity trials?
Manual anthropometric measurement can vary across sites with staff technique, tools, training, participant preparation and documentation practice. Even with a clear protocol, these local workflow decisions accumulate across sites and over the study period. FitXpress runs the same guided capture sequence at every site and visit.
How can a hybrid or decentralized trial collect body measurements remotely?
Through a guided digital workflow that takes participants or coordinators through the same capture steps, generates timestamped records and produces outputs for monitoring and review. The protocol determines which measurements can be captured remotely and at which time points.
How do FitXpress records relate to FDA guidance on digital health technologies and Good Clinical Practice?
The FDA guidance on digital health technologies (DHTs) for remote data acquisition (December 2023) sets sponsor responsibilities for selecting a DHT and for verification and validation that are fit for the intended use. International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) E6(R3) Good Clinical Practice addresses essential records, source data, audit trails, metadata and traceability. Timestamped FitXpress records support those documentation expectations. Compliance results from the process design of the sponsor and the contract research organization.
Does FitXpress determine participant eligibility?
No. FitXpress can support a remote pre-check where the protocol allows it, surfacing BMI-based screening data before the site visit. Eligibility is the investigator team's determination under criteria the sponsor documents and the Institutional Review Board (IRB) approves.
Can FitXpress run inside a DCT platform or participant app?
Yes. The platform team embeds guided capture through native SDKs for iOS and Android, a React Native kit or web components, and configures the interface, flow, branding, consent text and privacy links. Participants stay inside the platform's app throughout the scan.
Can scan results stay hidden from participants and site staff?
Yes. Capture runs in the participant app, and results go to the study's backend through the API. The study team decides which outputs, if any, participants and site staff see, which supports blinded study designs.
Can participants use their own phones?
Yes. Capture runs on iOS 15 or later and on Android phones, including older models, which may show a lower frame rate during capture. Sponsors can also provide study devices. The participant stands for the scan, and the current dataset does not support wheelchair users or amputees. Two gender options are available. Height, weight and gender are required inputs. A height difference above 2 cm affects results, and studies can enter the height measured at the site.
How does scan data reach the EDC system?
Results return through the API as JSON, delivered through webhook events with polling as the documented fallback. Webhooks currently have no automatic retries or signature verification, and polling is the recommended backup. The study's technology team or platform vendor builds the mapping to the EDC system or eCOA platform, and integration paths are confirmed during procurement. The FitXpress Admin Panel provides CSV export of all scans under the study's API key.
Where is data hosted, and how are participants in the EU and UK covered?
FitXpress data is hosted on AWS, primarily in the US-West-2 region and partly in the US-East-1 region. For participants in the EU and UK, 3DLOOK provides a DPA with SCCs for international transfers and a UK Addendum where UK GDPR applies. In most deployments, the contracting organization is the data controller and 3DLOOK is the data processor.
