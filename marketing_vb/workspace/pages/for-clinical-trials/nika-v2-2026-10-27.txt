The problem

## Obesity trials now ask what kind of weight participants lose, and body data beyond the scale depends on site and imaging visits

FitXpress adds body measurements between those visits from a phone, inside the app participants already use for the study.

92.8%
In the phase 2b BELIEVE trial, 92.8% of the weight lost with bimagrumab and semaglutide was fat, compared with 71.8% with semaglutide alone. Trials now report the composition of weight loss alongside its amount. FitXpress adds waist, hip and body composition estimates between imaging visits.Medscape, June 2025. Results presented at the American Diabetes Association Scientific Sessions.

“Expensive”
Draft guidance from the US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) describes dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) as expensive, requiring multiple blinded central readers, and asks for body composition assessment in a representative subset of participants. FitXpress adds body measurements across the study, from home, between those assessments.FDA draft guidance on obesity drug development, January 2025.

White-label integration

## Run body scans inside the participant app the study already uses

The platform team embeds FitXpress through web and mobile software development kits (SDKs), with the platform’s own branding, consent language and flow. Participants scan in the same app they use for diaries and questionnaires, with no separate app to install.

Results return to the platform’s backend through the FitXpress application programming interface (API). The platform decides where the data appears, which results participants see and how records reach the electronic data capture (EDC) system.

Kits for each stack. Native SDKs for iOS and Android, a React Native kit and web components. Native kits capture on the device and work offline.

Between visits

## Add body measurements between site and imaging visits

Site visits and imaging visits set the reference points. Between them, participants complete scheduled scans at home on their own phone or a study-provided device. Each home scan returns the same fields as the baseline scan taken at the site.

The study team decides how the additional data is used: an exploratory endpoint, a substudy or participant follow-up between visits.

Results visibility, set by the protocol. Outputs return to the study’s backend through the API. The study team decides which results, if any, participants and site staff see.

Outputs and capabilities

## One record format at baseline and at every later scan

FitXpress returns each scan as a timestamped record through the API, with the same fields at every scan. The study’s data management team or platform vendor maps these fields to the study database.

 Body measurements

### 80+ body measurements
Waist, hip, chest, thigh and other dimensions in centimeters, produced by the same processing at every scan.
 Body composition

### Body composition estimates
Body-fat percentage, fat mass and lean mass estimates. BMI and basal metabolic rate (BMR) are calculated metrics.
 3D model

### 3D body model and 3D Body Progress
A 3D model reconstructed from each scan. On Pro, compare two scans in a 3D view.
 Capture integrity

### Quality checks and flags
Real-Time Pose Validation (RTPV) results and Clothing Detector flags are stored with each scan. The protocol defines when a participant retakes the scan.
 Traceability

### Timestamp and scan identifier
Each record shows when the values were produced and how they were captured. Scan records use randomly generated identifiers, which the study maps to its own participant numbers.
 Data delivery

### Formats for the study database
JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) through the API, comma-separated values (CSV) export from the FitXpress Admin Panel and 3D model export.

FitXpress is not a medical device. It captures and documents body measurements as additional study data; endpoint selection, endpoint validation and protocol compliance remain with the sponsor and the investigator.

Validation and data handling

## Understand measurement performance and data handling before designing the study

For a measurement repeated between visits, repeatability is the deciding property. It determines whether a baseline scan and a week 12 scan can be compared. FitXpress’s internal validation evaluates repeated scans and comparisons with expert manual measurements.

< 1 cmTypical scan-to-scan difference for most evaluated body measurements.

96 to 97%Body-measurement accuracy against expert manual measurement, with typical absolute error of 1.5 to 2.0 cm.

These figures come from internal validation and have not been peer-reviewed. The validation population covered ages 16 to 78, weights of 38 to 210 kg and heights of 150 to 220 cm, from the US and Europe; performance outside this range has not been characterized. Accuracy is not published by body shape or skin tone. Body composition values are estimates. Supporting methodology is available under a non-disclosure agreement (NDA).

Body scanning accuracy, repeatability and validation methodology

- Photo retentionFitXpress deletes photos immediately after processing or retains them for up to 30 days under a contract-specific policy. Retained photos are blurred, and face obfuscation is applied during capture.

- Participant identifiersScan records use randomly generated identifiers, and 3DLOOK cannot identify an individual from stored records. The study maps them to its own participant numbers.

- HostingFitXpress data is hosted on Amazon Web Services (AWS), primarily in the US-West-2 region and partly in the US-East-1 region.

- General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)In most enterprise deployments, the contracting organization acts as data controller and 3DLOOK acts as data processor. A data processing agreement (DPA) with Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) covers international transfers, with a UK Addendum where UK GDPR applies.

- Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA)Where HIPAA applies, FitXpress can support the deployment with 3DLOOK acting as a business associate under an executed Business Associate Agreement (BAA).

- Regulatory statusAn independent regulatory assessment concluded that FitXpress does not meet the medical-device definition under the UK Medical Devices Regulations 2002 (UK MDR) or the EU Medical Devices Regulation (EU MDR) for its current intended purpose. FitXpress is not cleared, authorized or approved by the FDA.

- Vendor qualification documentsA security overview, penetration-test summary, architecture and data-flow diagrams and an incident-response overview are available under NDA. 3DLOOK is working toward a System and Organization Controls (SOC) 2 attestation report.

- Encryption and model trainingData is encrypted in transit and at rest. 3DLOOK does not use production customer data to train its models without the customer’s explicit, documented authorization.

Frequently asked questions

## Questions from clinical operations, data management and platform teams
Is FitXpress a medical device?
No. An independent regulatory assessment concluded that FitXpress does not meet the medical-device definition under UK MDR or EU MDR for its current intended purpose. FitXpress is not cleared, authorized or approved by the FDA. The sponsor assesses the regulatory status of the complete study workflow, including how the outputs are used.
How can a study use FitXpress data?
The sponsor decides how the data is used, for example as an exploratory endpoint, in a substudy or for participant follow-up between visits. The FDA guidance on digital health technologies for remote data acquisition (December 2023) expects verification and validation that are fit for the intended use, whether or not the technology is a device. Protocol-defined methods, such as a calibrated scale, tape measurement, DXA or MRI, keep their role.
Can FitXpress run white-label inside our trial platform?
Yes. The platform team embeds guided capture through native SDKs for iOS and Android, a React Native kit or web components, and configures the interface, flow, branding, consent text and privacy links. Participants stay inside the platform's app throughout the scan.
Can scan results stay hidden from participants and site staff?
Yes. Capture runs in the participant app, and results go to the study's backend through the API. The study team decides which outputs, if any, participants and site staff see, which supports blinded study designs.
Can participants use their own phones?
Yes. Capture runs on iOS 15 or later and on Android phones, including older models, which may show a lower frame rate during capture. Sponsors can also provide study devices. The participant stands for the scan, and the current dataset does not support wheelchair users or amputees. Two gender options are available. Height, weight and gender are required inputs. A height difference above 2 cm affects results, and studies can enter the height measured at the site.
How does scan data reach the EDC system?
Results return through the API as JSON, delivered through webhook events with polling as the documented fallback. Webhooks currently have no automatic retries or signature verification, and polling is the recommended backup. The study's technology team or platform vendor builds the mapping to the EDC system or the electronic clinical outcome assessment (eCOA) platform. The FitXpress Admin Panel provides CSV export of all scans under the study's API key.
Where is data hosted, and how are participants in the EU and UK covered?
FitXpress data is hosted on AWS, primarily in the US-West-2 region and partly in the US-East-1 region. For participants in the EU and UK, 3DLOOK provides a DPA with SCCs for international transfers and a UK Addendum where UK GDPR applies. In most deployments, the contracting organization is the data controller and 3DLOOK is the data processor.
How are software updates handled during a study?
3DLOOK notifies customers in advance of API changes or updates. Version requirements for the study period are agreed with 3DLOOK before the study starts.
Does FitXpress measure muscle?
FitXpress reports fat mass and lean mass estimates. Lean mass includes organs, bone and water along with muscle; it is not a direct measurement of skeletal muscle. Trials that assess muscle preservation use DXA, MRI or another method the protocol defines.
What documentation is available for vendor qualification?
The security overview is available on request. The penetration-test summary, architecture and data-flow diagrams, incident-response overview and business continuity summary are available under NDA. The DPA and, where HIPAA applies, the BAA are available during contracting. 3DLOOK completes security questionnaires on request. Documentation requests go to legal@3dlook.me.
