# Clinical trials page: fact-check of the problem block (2026-10-28)

All quotes below come from the primary documents. FDA PDFs were read from the Wayback copy of the fda.gov URL because fda.gov blocks our VPS. The checksums match every archived capture, the latest from September and October 2026, so the text has not changed. Line numbers are FDA's own margin numbers.

## Part A: claims in the drafts

### 1. FDA: waist circumference is "not standardized". Verdict: **correct** (minor paraphrase)

- **Source:** FDA, *Obesity and Overweight: Developing Drugs and Biological Products for Weight Reduction*, **draft** guidance, January 2025, Revision 2 (docket FDA-2007-D-0435). Still a draft: the FDA page captured 2026-09-26 says "Draft Level 1 Guidance". §III.B.3.b *Secondary efficacy endpoints*, p. 8, lines 316–322. https://www.fda.gov/media/71252/download
- **Quote:** "In clinical practice, waist circumference is sometimes used as an indirect measure of visceral fat, as it is easy and inexpensive to measure; but for regulatory purposes, it is not considered a surrogate for visceral fat content or metabolic abnormalities because the procedure is not standardized, the measurement is impacted by the extent of non-visceral fat, and the accuracy is reduced in patients with BMI greater than 35 kg/m2. Nevertheless, change in waist circumference may be evaluated as a secondary endpoint. Acceptability for labeling would depend on the quality of the data."
- **Notes:**
  - FDA says "surrogate for visceral fat content or metabolic abnormalities", not "surrogate endpoint".
  - FDA gives three reasons. Standardized capture addresses only the first. The other two (non-visceral fat, lower accuracy at BMI > 35) belong to the measure itself. In BELIEVE, mean baseline BMI was 37.3 and 63% of participants had BMI ≥35 (Table 1), so obesity-trial populations sit largely in the range where FDA says accuracy drops.
  - nika-v3 follows the quote with "With FitXpress, every site … produces the same record". That must not read as if FitXpress removes FDA's objection.
- **Safe wording:** "FDA's January 2025 draft guidance does not consider waist circumference a surrogate for visceral fat, partly because 'the procedure is not standardized.' Waist change may still be a secondary endpoint, and acceptability for labeling 'would depend on the quality of the data.'"

### 2. FDA: DXA/MRI are expensive, need blinded central readers, "representative subset". Verdict: **correct but stitched and misquoted**

- **Source:** same draft guidance.
  - §II.A, p. 3, lines 88–96: "Imaging modalities, such as dual x-ray absorptiometry (DXA) or magnetic resonance imaging, may provide more precise measures of body fat but are expensive and require use of multiple blinded, central readers for implementation in a trial. Trial results based on imaging changes may not be generalizable to clinical care … change in fat mass based on imaging or other modalities is not as clearly tied to clinical outcomes as change in BMI."
  - §III.C, p. 12, lines 480–483: "To ensure that drug-induced or biologic-induced weight reduction is caused primarily by a reduction in fat content, not lean-body mass, a representative sample of trial subjects should have a baseline and follow-up measurement of body composition by DXA or a suitable alternative."
- **Notes:**
  - The two quotes come from different sections: background on adiposity, and safety (lean-mass loss).
  - FDA's words are "representative **sample** of trial subjects", not "subset of participants".
  - FDA names no "suitable alternative". Do not suggest FitXpress is one.
- **Safe wording:** "FDA's draft guidance notes that DXA and MRI 'are expensive and require use of multiple blinded, central readers,' and asks that 'a representative sample of trial subjects' have body composition measured 'by DXA or a suitable alternative.'"

### 3. Primary endpoint "measured on a calibrated scale". Verdict: **endpoint correct; "calibrated scale" not in the guidance**

- **Source:** same draft guidance, §III.B.3.a, p. 8, lines 283–286.
- **Quote:** "The efficacy of a weight-reduction drug in adults should be assessed by analyses of mean percentage change from baseline body weight in the investigational drug group versus the control group."
- **Notes:**
  - The guidance never says "calibrated" or "scale" about body weight. "Scale" appears only in the name of the C-SSRS questionnaire.
  - EMA (EMA/CHMP/311805/2014, §4.1) does not say it either.
- **Safe wording:** "Mean percentage change from baseline body weight remains the primary efficacy endpoint in FDA's draft guidance." Keep "calibrated scale" only as our own description of site practice, without attributing it to FDA.

### 4. "30 to 45% attrition" (JAMA 2016). Verdict: **figure correct; dated, and the visit-burden link is ours**

- **Source:** Khera R et al. *JAMA* 2016;315(22):2424–2434. doi:10.1001/jama.2016.7602. PMID 27299618. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5617638/
- **Quotes:**
  - Abstract, Results: "High attrition rates (30%–45% in all trials) were associated with lower confidence in estimates."
  - Results: "Overall, studies were considered to be at high risk of bias, with attrition rates of 30% to 45% in all trials."
- **Context:**
  - The review covers 28 one-year RCTs (29,018 patients) of orlistat, lorcaserin, naltrexone-bupropion, phentermine-topiramate and liraglutide. It is a systematic review with a network meta-analysis.
  - It does not attribute attrition to visits.
  - Recent GLP-1 trials can retain far more. In STEP 1 (semaglutide 2.4 mg), 1,849 of 1,961 participants (94.3%) completed the study (ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03548935, participant flow).
- **Problem in nika-v3:** "Long studies with repeated visits add participant burden" sits right under the figure and implies a cause the paper does not state.
- **Safe wording:** "A 2016 JAMA network meta-analysis of 28 one-year trials of five older weight-loss drugs reported attrition of 30 to 45% in every trial." Do not tie the figure to visit burden, and do not present it as current.

### 5. BELIEVE: 92.8% vs 71.8% fat. Verdict: **conference figures, superseded by the peer-reviewed paper; DXA confirmed**

- **Where 92.8/71.8 come from:** ADA press release, 23 June 2025, p. 1: "The combination therapy yielded 92.8% of total weight loss from fat mass compared to semaglutide alone (71.8%) and a 22.1% decrease in bodyweight." https://professional.diabetes.org/sites/dpro/files/2025-06/ADA2025_BUNDLEDMUSCLEMASS6.21.pdf
- **Primary publication:** Heymsfield SB et al., "Bimagrumab plus semaglutide alone or in combination for the treatment of obesity: a randomized phase 2 trial", *Nature Medicine*, published online 2 March 2026. doi:10.1038/s41591-026-04204-0. PMID 41772149. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13004672/
- **Quotes from the paper:**
  - Week 48 (treatment-regimen estimand): "At week 48, the proportion of weight loss due to fat mass (fat loss index) was 100% (bimagrumab 30 mg kg−1), 71.1% (semaglutide 2.4 mg) and 92.3% (high-dose combination)."
  - Week 72: "At week 72, the proportion of weight loss due to fat mass was 100% (bimagrumab 30 mg kg−1), 75.6% (semaglutide 2.4 mg) and 92.2% (high-dose combination)."
  - Method: "Fat loss index is defined as change in total body fat mass (measured using DXA) …"
- **Notes:**
  - "High-dose combination" means bimagrumab 30 mg/kg IV plus semaglutide 2.4 mg.
  - The paper calls it "phase 2". The ADA release says "Phase 2b".
- **Safe wording:** "In the phase 2 BELIEVE trial (Nature Medicine, 2026), DXA showed that 92.3% of weight lost by week 48 on high-dose bimagrumab with semaglutide was fat, versus 71.1% on semaglutide alone." Cite Nature Medicine, not Medscape.

### 6. FDA guidance on DHTs for remote data acquisition. Verdict: **correct; two wording fixes**

- **Source:** FDA, *Digital Health Technologies for Remote Data Acquisition in Clinical Investigations: Guidance for Industry, Investigators, and Other Stakeholders*. **Final**, December 2023 (Federal Register notice 22 Dec 2023; the draft was Dec 2021). https://www.fda.gov/media/155022/download
- **Quotes:**
  - §IV.C, p. 11: "This guidance uses the terms verification and validation to describe steps that help ensure that the DHT is fit-for-purpose for remote data collection in a clinical investigation. Verification and validation should be addressed regardless of whether the DHT meets the definition of a device under section 201(h) of the FD&C Act."
  - §IV.A, p. 8: "In choosing an appropriate DHT, sponsors should consider the clinical event or characteristic … identify appropriate technical and performance specifications of a DHT, and consider the proposed trial population …"
  - §IV.A.4, p. 9: "Sponsors should evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of allowing trial participants to use their own DHTs … and/or other technologies (e.g., general-purpose computing platforms such as smartphones and tablets) …"
- **Wording fixes:**
  - FDA's term is "fit-for-purpose", not "fit for the intended use".
  - "Sets sponsor responsibilities" overstates it. On p. 3 FDA says its guidances "do not establish legally enforceable responsibilities" and "should be viewed only as recommendations".
- **Watch:** on 31 March 2026 FDA published a Request for Information, "Advancing the Use of Digital Health Technologies in Clinical Investigations for Drugs and Biological Products" (Federal Register 2026-06184).
- **Safe wording:** "FDA's final guidance on DHTs for remote data acquisition (December 2023) recommends that sponsors select fit-for-purpose DHTs and address verification and validation 'regardless of whether the DHT meets the definition of a device.'"

### 7. ICH E6(R3) covers essential records, source data, audit trails, metadata and traceability. Verdict: **correct**

- **Source:** ICH E6(R3) Guideline for Good Clinical Practice, final version, "Adopted on 06 January 2025" (Step 4). https://database.ich.org/sites/default/files/ICH_E6%28R3%29_Step4_FinalGuideline_2025_0106.pdf
- **Status by region:**
  - **EU:** "The overarching principles and Annex 1 … came into effect on 23 July 2025". Annex 2 reached Step 4 on 3 June 2026 and comes into effect on 15 January 2027. https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/ich-e6-good-clinical-practice-scientific-guideline
  - **US:** FDA published it as final guidance (Federal Register 9 Sept 2025, doc 2025-17311).
- **Where each topic sits in the document:**
  - **Data governance:** §4 intro, p. 45: "appropriate management of data integrity, traceability and security".
  - **Metadata and audit trails:** §4.2.2 "Relevant Metadata, Including Audit Trails", p. 46.
  - **Source records:** §2.12.2, p. 21: "Changes to source records should be traceable … (via an audit trail)".
  - **Essential records:** Appendix C "Essential Records for the Conduct of a Clinical Trial", p. 63.
  - **DHTs:** Principles, p. 2: "digital health technologies, such as wearables and sensors, may expand the possible approaches to trial conduct … This guideline is intended to be media neutral".
- **Note:** the guideline mostly says "source records". "Source data" appears only twice, in the monitoring and computerised-systems sections.
- **Safe wording:** "ICH E6(R3) (adopted January 2025) covers essential records, source records, metadata and audit trails, and data traceability."

## Part B: the buyer's pain, in primary sources

1. **Waist tape varies by who holds it.**
   - Panoulas 2008, *Postgrad Med J*, 102 patients: inter-operator variability "could lead to disagreement among operators regarding the presence of central obesity in 9% of the patients". With written instructions it "lost significance". https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18716012/
   - Verweij 2013, *Public Health Nutr*, review of 9 studies: "The inter-observer measurement error varied from 1·4 cm to 15 cm." https://doi.org/10.1017/S1368980012002741
2. **No single protocol.** IAS/ICCR consensus (Ross 2020): "there is no consensus regarding the optimal protocol for measurement of waist circumference". The WHO site (midpoint) and the NIH site (iliac crest) differ by about 2 cm in women, and by about 9 cm in one Japanese sample. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7027970/
3. **Remote weight is accepted in principle, with V&V.**
   - FDA DHT guidance, §IV.D.2, p. 16: "When DHT measurements replicate existing measurements (e.g., weight measurements at home versus in the clinic) for the same endpoint, a new justification for the choice of the endpoint may not be needed. However, the DHT should still be verified and validated".
   - Gavin 2023: "cellular scale weights were 0.6 kg (95% CI: −2.9, 2.2) lower than in-person weights". https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37546286/
   - EMA, 2016 guideline, §4.2.1, p. 5: waist circumference "should be documented as secondary endpoints". https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/scientific-guideline/guideline-clinical-evaluation-medicinal-products-used-weight-management_en.pdf
4. **Self-measured waist is no substitute.**
   - Contardo Ayala 2014, 622 participants: "self-measured WC was overestimated by 5.98 ± 4.82 cm". Ross 2020 summarises studies in which people underestimate by 1 to 3 cm, so even the direction of the error varies. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25274418/
   - FDA DCT guidance (final, Sept 2024, §III.A, p. 4): home tests "may introduce variability compared to tests performed under supervision". https://www.fda.gov/media/167696/download
5. **FDA treats visit burden as a retention issue.**
   - Obesity draft guidance, §V.B, p. 17: protocols should include "offers of transportation to the clinic", along with options for those "unable or unwilling to continue all study visits".
   - DCT guidance, p. 7: home-based activities "may reduce the need for travel and improve engagement, recruitment, and retention".

**Not supported by primary sources. Do not use:**
- Dropout driven by "measurement-only" visits. No source found.
- Any figure for how much remote check-ins reduce dropout. FDA only says "may".
- 30 to 45% as today's attrition. In STEP 1, 94.3% of participants completed the study (https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03548935). In BELIEVE, 74.4% completed the 48-week treatment period.
- "Waist tape is unreliable" as a blanket claim. IAS/ICCR calls waist circumference "easy to standardize", and written instructions removed the significant inter-operator effect (Panoulas). The real pain is inconsistent protocol and training across sites.
- "FDA requires DXA". FDA asks for "DXA or a suitable alternative" in a representative sample.
