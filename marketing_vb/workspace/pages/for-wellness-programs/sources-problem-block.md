# For Wellness Programs: source check for the problem block

**Read first.** All four numbers exist in primary sources. Three of the cards stretch them, though:

- **Card 1 (40%)** measures how far off a national prevalence estimate is. It says nothing about any one person's error. It also concerns BMI from self-reported height and weight, and FitXpress BMI comes from height and weight the user types in. The card attacks an input we still use.
- **Card 3 (62%)** counts incentives for *completing* a screening. Only 14% of large firms reward *results*, so "when money depends on the result" is wrong for the 62%.
- **Card 4 (78%)** covers remote-capable workers only, about half the US workforce. Remote screening also exists already: 50% of large firms with a screening program allow it (KFF 2025).
- **KFF 2026 EHBS** (published 2026-10-22) did not ask about wellness or biometric screening. **2025 is the latest data year** for cards 2 and 3.

## Part A: claims on the draft

### 1. "40%: self-reported height and weight underestimated the prevalence of severe obesity by 40%" (CDC)
- **Verdict:** correct as quoted, but overstated if it is read as individual error.
- **Source:** Zhao L, Park S, Ward ZJ, Cradock AL, Gortmaker SL, Blanck HM. *Prev Chronic Dis* 2023;20:230005 (13 Jul 2023), doi:10.5888/pcd20.230005. Published in CDC's journal; three authors are CDC staff and three are from Harvard. Data: BRFSS 2020 (n=344,087), bias-corrected against measured NHANES 2013-2018. https://www.cdc.gov/pcd/issues/2023/23_0005.htm
- **Quote (Abstract, Conclusion):** "Self-reported BRFSS data underestimated the overall prevalence of severe obesity by 40% (5.3% vs 8.8%)." Methods: "Severe (also known as class III) obesity was defined as BMI of 40 or higher." Introduction: biases "tend to be greater among people with higher BMI".
- **What kind of figure:** a relative gap in population prevalence (5.3% vs 8.8% of US adults with BMI ≥40). It is not the size of any one person's error.
- **Safe wording:** "In national survey data, self-reported height and weight undercounted severe obesity among US adults by about 40% (5.3% vs 8.8%, 2020). Source: Zhao et al., *Preventing Chronic Disease* (CDC), 2023." Use it only if the copy does not suggest that FitXpress removes self-report from BMI.

### 2. "43% of larger firms offering health benefits provide biometric screening" (KFF EHBS)
- **Verdict:** correct for **2025**, the latest data year. In 2024 the figure was 44%.
- **Source:** KFF *Employer Health Benefits 2025 Annual Survey*, Section 12, p. 185, and Summary of Findings, p. 14. 1,862 firms, fielded Jan-Jul 2025. https://files.kff.org/attachment/Employer-Health-Benefits-Survey-2025-Annual-Survey.pdf
- **Quote:** "Among firms that offer health benefits, 22% of firms with 10 to 199 workers and 43% of larger firms provide workers the opportunity to complete a biometric screening."
- **Definition:** "larger firms" means 200 or more workers (Fig. 12.5: "All Large Firms"). The measure is "provide the opportunity", i.e. the firm offers screening. It does not say workers took it. KFF defines biometric screening as "in-person health examinations conducted by a medical professional" covering "cholesterol level, body mass index (BMI), blood pressure, and blood glucose levels".
- **Not in the source:** "a recurring verification workload for HR". That is our gloss, so cut it or rephrase it as opinion.
- **Safe wording:** "43% of US firms with 200+ workers that offer health benefits give employees the option of a biometric screening (KFF, 2025)."

### 3. "62% of large firms with biometric screening tie it to incentives or penalties" (KFF EHBS)
- **Verdict:** the number is correct, but the meaning is overstated. It describes incentives for *completing* the screening, not for the results.
- **Source:** KFF EHBS **2025**, Section 12, p. 185, and Fig. 12.6. Same PDF as claim 2.
- **Quote:** "Among firms with a biometric screening program, 34% of firms with 10 to 199 workers and 62% of larger firms use incentives or penalties to encourage workers to complete the assessment." Outcomes, Fig. 12.6: **14%** of large firms with a screening program have "Incentives to Achieve Biometric Outcomes" (21% at firms with 5,000+ workers). The report text instead gives "21% have incentives or penalties tied to whether workers meet specified biometric outcomes" without naming a firm size. Cite the 14% from the figure.
- **Safe wording:** "62% of large firms with a biometric screening program use incentives or penalties to get employees to complete it; 14% tie rewards to meeting biometric targets (KFF, 2025)." Then drop "When money depends on the result", or attach it to the 14%.

### 4. "78% of remote-capable US workers are hybrid (52%) or fully remote (26%)" (Gallup)
- **Verdict:** correct. The latest data point is **May 2026**.
- **Source:** Gallup, *Hybrid Work* indicator, Gallup Panel web survey of employed US adults. https://www.gallup.com/401384/indicator-hybrid-work.aspx
- **Quote:** "Gallup's data from May 2026 show that the majority of remote-capable employees are currently working in a hybrid (52%) or exclusively remote arrangement (26%), with 22% working on-site." The base is the remote-capable workforce only: "Remote-capable workers make up about half of the total U.S. workforce" (Gallup, 2 Sep 2025, https://www.gallup.com/workplace/694361/hybrid-work-retreat-barely.aspx).
- **Not supported:** "Onsite screening days miss many of them" is our own inference. KFF 2025 Fig. 12.4: 61% of all firms with screening, and 50% of large firms, already allow remote completion.
- **Safe wording:** "Among US employees whose jobs can be done remotely, 52% work hybrid and 26% fully remote (Gallup, May 2026)." Pair it with: "Half of large firms with a screening program offer no remote option (KFF, 2025)."

## Part B: the buyer's pain (primary facts)

1. **What screening is and how it is delivered.** KFF 2025 (p. 185): BMI, blood pressure, cholesterol and glucose, done "in-person … by a medical professional". Even so, "61% allow employees to complete the screening remotely" (all firms); the large-firm figure is 50% (Fig. 12.4). KFF does not define "remotely". Don't claim kits or physician forms are the norm.
2. **Participation is partial.** In the RAND *Workplace Wellness Programs Study* (DOL/HHS, 2013, §4.1.1, p. 36), employees "participate in clinical screenings (46 percent), if offered", with rates "ranging from 0 to 100 percent". The data is old, so give the year. https://aspe.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/migrated_legacy_files/44011/rpt_wellness.pdf (403; Wayback works). In an RCT at a US warehouse retailer: "mean participation rate in surveys and screenings at intervention sites was 36.2% to 44.6%" (Song & Baicker, *JAMA* 2019;321:1491, PMID 30990549).
3. **The people who show up are healthier.** In the Illinois Workplace Wellness Study: "strong patterns of selection: … program participants had lower medical expenditures and healthier behaviors than nonparticipants" (Jones, Molitor, Reif, *QJE* 2019;134:1747, PMID 31564754).
4. **Federal rules: what not to say.** EEOC's 2016 ADA/GINA rules capped incentives at 30%. *AARP v. EEOC* (D.D.C. 2017) vacated the incentive sections "effective January 1, 2019", and EEOC removed them from the CFR (83 FR 65296, 20 Dec 2018; appendix cleanup 89 FR 55520, 5 Jul 2024). The January 2021 proposed rules ("no more than a de minimis incentive", EEOC release 7 Jan 2021) were never published in the Federal Register and were withdrawn on 12 Feb 2021 (K&L Gates, secondary). Reginfo last lists RIN 3046-AB10 in Fall 2020; I found no newer rule. The separate HIPAA/ACA rule (78 FR 33158, 2013) still caps health-contingent rewards at 30% (50% for tobacco). **So:** say nothing about "EEOC-compliant", "ADA/GINA-safe", "30% safe harbor" or "HIPAA-compliant incentives".
5. **Self-report vs measured, at work.** Among 1,298 overweight Dutch employees, "BMI was significantly … under-reported by 0.7 kg/m2. WC was significantly … over-reported by 1.1 cm". The authors call self-report "satisfactorily accurate" *for prevalence*, while the error grew with measured weight and waist (Dekkers et al., *BMC Med Res Methodol* 2008;8:69, PMID 18957077). **So:** don't claim that self-reports are badly wrong for each person.
6. **HR admin burden:** I found no primary source that puts a number on it. Keep it qualitative.

**Often repeated online, not supported by the primary source:** "43% of employers offer biometric screening" (the figure covers large firms only; small firms are at 22%). "62% reward biometric results" (the 62% is for completion; results-based is 14%). "78% of US workers are hybrid/remote" (it is 78% of remote-capable workers). "CDC: self-reported BMI is 40% off" (it is the prevalence of BMI ≥40). "EEOC caps incentives at 30%" (vacated in 2019). FitXpress is not a biometric screening in KFF's sense: no lipids, glucose or blood pressure.
