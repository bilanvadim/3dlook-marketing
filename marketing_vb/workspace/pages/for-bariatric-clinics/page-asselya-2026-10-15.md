How it works

## Add body scans to the pathway your patients already follow

Your team embeds FitXpress through web and mobile software development kits (SDKs) and retrieves results through the API. Scans run inside the program’s patient app or system. The FitXpress Admin Panel gives a central view of scan results, with comma-separated values (CSV) export, for teams that do not build their own dashboard.

### Scan before the first visit

After the first enquiry, the patient completes the program’s consent flow and a guided front and side capture on their phone. Real-Time Pose Validation (RTPV) guides pose and framing.

### Review before booking

The coordinator reviews completed scan results and available quality information, then refers cases for clinical review according to the program’s workflow. Your program sets review thresholds and defines when a discrepancy requires another scan, a coordinator call or an in-clinic measurement.

### Repeat after surgery

The pre-surgery scan becomes the baseline. Patients complete follow-up scans from home on the schedule your program sets.

The recommended integration combines the SDK’s guided capture with the API for results retrieval. [3DLOOK’s mobile body scanning technology](https://3dlook.ai/technology/) explains the capture and processing approach.

Validation and data handling

## Understand measurement performance and patient data handling

Repeatability describes how much repeated measurements vary under comparable conditions and helps teams interpret changes over time. FitXpress’s internal validation evaluates repeated scans and comparisons with expert manual measurements.

< 1 cmTypical scan-to-scan difference for most evaluated body measurements.

96–97%Body-measurement accuracy against expert manual measurement, with typical absolute error of 1.5–2.0 cm.

These body-measurement figures come from internal validation and have not been peer-reviewed. The validation population covered weights of 38–210 kg and heights of 150–220 cm; performance outside this range has not been characterized. Body composition values are estimates. These accuracy and repeatability figures apply to body measurements. They do not establish the accuracy of body composition estimates or performance specifically after bariatric surgery. Frequently asked questions

## Questions about remote follow-up and pre-qualification

How often can patients scan after surgery?

Your program sets the follow-up schedule and selects the scans for comparison. Body Progress compares two completed scans using their scan IDs. Your program links scans to patient records and decides which results patients see. Available outputs depend on the submitted profile information and implementation.

Does FitXpress measure muscle loss after surgery?

FitXpress reports fat mass and lean mass estimates. Lean mass includes organs, bone and water along with muscle; it is not a direct measurement of skeletal muscle or muscle loss. The care team determines whether further assessment, such as dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (DXA) or bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA), is required.

Can FitXpress run inside the program’s existing patient app?

Yes. Your team embeds guided capture through web and mobile software development kits (SDKs) and retrieves results through the FitXpress API into the program’s patient app or system. The FitXpress Admin Panel gives a central view of scan results with comma-separated values (CSV) export. The [FitXpress API documentation](https://docs.fitxpress.3dlook.me/) covers integration details.

Does FitXpress decide whether a patient qualifies for surgery?

No. FitXpress supplies body metrics and discrepancy flags for review. Surgical candidacy remains the clinical team’s determination under the program’s criteria. FitXpress is not a medical device and does not diagnose conditions, make treatment decisions, or determine treatment eligibility.

What happens when a patient cannot complete the scan?

The capture requires a smartphone and standing front and side photos. Mobility, available space and smartphone access affect completion. Your program sets the retake logic and an in-clinic alternative, and a pilot measures completion with and without assistance.

Has FitXpress been validated at higher body weights?

Internal body-measurement validation included participants weighing 38–210 kg, with heights of 150–220 cm and ages of 16–78, from the US and Europe. Performance outside these ranges has not been characterized. Use the pilot to evaluate capture completion and measurement performance in your intended bariatric patient population.

Can the record support prior-authorization documentation for US programs?

Each insurer or health plan sets its documentation requirements and accepted data sources. The program can include dated scan outputs and the inputs used to calculate BMI in its supporting records. Acceptance depends on the payer’s requirements. FitXpress does not guarantee payer acceptance or approval.
