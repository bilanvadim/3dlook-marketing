## Use case (1 sentence)

An Australian app, club or health-fund program adds a white-label FitXpress scan to the check-ins it already runs, inside its own member app. The member takes two guided photos on their own phone with no hardware and no club visit, and the program gets one timestamped record per scan through the API (80+ body measurements and body composition estimates, along with BMI and BMR as calculated metrics). The record is linked to the member profile, and the program decides what the member and the coach see. 3D Body Progress tracking and 3D Goal Visualization come on the Pro plan.

## Why this is plausible (3 reasons grounded in evidence)

1. **In Australia, body-composition checks happen in the club, while more training happens away from it.** In-club scanners are common: a national gym network rolling them out ([Ausleisure](https://ausleisure.com.au/news/genesis-health-fitness-continues-its-rollout-of-evolt-body-scanners-nationally)), council leisure centres ([Yarra Leisure](https://www.yarracity.vic.gov.au/yarra-leisure/gym/intelligent-body-scanner)) and YMCA Victoria ([YMCA](https://victoria.ymca.org.au/programs-services/recreation/body-composition-scan)). Each repeat of a scan on club hardware needs a club visit (the page's FAQ). Meanwhile AusPlay data put 2024/25 gym participation 15.6% below 2022/23, with some people moving to home equipment and outdoor exercise ([Ausleisure, September 2026](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/australian-fitness-industry-revenue-forecast-to-approach-38-billion)). AUSactive's trends survey ranks Pilates first for 2027 and has technology gaining momentum ([Ausleisure](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/pilates-tops-ausactives-fitness-trends-survey-for-2027-as-technology-and-competitive-fitness-gain-momentum)). Studio formats are spreading fast: inLIFE Wellness at 56 locations ([Ausleisure](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/inlife-wellness-opens-franchising-as-it-targets-expansion-across-australia-new-zealand-and-the-usa)), Club Pilates at 40 Australian studios with 60 more planned ([Ausleisure](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/club-pilates-reaches-40-australian-studios-with-60-more-planned)), Stepz planning to pass 30 clubs ([Ausleisure](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/stepz-fitness-accelerates-national-growth-with-up-to-six-new-clubs-before-year-end)).
2. **Apps lose members before the program shows results, and acquisition is getting dearer.** 24% of health and fitness app users are active on day 1 and 7% on day 30 (Adjust benchmark, as cited on the page). A median 70% of users quit lifestyle and mental health apps within 100 days, with lack of personalisation among the reasons (Kidman, Curtis, Watson and Maher, [JMIR 2024;26:e56897](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897)). Australian operators report growth while competition and acquisition costs rise ([Ausleisure](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/fitness-operators-report-ongoing-growth-as-competition-and-acquisition-costs-rise)). Health-fund programs measure effort, not body change. For example, Medibank's Weekly Goal counts 7,000 steps, active minutes and 7 hours of sleep, and states that activity data cannot affect premiums ([Medibank Live Better](https://medibank.com.au/livebetter/rewards/earn/weekly-goal)). HCF's Healthful app offers a digital coach and health programs ([HCF](https://www.hcf.com.au/members/manage-your-health/hcf-apps/healthful)).
3. **The live page answers both pains, with production behind it.** Source: `published-live-2026-11-19.md`. FitXpress gives an in-app scan on the member's own phone, with no hardware and no club visit, and Real-Time Pose Validation guides pose and framing. It returns 80+ body measurements (including waist, hip, chest, thigh and calf), along with body composition estimates (body-fat percentage, lean mass and fat mass) and BMI and BMR as calculated metrics. The program gets one timestamped record per scan with a processing status, through the API, and links the scan ID to the member profile. The product is white-label, through web and mobile SDKs (iOS and Android supported). 3D progress tools are on the Pro plan. The pilot design is on the page, and it runs on "production infrastructure already deployed in remote weight-management and BMI-verification workflows". **Honest limits:** body composition values are estimates; there is no Australian reference customer and no fitness reference we can name; the validation population covers the US and Europe; performance is not established for people with physical disabilities, and the scan is not for people under 16 or in pregnancy (`proof-points.md`).

## Target buyer persona

**Who buys:** the person who owns the member app or the program inside it. At app-first brands that is the founder or CEO, together with the product head. At franchisors it is head office: CEO, general manager, digital or product, and programming. At health funds it is the owner of the wellbeing program and the product owner of the member app. Partnership owners are a way in at every group.

- **Founder, CEO or Managing Director (A, B, D).** Owns differentiation and the subscription or membership bet. Cares about members who stay past the first month, and about premium-tier take-up. Objection: "Will members actually scan?" Answer: the pilot measures scan completion and repeat scans separately, and the product team chooses where the scan sits.
- **Chief Product Officer, Head of Product, Product Manager or Owner, Head of Digital (A, B, C).** Owns build-versus-buy and the check-in flow. Cares about activation, onboarding completion and roadmap load. Objection: "A scan step could hurt onboarding completion." Answer: the page's FAQ says it can; the scan can sit at the first check-in instead, and the pilot compares onboarding completion with the usual flow.
- **Head of Programming, Head of Training, Head Coach, National Fitness Manager (A, B).** Owns how coaches review progress. Cares about program adherence and coach time. Here the page's coach line applies: the coach reviews the same record before setting the next program block.
- **GM or Head of Health and Wellbeing, Head of Member Health, Health or Wellbeing Programs Manager (C).** Owns participation and what the program can show members. Cares about engagement between health checks and member trust. Objection: "Body data near an insurer is sensitive." Answer: the fund decides what members see; the scan is for wellness and engagement only; nothing touches premiums, cover or claims.
- **Head of Partnerships, Partnerships Manager, Business Development Manager, Head of Rewards (all groups).** Owns third-party features in the member offer. The `partnerships` lane, and the route in below C-level (PPL-1, PPL-3).
- **CTO or Head of Technology (A, B).** Implementer and an internal champion, not the economic buyer: `technical-integration`, P3.
- **Franchise and studio owners, franchise business managers (B):** `referral`. They point to head office and are never pitched adoption.

**What they care about (KPIs):** day-30 and day-90 retention; free-to-premium or class-pack to membership conversion; onboarding completion; program adherence; at funds, active participation in the program.

**Likely objections:** members will not scan (pilot measures it); onboarding friction (the scan can move to the first check-in); "we already have scans in the club" (an in-club scan suits members who visit; the phone scan covers the check-ins between visits, said without naming any device); privacy (replies only, from `compliance.md` §10 and the FAQ link); price (Open question 1).

**Not the buyer:** marketing, brand, social and content; CRM, lifecycle, retention and growth marketing (PPL-3: 0/44); customer service and member support; finance, legal, HR and payroll; claims, underwriting, pricing, actuarial, risk, broker and hospital contracting at funds; dental, optical and hearing at funds; non-clinical operations without app ownership (PPL-3: 0/55); engineers below head level; studio front-desk staff.

**The pain, by group (a target for the writer, about the method; never about the company's staff, coaches, members or what its app lacks):**

- **A and D (apps).** Progress at a check-in is usually a weight entry and progress photos, and the photos are not measured, so changes in waist, lean mass or fat mass do not show as numbers. The industry benchmark behind it: Adjust's day-1 to day-30 drop for health and fitness apps, or Kidman et al.'s 100-day median. This is the HYP-6 test (see "Learnings applied").
- **B (clubs and studios).** Measurement that runs on club hardware needs a club visit for every repeat, so check-ins between classes or at home have no measured record. Never say or imply that the club has a scanner, and never name a device type or maker.
- **C (funds).** Activity programs record effort (steps, active minutes, sleep), while body measures need an appointment or a self-entered number. Never say or imply that members misreport.

**The fix, every group (mechanism plus what the buyer gets, from the live page; at least two of these in every Message 1, rotated across people at one company):** the scan runs inside the brand's own app on the member's phone, with no hardware and no club visit; two guided photos, with pose and framing guidance during capture; 80+ body measurements including waist and hip, along with body composition estimates (body-fat percentage, lean mass, fat mass); one timestamped record per scan through the API, linked to the member profile; the platform chooses what the member and the coach see, under its own brand; the coach can review the same record before the next program block (A, B); 3D Body Progress tracking and 3D Goal Visualization on the Pro plan, as a feature of a paid tier (A, B, D only).

**Question topics (Message 1 ends on one):** where in the program progress is checked today; what a member sees at the first check-in; how coaches review a member before the next block; how the program shows change between club visits or health checks; whether 3D progress would sit in a paid tier (A, D).

- **Message 1, in this order (Vadim 2026-11-18).** First, one line that could only be written to this company, from Account facts. Then the pain for its group, about the method (see "Message angle"). Then how FitXpress fixes it: the mechanism and what the buyer gets, in two or three sentences from the fix list. Then ONE question from the topics. No rule in this file shrinks the product to one clause. A Message 1 that names only "two photos" without what comes back fails.
- **Message 1 gate.** Every message 1 carries at least one product specific from `proof-points.md` and one line that could only have been written to that company; each pair of messages cites at least one number. The `referral` lane is not exempt.
- **Message 2.** It opens with one value line that continues that person's own Message 1 question. If it offers a pilot, it describes the design whole, as in the next rule. It ends with the soft ask. People who are not the buyer get an easy out that points to whoever owns the member app or the wellbeing program at head office, with the target following from that person's Message 1 topic. No two people at one company share an easy-out target wording or a closing shape.
- **The pilot (from the page), as one design:**
  - **Who scans:** one member cohort, at onboarding and at the first check-in.
  - **What it is compared with:** a comparable cohort that keeps the usual check-in flow, assigned at random before the pilot where practical.
  - **What is counted:** scan completion and retake rates; repeat scans at the first check-in; day-30 and day-90 retention against the comparison cohort; free-to-premium conversion where 3D progress sits in a paid tier; onboarding completion against the usual flow.
  - **How it is judged:** against criteria agreed before the pilot. If they are met, the scan goes live for all members on the matching plan.
  - **What it does not touch:** no promised lift, and no decision about any member is made from a scan.
  - **At funds (C):** retention means members still active in the program at day 30 and day 90. Leave out the paid-tier measure, because a fund has no paid tier and the word would read as insurance pricing. Nothing in the pilot touches premiums, cover or claims.
- **Retention numbers (HYP-6 test).** Groups A, C and D only. The Adjust figures (24% active on day 1, 7% on day 30, for health and fitness apps) or the Kidman et al. median (70% quit lifestyle apps within 100 days) may give the pain its stakes in Message 1, with the source named in a few words.
