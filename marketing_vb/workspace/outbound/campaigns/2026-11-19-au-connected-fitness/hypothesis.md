## Use case (1 sentence)

An Australian or New Zealand fitness app, studio network or club chain adds a white-label FitXpress scan to the progress check-ins it already runs in its own member app, where the member takes two guided photos on their own phone with no hardware and no club visit, the app receives one timestamped record per scan through the API (80+ body measurements, body composition estimates, and BMI and BMR as calculated metrics) linked to the member profile, the coach or instructor reviews the same record before the next program block, and 3D Body Progress tracking and 3D Goal Visualization on the Pro plan can sit in the brand's paid tier.

## Why this is plausible (3 reasons grounded in evidence)

1. **Training happens more and more away from the club, while body checks stay in it.** In-club scanners are common in Australia: a national gym network rolling them out ([Ausleisure](https://ausleisure.com.au/news/genesis-health-fitness-continues-its-rollout-of-evolt-body-scanners-nationally)), council leisure centres ([Yarra Leisure](https://www.yarracity.vic.gov.au/yarra-leisure/gym/intelligent-body-scanner)) and YMCA Victoria ([YMCA](https://victoria.ymca.org.au/programs-services/recreation/body-composition-scan)). The page's FAQ says each repeat of a scan on club hardware needs a club visit. AusPlay data put 2024/25 gym participation 15.6% below 2022/23, with some people moving to home equipment and outdoor exercise ([Ausleisure, September 2026](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/australian-fitness-industry-revenue-forecast-to-approach-38-billion)). AUSactive's trends survey ranks Pilates first for 2027, with technology gaining momentum ([Ausleisure](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/pilates-tops-ausactives-fitness-trends-survey-for-2027-as-technology-and-competitive-fitness-gain-momentum)). In New Zealand, the largest gym brand runs 65 locations with more than 250,000 members ([AdNews, May 2026](https://www.adnews.com.au/news/sundance-fitness-media-strikes-nz-partnership-with-cityfitness)) and a member app of its own ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1460262509)), and plans up to NZ$100 million of expansion over five years, including a women-only format ([Ausleisure, October 2026](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/cityfitness-announces-100-million-new-zealand-expansion-and-move-into-women-only-market)). Both markets have brands whose product is the at-home program: LES MILLS+ from Auckland ([App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id1483001711)), Studio Pilates+ from Brisbane ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/tt/app/studio-pilates/id1510535255)), Fluidform Pilates at Home from Sydney ([App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/id6444091702)).
2. **At a check-in, most apps hold a weight entry and progress photos that are not measured, and members cost more to win.** The page's comparison table sets it out: a weight log and progress photos record weight, and the photos are not measured. Australian operators report growth while competition and acquisition costs rise ([Ausleisure](https://www.ausleisure.com.au/news/fitness-operators-report-ongoing-growth-as-competition-and-acquisition-costs-rise)). App-market context for this file only, never for copy (neither figure is in `proof-points.md`): the page cites an Adjust benchmark of 24% of health and fitness app users active on day 1 and 7% on day 30 (global data, confirmed in October only through a search index), and a median 70% of users quitting lifestyle and mental health apps within 100 days (Kidman, Curtis, Watson and Maher, [JMIR 2024;26:e56897](https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897)).
3. **The live page answers it, with production behind it.** Source: `published-live-2026-11-19.md`. FitXpress runs an in-app scan on the member's own phone, with no hardware and no club visit, and Real-Time Pose Validation guides pose and framing. It returns 80+ body measurements (including waist, hip, chest, thigh and calf), body composition estimates (body-fat percentage, lean mass and fat mass), and BMI and BMR as calculated metrics. The program gets one timestamped record per scan with a processing status through the API, links the scan ID to the member profile, and chooses what the member and the coach see; for comparisons over time, the program compares scans it selects (`compliance.md` §4). The product is white-label through web and mobile SDKs (iOS and Android supported), with the capture layer fixed. 3D Body Progress tracking and 3D Goal Visualization are on the Pro plan. The pilot design is on the page, and FitXpress runs "on production infrastructure already deployed in remote weight-management and BMI-verification workflows". **Honest limits:** body composition values are estimates; there is no Australian, New Zealand or fitness reference customer we can name; the validation population covers the US and Europe; performance is not established for people with physical disabilities, and the scan is not for people under 16 or in pregnancy (`proof-points.md`).

## Target buyer persona

**Who buys:** the person who owns the member app or the program inside it. At app-first brands (A, D) that is the founder or CEO, together with the product head. At studio and club networks (B) it is head office: CEO or general manager, digital or product, and programming or training. Partnerships owners are a way in at every group.

- **Founder, CEO, Managing Director, General Manager (A, B, D).** Owns differentiation and the subscription or membership bet. Cares about members who stay past the first month and about paid-tier take-up. Objection: "Will members actually scan?" Answer: the pilot measures scan completion and repeat scans separately, and the product team chooses where the scan sits.
- **Chief Product Officer, Head of Product, Product Manager or Owner, Head of Digital (A, B, D).** Owns build or buy and the check-in flow. Cares about activation, onboarding completion and roadmap load. Objection: "A scan step could hurt onboarding completion." Answer: the page's FAQ says it can; the scan can sit at the first check-in, and the pilot compares onboarding completion with the usual flow.
- **Head of Programming, Training, Education or Fitness; Head Coach; National Fitness Manager (A, B, D).** Owns how coaches and instructors review progress. Cares about program adherence and coach time. The page's coach line is their lane: the coach reviews the same record before setting the next program block.
- **Head of Partnerships, Partnerships Manager, Business Development Manager (all groups).** Owns third-party features in the member offer. The `partnerships` lane, and a route in below C-level (PPL-1, PPL-3).
- **CTO, Head of Technology, Head of Engineering (A, B).** Implementer and internal champion, not the economic buyer: `technical-integration`, P3, at most two per company.
- **Franchisees, studio and club owners, franchise business managers (B):** `referral`. They point to head office and are never pitched adoption.

**What they care about (KPIs):** day-30 and day-90 retention; free-to-premium or class-pack to membership conversion; onboarding completion; program adherence.

**Likely objections:** members will not scan (the pilot measures it); onboarding friction (the scan can move to the first check-in); "we already measure members in the club" (the phone scan covers the check-ins between visits, said without naming any device); "we could build it" (a pre-trained model through SDK and API, with no computer-vision team to hire); privacy (replies only, from `compliance.md` §10 with the FAQ link); price (replies only, from the public page).

**Not the buyer:** marketing, brand, social, content and PR; CRM, lifecycle, retention and growth marketing (PPL-3: 0/44); customer service and member support; membership sales and front desk; finance, legal, HR and payroll; non-clinical operations without app ownership, COO included (PPL-3: 0/55); engineers below head level; individual personal trainers and instructors without a head-office role; investors and board members.

## Message angle: lanes and account facts
