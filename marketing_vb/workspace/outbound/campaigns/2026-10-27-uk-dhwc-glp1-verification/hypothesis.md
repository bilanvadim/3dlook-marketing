## Use case (1 sentence)

A UK online pharmacy or GLP-1 prescriber replaces the camera-roll photo upload at its BMI eligibility step with a live, in-session FitXpress capture that cross-checks a Smart Scales weight estimate against the self-reported figure and leaves a timestamped structured record for clinical review.

## Why this is plausible (3 reasons grounded in evidence)

1. **The edited-photo route is live and documented.** On 2 January 2026 an ITV News investigation found people with healthy BMIs using AI and basic photo editing to obtain weight-loss jabs; the reporter widened her own body in an image and, within a week, three separate companies approved her and dispatched prescriptions, sometimes on a single edited image (ITV News, 2026-01-02). Market context only; the figure is cited as reporting, never as a claim about any target company.
2. **The regulator has said photo-only checks fail, and is acting.** Responding to the ITV report, the GPhC stated that "having one photo by itself is not going to be sufficient" and said it is taking action against online pharmacies that failed to carry out full and appropriate checks when prescribing weight-loss medication (Chemist+Druggist, January 2026; GPhC statement on the ITV News report). This sits on top of the February 2025 GPhC distance-selling guidance, which already required prescribers to independently verify a person's weight, height and/or BMI and said an online questionnaire alone is insufficient. Pharmacies now fail inspection if they do not independently verify these. The pressure is on the eligibility step this product sits in.
3. **FitXpress closes that gap the way these buyers want, and the proof is already published.** From the live guide and `proof-points.md`: a guided two-photo capture through the SDK with the camera-roll picker disabled; liveness and pose/clothing checks; a Smart Scales predicted weight the pharmacy compares with the self-reported figure against its own mismatch threshold; BMI from supplied height and predicted weight; 80+ body measurements; structured, timestamped session and validation records; "under 45 seconds from the photos to structured results"; and a server-side (Pattern B) option where the patient does not see the body metrics. It does not diagnose or make the eligibility decision; the pharmacy's qualified team does. Compliance fits UK buyers: FitXpress is not a medical device under UK MDR for its current intended purpose; the GDPR controller/processor roles, a DPA with SCCs and a UK Addendum (`compliance.md`). **Limits, stated honestly:** no pharmacy is named as a reference; the Smart Scales figure is a software estimate, not a scale reading; hosting is AWS US-West-2 (and part US-East-1), with no EU/UK data-residency offer; identity verification and height are separate controls the scan does not settle; performance for people with physical disabilities has not been established.

**Why now (UK):** the ITV report broke on 2 January 2026, the GPhC responded in the same week, and GPhC inspection now fails pharmacies that do not independently verify weight/height/BMI. Buyers are rebuilding this exact step this year, and the congress on 23-24 November is a natural place to talk about it in person.

## Target buyer persona

- **Titles that buy / decide:**
  - **Superintendent Pharmacist / Responsible Pharmacist** — the GPhC-accountable role for a registered online pharmacy; owns whether the eligibility check passes inspection.
  - **Head of Compliance & Risk / Head of Clinical Governance** — owns the defensibility of the intake workflow and the audit evidence.
  - **Chief Medical Officer / Medical Director / Clinical Operations Director** — owns the prescribing protocol and what an estimated value may be used for.
  - **Founder / CEO** — at smaller prescriber platforms, decides whether to add the step.
  - **Head of Product / Product Manager** — owns where the step sits in the order flow and checkout.
- **Why these titles, not others:** in a UK registered online pharmacy the superintendent pharmacist carries regulatory accountability for the service, so this is the one title a generic health-tech list misses and the one that matters most here. Compliance, clinical-governance and medical leaders own the evidence process and the protocol; the CEO decides at small platforms; product owns the checkout placement. Growth, performance-marketing, dispensing-only and logistics roles do not own the eligibility decision and are not pulled. Pure engineers go to the `technical-integration` lane, not the primary pull.
- **What they care about (KPIs):** GPhC inspection outcome and enforcement risk; integrity of the eligibility screen; manual image-review burden and cost per qualified prescription; regulator complaint rate; checkout completion / drop-off; audit-readiness of the record.
- **Objections they likely have, with the honest answer:**
  - "An estimate is not a measured weight." True. Smart Scales is a software estimate at about 3.5% mean absolute error under evaluated conditions, not a scale reading. The pharmacy sets the mismatch threshold around the model's expected error and reviews flagged cases; it does not auto-decide.
  - "Will it add friction and drop-off?" Under 45 seconds from the photos to structured results; the pharmacy chooses where the step sits, and an evaluation measures completion and retake rates.
  - "Where is the data, and is it UK-resident?" AWS, primarily US-West-2 and partly US-East-1; no EU/UK residency is offered. GDPR controller/processor roles, a DPA with SCCs and a UK Addendum. Documents through legal@3dlook.me.
  - "We already ask for a photo next to a scale / a video." Those help; this makes the body-data capture itself in-session, with a predicted-weight cross-check and a structured record, rather than another image to review by eye.
  - "Is this a medical device / does it decide eligibility?" FitXpress is not a medical device and does not make the eligibility decision; the pharmacy's clinicians do.
- **Not the buyer (held for Vadim or FAIL):** growth / performance marketing; general customer support; dispensing or logistics pharmacists with no governance role; data/ML engineers and infrastructure (SysAdmin, DevOps) → FAIL; CTO / VP Engineering → `technical-integration`, not primary. Anyone located outside the UK whose service does not prescribe into the UK under GPhC.
