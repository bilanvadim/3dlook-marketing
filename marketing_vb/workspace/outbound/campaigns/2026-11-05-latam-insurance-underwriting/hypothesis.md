## Use case (1 sentence)

A Brazilian or Mexican life insurer adds a two-photo step to the life applications it decides on what the applicant states (the health declaration, or a tele-interview above the declaration limit), and FitXpress flags any gap between declared and estimated weight that crosses the insurer's own threshold, as structured height-and-weight evidence on cases where nobody measures the applicant.

## Why this is plausible (3 reasons grounded in evidence)

1. **Insurers in both markets accept individual life on what the applicant states, up to a limit, and the forms seen ask height and weight.** No source gives the share of policies decided this way in either market. Limits seen: Icatu, the DPS up to R$3 million, with the DPS or a tele-interview chosen by accumulated sum (2024 article); Azos, R$5 million with no exams (July 2026); Banorte, MXN 4.5 million with no medical exam, depending on age (read through search on 2026-11-05). Forms seen: Bradesco's mortgage-life DPS (11/2025); Allianz Brasil's complementary health and financial declaration (12/2022, path to confirm); a bank's mortgage DPS naming Itaú Seguros and Tokio Marine (03/2013); Icatu's DPS (blog); AXA México's application. A tele-interview leaves the same thing on file: height and weight as the applicant states them. Growth puts more cases on these paths: Brazil individual life +13.6% (January to November 2025), Mexico individual policies +9.7% (2025).
2. **Self-reported height and weight are close on average and drift where it matters.** Brazil: the validation inside the 2019 national health survey (6,381 adults with both measures) supports self-report for surveillance, and women were more likely to understate weight and overstate height (Brito and Oliveira, Rev Saúde Pública, July 2024); on 2013 survey data (40,366 adults) agreement was high and the authors advise caution (Moreira et al., Cad Saúde Pública 2018); obesity among women came out lower on self-report than on measurement (Fiocruz thesis comparing the 2013 and 2019 surveys). Mexico: self-reported weight understated overweight and obesity among women of reproductive age (PLoS One 2020); among Mexican and Puerto Rican men, misreporting grew with measured BMI (Am J Men's Health 2021). Prevalence: 36.9% of Mexican adults live with obesity on measurement (ENSANUT 2022); 25.7% in Brazil's state capitals on self-report (Vigitel, 2024 data). Market context only, never copy.
3. **FitXpress adds evidence, the insurer decides, and the evaluation path is ready.** Outputs and citable numbers: Rules (from `proof-points.md` and the landing). Pilot path (landing): walkthrough, shadow evaluation with outputs kept out of decisions, comparison with independently collected evidence on criteria agreed in advance; it counts completion and retake rates, agreement with reference evidence, flags per candidate threshold and the effect on review volume. Data facts (`compliance.md`): AWS, primarily US-West-2, partly US-East-1; TLS in transit, SSE-S3 at rest; photos deleted after processing or within 30 days; does not make underwriting decisions. **Limits:** no insurer reference and no Latin American customer; not a calibrated scale; overstated height not caught; validated with US and European participants ("Locations: US, Europe", `proof-points.md`), so performance on Brazilian and Mexican populations has not been characterized separately; performance for people with physical disabilities not established; no lab, blood pressure or tobacco values; no approved Portuguese or Spanish wording; data hosted in the US.

**What the flag can separate from estimate error (the Smart Scales honesty point, as in the twins).** At about 3.5% mean absolute error the typical estimate error is about 2.5 kg at 70 kg and 3.5 kg at 100 kg (arithmetic on the product figure). The Brazilian and Mexican studies above find average self-report gaps small, inside that error: the flag does not correct a typical applicant's weight and is never sold that way. It can refer the larger understatements, which the studies place among higher-BMI applicants and women. `proof-points.md` publishes only the mean error, not its spread, its behaviour by BMI band or any Latin American figure, so how many accurately declared applicants a given threshold would flag is unknown. The shadow evaluation measures it (Risk 4).

**Why now:** individual life grows at double digits in Brazil and near them in Mexico; insurers automate declaration cases (MetLife Brasil, MAG, Azos's 40% target); Brazil's new contract law (in force December 2025) puts the insurer's questionnaire at the centre of risk declaration. Buyer context only.

## Target buyer persona

**Who buys:** the owner of the declaration rules and of how height and weight are rated. At an insurer that is the head of underwriting (Diretor or Superintendente de Subscrição, Director de Suscripción, Director de Selección de Riesgos, Chief Underwriting Officer), the underwriting and acceptance managers who run the DPS and tele-interview routes, the medical director who decides what an estimate may be used for (Diretor Médico, Consultoria Médica, Director Médico, the head of medical underwriting or dictaminación médica), and the individual life, persons-insurance, product and actuarial heads who place the step in the application. In bancassurers, the life product heads. At reinsurers, the Latin American L&H underwriting, medical and client-underwriting heads. At Azos and Klimber, the founders and the heads of product and underwriting.

- **Why these titles, not others:** underwriting heads own the declaration limits and the rules that send a case to an exam; medical directors sign off on non-measured inputs; product, actuarial and individual life heads own the application and the extra premiums tied to height and weight. In Brazilian insurers the Diretor Técnico often owns underwriting and actuarial work: pulled, lane by scope. From the other §3 titles, innovation leads are pulled (P2 `proposition` with visible life scope, else P3 `referral`); CRO, population health, compliance and fraud-prevention teams are not (they do not own a case's evidence, and fraud framing is banned in copy). A bare product title at a multi-line insurer returns auto, home and health owners; life scope must be visible.
- **What they care about (KPIs):** share of cases accepted on the declaration or a tele-interview, with no exam; time to issue; evidence cost per case (medical exams, tele-interviews, lab tests); drop-off between quote and issue, with brokers, in bank branches and online; consistent, documented acceptance decisions; claims experience on declaration cases (never in copy).
- **Objections they will likely raise, with the honest answer:**
  - An estimate is not a weighing, and the typical gap is inside your error. True: Smart Scales is a software estimate at about 3.5% mean absolute error under evaluated conditions. The flag refers only gaps well above that, on the insurer's threshold; the rest stay on the declaration path.
  - Was it validated on our population? Validation participants were from the US and Europe; Brazilian and Mexican populations have not been characterized separately. A shadow evaluation on the insurer's own applicants measures it.
  - How many cases would that catch? Unknown until an evaluation on cases the insurer measures counts flags per threshold against the measured weight.
  - We would never accept or decline on photos. FitXpress does not make underwriting decisions; the insurer's rules and underwriters do.
  - Brokers and branch staff will not add a step. Under 45 seconds from the photos to structured results; the insurer decides where it is offered; the evaluation measures completion and retakes.
  - Where is the data, and what about LGPD or the Mexican data law? AWS, primarily US-West-2, partly US-East-1; photo handling as in `compliance.md`. No approved Brazilian or Mexican legal wording exists; these questions go to legal@3dlook.me (Open question 5).

## Anti-cases (where it does NOT work)

- **Group life, employee benefits and collective accident cover** (vida em grupo, seguro de vida grupo, colectivo): members are accepted within free-cover limits without individual height and weight.
- **Credit life and prestamista below the free-cover limit:** no individual DPS. Only cases where each borrower fills a DPS are in.
- **Health insurance and health plans** (Bradesco Saúde, SulAmérica Saúde, Gastos Médicos Mayores lines): a different use case; Brazilian health plans may not require a medical evaluation at enrolment (Idec).
- **Embedded micro-life, funeral and accident-only products** sold in apps, retail and payments: few or no health questions.
- **Pensions, savings and annuities** (previdência privada, rentas vitalicias).
- **Brokers, comparators and agencies that do not own the decision.** Azos and Klimber are in only because they run the decision journey.
- **Exam, lab and tele-interview vendors:** a later partner conversation.
