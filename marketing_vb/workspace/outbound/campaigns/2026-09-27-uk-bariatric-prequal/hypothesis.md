## Vertical

**UK private obesity-care providers that run a surgical (bariatric / metabolic) pathway**, i.e.
organisations that must decide, before a surgeon's consultation, whether a self-pay or
PMI-funded patient is worth a consult slot.

## Sub-segment

UK-HQ providers where the decision "is this patient a surgical candidate?" is made inside the
organisation, with a remote enquiry-to-consult funnel. Three flavours, in priority order:

1. **Specialist bariatric surgery groups and clinic networks.** Private weight-loss-surgery
   brands operating across several cities, usually through partner hospitals (the Healthier
   Weight / Tonic shape, both already contacted, see Anti-cases). Signals: online BMI or
   eligibility checker, free or virtual first consultation, finance plans, a multi-year
   aftercare programme, a patient app.
2. **Hybrid surgical + medical obesity clinics.** Consultant-led private clinics offering
   surgery *and* endoscopic options (balloon, ESG) *and* GLP-1 programmes under one intake.
   This is the flavour the article's GLP-1 section speaks to most directly: patients arrive
   already on medication, with a BMI history that has moved.
3. **Independent hospital groups with a named weight-loss-surgery service line.** Spire
   Healthcare, Nuffield Health, Circle Health Group all publicly market private gastric sleeve
   surgery ([Spire](https://www.spirehealthcare.com/treatments/weight-loss/gastric-sleeve-surgery-sleeve-gastrectomy/),
   [Nuffield](https://www.nuffieldhealth.com/hospitals/leeds/weight-loss-surgery),
   [Circle](https://www.circlehealthgroup.co.uk/treatments/gastric-sleeve-sleeve-gastrectomy));
   also check HCA Healthcare UK, Ramsay Health Care UK, Practice Plus Group, The London
   Clinic, Cleveland Clinic London. Enterprise, slow, but the only accounts with volume. We sell
   to the **service line / self-pay pathway**, not to the hospital group as a whole.

**Qualifying criteria (step 2 applies these):**

- **Geo:** UK-HQ, whole UK (England, Scotland, Wales, NI). UK-HQ = the surgical pathway is run
  from the UK. Clinics abroad that sell to UK patients are out (see Anti-cases).
- **Size:** revenue $2M+ (use-case baseline) **or** ≥3 sites / partner hospitals. **Headcount
  floor 10**, not 50: specialist bariatric brands are small by headcount and large by ticket
  (a web-search summary put private gastric sleeve surgery at £7,000-11,000 per procedure;
  the provider page behind that figure was not identified, so it is **unverified** and stays
  out of copy). This is a **campaign-level deviation**
  from the `icp-detail.md` universal "<50 employees" exclusion, the same kind Vadim granted
  on 2026-09-12 for erakulis-similar. Needs his OK (Open question 2).
- **Must have:** a live surgical weight-loss offer (not cosmetic-only, not medication-only),
  and a remote first step (web eligibility form, BMI checker, virtual consult).
- **Nice to have:** multi-year aftercare programme or patient app (the article's second half,
  progress tracking, lands there), PMI acceptance, finance partner.

## Use case (1 sentence)

Before a surgeon's consultation, the provider sends the enquirer a scan link; a two-photo
smartphone capture returns a dated, structured body-data record (predicted weight from Smart
Scales, a BMI comparison against self-report, 80+ body measurements, body composition
estimates, capture-quality flags), so the intake team can screen against its own criteria,
the consultation starts from data, and the same record becomes the pre-op baseline for
post-op progress review.

## Why this is plausible (grounded in evidence)

1. **UK eligibility is a BMI gate with more branches than it used to have.** NICE NG246
   (Overweight and obesity management) offers surgical assessment at BMI ≥40, or 35-39.9 with a
   significant condition; it lowers both thresholds by 2.5 kg/m² for people of South Asian,
   Chinese, other Asian, Middle Eastern, Black African or African-Caribbean background, and adds
   expedited assessment at BMI ≥35 with recent-onset type 2 diabetes
   ([NICE NG246, Medicines and surgery](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng246/chapter/Medicines-and-surgery)).
   Private PMI cover follows similar gates: Bupa is reported to consider bariatric cover at BMI
   40+ or 35+ with comorbidities, after failed non-surgical attempts and an MDT recommendation
   ([secondary summary, bismaz.co.uk](https://bismaz.co.uk/bupa-weight-loss-surgery-cost-uk/);
   confirm against Bupa's own policy wording before using). Every branch starts with a dated
   BMI whose source is known. That is precisely what the article's Stage 1-2 workflow
   produces, and what a self-reported web form does not.
2. **GLP-1s changed who arrives at the surgical front door, and with what BMI history.**
   More than 2 million people in the UK were paying privately for Mounjaro / Wegovy by July
   2025 per an IQVIA estimate reported by
   [heySlim](https://www.heyslim.co.uk/blog/uk-weight-loss-medication-statistics-2026)
   (secondary source; find the IQVIA original before quoting). The article already carries the
   clinical side: an ASMBS-reported conference study of 6,700+ patients who used GLP-1s before
   surgery lost about 8% of body weight pre-procedure (US data, cited in the live article), so
   "a patient may arrive at bariatric intake with a current BMI below an earlier documented
   value". The flip side for providers: one UK pharmacy guide claims private bariatric surgeries
   fell by around four-fifths as non-surgical options grew
   ([chemist-4-u](https://www.chemist-4-u.com/guides/weight-loss/weight-loss-statistics/)).
   **Unverified, weak source, do not use in copy.** If it is even directionally right,
   surviving private providers are fighting for fewer, higher-intent enquirers, which makes
   "stop spending surgeon time on enquirers who don't qualify" a sharper pain, not a weaker one.
3. **Consultation capacity is the scarce resource, and it leaks to abroad.** The Royal College
   of Surgeons Bulletin reports about 5,000 UK patients had bariatric surgery overseas in 2024,
   against about 4,500 treated annually by the NHS
   ([RCS Bulletin 2024;106(6), Aggarwal & Ahmed](https://publishing.rcseng.ac.uk/doi/10.1308/rcsbull.2024.105);
   figures as summarised in search, verify in the full text). Packages abroad are marketed at
   £3,000-5,000 ([search summary of UK pharmacy guide](https://www.boltpharmacy.co.uk/guide/bariatric-surgery-in-turkey),
   unverified). A UK private clinic competing on safety and aftercare cannot compete on price,
   so it competes on the speed and quality of its first response. A remote, structured
   pre-qualification step is a speed lever that does not cost surgeon time.
4. **The UK NHS pool is ~3/4 of historic volume and is not our buyer, which keeps this
   campaign clean.** NBSR data for 2015-2019: 34,580 primary bariatric procedures, 75.9% NHS
   funded ([Bolckmans et al., Clinical Obesity 2023](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/cob.12585)).
   The remaining quarter (self-pay / PMI) is where a vendor can be bought by one decision-maker
   in weeks. We aim only at that quarter and say so.
5. **Our product covers the workflow the article describes, and nothing more.** Outputs, the
   Smart Scales self-report comparison, capture-quality flags, validation population (16-78 yrs,
   38-210 kg) and the limits ("FitXpress does not diagnose conditions, make clinical decisions,
   or determine treatment eligibility", "FitXpress is not a medical device.") are all in the
   live article and in `accuracy-formulations.md` / `compliance.md`. No UK bariatric reference
   customer exists; the nearest UK proof is an online-pharmacy BMI-verification deployment
   (see Message angle, proof rule).

**Likely objections:**
- "Our MDT weighs every patient in clinic anyway." → Answer: the scan does not replace the
  clinic weigh-in (article FAQ: calibrated scales remain the method of record where required);
  it moves the first usable record before the consult, and flags self-report mismatches.
- "Patients at BMI 40+ struggle with a standing capture." → Real. The article names it as a
  pilot question (accessibility, fallback, population outside the 38-210 kg validation range).
  Say so; do not hide it.
- "Is this a medical device / is health data leaving the UK?" → "FitXpress is not a medical
  device." + GDPR controller/processor sentence + DPA with SCCs and UK Addendum. Storage is AWS
  US regions (`compliance.md` §10): expect this to be the first hard question.
- "Enquiries are down, why add a step?" → Frame the scan as the eligibility checker they
  already run on their website, with a verified weight instead of a typed one.

## Risks

1. **Pool too small** (most likely failure). Mitigated by the floor + expansion A.
2. **Shrinking private surgical demand** (if the 80% claim is anywhere near true) means some
   listed clinics are winding down surgery or pivoting to GLP-1 only. Step 2 must check each
   provider still actively books surgery (live pricing / booking page).
3. **Accessibility at high BMI.** Standing two-photo capture for BMI 45-60 patients is a real
   question; validation population tops out at 210 kg. A surgeon will ask on the first call.
4. **Article mismatch.** The asset is US-framed in its middle section; a UK reader may bounce
   off CMS-0057-F. Consider a UK note or one-pager before send (Open question 7).
5. **Data residency.** Special-category health data stored in AWS US regions; UK providers with
   NHS-adjacent governance may block on it.
6. **No bariatric reference customer.** Proof comes from adjacent use cases only.
