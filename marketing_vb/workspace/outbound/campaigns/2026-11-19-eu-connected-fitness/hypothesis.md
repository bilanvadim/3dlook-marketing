## Use case (1 sentence)

A Continental European club operator, or the club-software vendor behind its white-label app, adds a FitXpress scan to the progress journey its member app already runs, at onboarding and at each progress check-in (an assessment, a training-plan review, a progress log), giving members 80+ body measurements and body composition estimates from their own phone between club visits, and giving head office one timestamped record per scan through the API, under its own brand.

## Target buyer persona

**Who buys:** the head-office owner of the member app and its progress journey. At club operators that is the CPO, Chief Digital Officer, Head or Director of Product, Head of Digital or Head of App. Next to them sit the Product Owners or senior product managers who own the app's progress and onboarding features, and the Head of Member Experience where that role owns the journey. At club-software vendors (group B) it is the founder or CEO, CPO or Head of Partnerships. Where a club's app is white-label from a vendor (`app_build=`), head office may have no product owner; the head of digital or the training lead is then the route in.

**Who routes (PPL-1, PPL-4):** the head-office training lead (Head of Training, Head of Fitness, Fitness Director, Head of Personal Training, Head of Coaching). They decide what a trainer reviews with a member, and the page's coach line ("the coach reviews the same record before setting the next program block") is their lane. Every IN account gets at least one person below C-level when the export has one.

- **Why these titles, not others:** the product decision is "a new step in our app", which the member-app owner makes. Training leads are the practitioners, the function that produced most positive replies in `learnings.md` (PPL-4). Partnerships and BD replied most often of any function (PPL-3) and carry group B; at club operators they sell corporate memberships, and go to `referral`, never `partnership`.
- **What they care about:** app engagement and repeat progress check-ins; onboarding completion; membership retention (Basic-Fit tracks average length of stay); upgrades to a premium tier; what a trainer has to work with between sessions; no hardware rollout across clubs; data ownership in their own brand.
- **Likely objections:**
  - "We already have a device in the clubs." Answer: it stays; the phone scan covers the progress check-ins between visits, in the company's app (page).
  - "A scan step will cut onboarding completion." Answer: it can; the product team chooses where it sits, and the pilot compares onboarding completion with the usual flow (page FAQ).
  - "We could build it." Answer: a pre-trained model through SDK and API, with no computer-vision team to hire.
  - "Our members sign for a year, so day-30 retention tells us nothing." Answer: the pilot reads what the term allows, activity in the app's progress journey and repeat progress check-ins at day 30 and day 90; membership retention only where members can leave monthly.
  - "Where is the data hosted?" Answer from `compliance.md` §10 on reply, never in cold copy.
  - "Is this medical?" Answer: "FitXpress is not a medical device."
- **CTO / Head of Engineering / Head of Mobile:** `technical-integration`, at most two per company, never the only person at an account (engineering 1/90, PPL-3).
- **C-level at groups over ~500 staff (CEO, CFO, COO):** `referral`, never P1 for title alone (PPL-1).
- **Not the buyer:** marketing, brand, content, social and PR; CRM, retention and lifecycle managers (0/44 by function, PPL-3; HYP-6); user acquisition; membership sales; club managers, franchise unit owners and individual trainers or instructors; operations and facilities; finance below the CFO; HR; legal and privacy counsel; customer service; engineers below lead; investors and board members.

## Message angle: lanes and account facts

Every person gets exactly one lane. Lane notes are instructions to the writer; none of their sentences goes into a message.

**The angle, for every lane (a target; each writer finds new words for each person):** how body progress is checked today at this company (self-reported weight, unmeasured photos, a device in some clubs, a trainer's assessment) and what a phone scan adds to the progress check-ins its own app already runs between club visits: what comes back and who sees it. Facts about the company come only from Account facts.

**Lanes:**
- **`member-app`** Target: the progress journey inside the company's app (onboarding, assessments, training plans, a progress log) and the place a body scan would take in it. Question targets: body progress in the app between club visits today; scan placement within the member journey; judging a new step's effect on onboarding completion; the paid tier or higher membership that 3D progress would belong to. Message 2 value: the pilot design in its parts (Rules), with retention as the measured outcome under the row's `term=`, never a promised one; the HYP-6 read counts this lane's replies after Message 2.
- **`training`** Target: what a trainer or coach has to work with when reviewing a member's progress. Question targets: trainer reviews of body change between sessions; contents of a scan record before the next programme block; division of work between a club device and a phone scan for remote progress check-ins. Message 2 value: one timestamped record shared by coach and member, with the company choosing what each one sees.
- **`partnership`** Target (group B only; partnerships and BD heads at club operators go to `referral`): one integration the vendor's client clubs or studios could switch on in their white-label member app. Question targets: the route by which client clubs get new features into their branded app; requests from client clubs beyond booking and access; packaging of add-on features for clubs on the platform. Message 2 value: web and mobile SDKs and the API, one record per scan back to the platform, under each club's brand; the pilot design in its parts for one client-club cohort.
- **`technical-integration`** Target: vendor review and integration, technical tone. Question targets: entry of a new SDK step into the app's onboarding; the path of a structured record to the member profile; review of a new data processor. Message 2 value: SDK with API as the recommended setup (pose validation runs in the SDK's capture layer), the GDPR sentence, photo handling and random identifiers in `compliance.md` forms; documents through legal@3dlook.me.
- **`referral`** Target: what the scan is and why it matters to this company's member app, then one question about the person's own work. Question targets: the person's own work where it touches the member app, built from their title. Message 2 value: a line that continues their question, the call offer with the calendar link, and an easy out pointing to one of five targets: the member-app owner; whoever runs the app's onboarding; the head-office training lead; the owner of the digital roadmap; whoever reviews new app vendors. Each easy-out target goes to at most about a fifth of a company's people, worded fresh for each person.

**Question discipline at one company.** Each person's question is built from their own title and worded for them alone; no wording, hook, structure or closer repeats at one company. Pain topics, value points and easy-out targets follow the one-fifth rule (Rules, Several people at one company).
