## Use case (1 sentence)

A UK pharmacy or online prescriber that sells GLP-1 medication replaces or backs up its current weight check (a camera-roll photo, a video of scales, a video call or a self-entered weight) with a live, in-session FitXpress two-photo capture that compares a Smart Scales weight estimate with the declared weight against the pharmacy's own threshold and leaves a timestamped structured record for clinical review; for in-branch services, the same capture is a remote option between visits.

## Why this is plausible (3 reasons grounded in evidence)

1. **The regulator expects independent verification.** The GPhC's February 2025 distance-selling guidance requires prescribers to verify independently a person's weight, height and/or BMI, and says an online questionnaire alone is not enough (GPhC, https://www.pharmacyregulation.org/about-us/news-and-updates/online-pharmacies-strengthen-safeguards-prevent-unsafe-supply-medicines).
2. **One photo is not enough, said the GPhC in January 2026.** After online pharmacies were reported to have approved GLP-1s on AI-edited photos, a GPhC spokesperson said "having one photo by itself is not going to be sufficient" (Chemist+Druggist, 2026-01-05, https://www.chemistanddruggist.co.uk/news/regulation/gphc-act-after-online-pharmacies-accept-ai-images-for-glp-1s-FADENMKWDNC63IKNMHP7IE7CNE/; GPhC statement, https://www.pharmacyregulation.org/about-us/news-and-updates/gphc-responds-itv-news-report-about-inappropriate-supply-medicines-used-weight-management). Context for this hypothesis only: copy uses the GPhC line, never the press reporting behind it (Rules).
3. **The shortlist's own pages show the step FitXpress fits, and the product covers it.** The 2026-11-17 research found how the IN companies check weight today: photo uploads (Superdrug Online Doctor, Asda Online Doctor, IQ Doctor, MedExpress, Weightless, PrivateDoc's three photos, Numan's in-app photos, Medicspot's full-body photo with manual review of edited images), a video of the patient on scales (PillSorted, Pharmulous, Oushk), video calls (Well, Assured, LIVVE, Rowlands, NowPatient, Rightdose) and a self-entered weight in an online form (St Helens, Chigwell, Kamsons, Prescription Doctor, Everslim). Each is a manual review of something the patient supplies. From the live guide and `proof-points.md`: a guided two-photo capture through the SDK with the camera-roll picker disabled; Real-Time Pose Validation (RTPV), which gives pose and framing guidance during capture; a Smart Scales predicted weight the pharmacy compares with the declared figure against its own threshold; BMI from supplied height and predicted weight; 80+ body measurements; structured, timestamped session records; under 45 seconds from the photos to structured results; a server-side option where the patient does not see the body metrics. It does not make the eligibility decision. FitXpress is not a medical device under UK MDR for its current intended purpose; GDPR controller and processor roles, a DPA with SCCs and a UK Addendum (`compliance.md`). **Limits, stated honestly:** no pharmacy is named as a reference; Smart Scales is a software estimate, not a scale reading, and no weight-accuracy number may be published until R&D publishes the baseline (`roadmap.md`); live human validation is ready for release but not live, and clothing detection is planned (`roadmap.md`), so neither is described in copy; hosting is AWS US-West-2 (partly US-East-1), with no UK data-residency offer; identity and height are separate controls the scan does not settle; performance for people with physical disabilities has not been established.
