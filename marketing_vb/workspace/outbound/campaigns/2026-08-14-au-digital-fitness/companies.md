# Target Companies, Australian Digital Fitness and Connected Health (2026-08-14)

> Sub-segment (from `hypothesis.md`): Australia-headquartered digital fitness, connected health and AI-nutrition companies running a recurring digital relationship with users, already collecting some activity or body data, with no phone-camera body-composition layer. Screen: at least 2 of 4 criteria verified (C1 AI food scanner, C2 mobile body-comp scan, C3 wearable integrations, C4 adaptive AI coaching), or 1 criterion plus a documented multi-pillar loop or existing body-measurement business.

## Executive summary

**26 companies delivered against a request for up to 30.** That is a real ceiling rather than a research shortfall: the Australian universe of digital fitness and health companies with a genuine data layer is finite, and roughly a dozen otherwise-good candidates were removed on hard rules (competitor, existing customer, prior-campaign overlap, offshore head office, product shutting down). Every exclusion is listed by name at the bottom with its reason.

**Tier split.** 10 companies (AUFIT-01 to AUFIT-10) have two or more of the four ICP criteria verified from primary sources. 16 (AUFIT-11 to AUFIT-26) match one criterion plus a documented multi-pillar loop or an existing body-measurement business, so they are a build-the-case conversation rather than a drop-in fit.

**The single clearest finding.** Australian platforms have already built the wearable and scale side of the Erakulis loop and stopped at body measurement. Juniper (Eucalyptus) ships a Bluetooth scale with the first delivery, syncs Apple, Fitbit and Garmin data, and then asks 100,000+ Australian women to measure their waist by hand. AIA Vitality accepts eight named device and app sources and pays points on steps, workouts, heart rate, mindful minutes and sleep, with no body-composition signal in the set. Everlab integrates 30+ wearables and buys clinical bone-density and DEXA scanning at facility cost. Those are three different buyer types with the same hole in the middle.

**The second finding, which shapes the pitch.** No Australian company in this search has a photo-based AI food scanner (C1), and none has phone-camera body composition (C2) unless body scanning is their own product, which makes them competitors rather than prospects (Advanced Health Intelligence, mPort). So C1 and C2 are effectively empty across the market: the Australian list competes on C3 and C4 only. The upside is that a scan is genuinely differentiating here, not table stakes. The downside is that nobody in this market has pre-validated the feature for their users, so every conversation carries an education load.

**Segment split.** Consumer subscription apps (9: Centr, Sweat, Kic, 28 by Sam Wood, Emily Skye FIT, 12WBT, Transform by Fitaz, The Fast 800, Defeat Diabetes). Metabolic, longevity and clinical-adjacent platforms (3: Vively, Everlab, Eucalyptus). Nutrition programs and software (2: Digital Wellness, Xyris). Connected fitness hardware (1: Vitruvian). B2B fitness and health software (4: Hapana, VALD, Catapult, dorsaVi). Corporate wellbeing and insurer-linked wellness (4: AIA Australia, Springday, Sleepfit, Sonder). Franchise and club networks with a real digital layer (2: Fernwood, Fitstop). Engagement and adherence platforms (1: Perx Health).

**Two rows carry a geography flag.** Centr (Melbourne origin, US private-equity owner, US workforce centralisation reported) and Perx Health (Sydney origin, New York head office listed). Both are Australian by history and operations, neither is cleanly Australian by head office. Kept and flagged rather than dropped quietly, per the open question in `hypothesis.md`.

## Tier A: two or more criteria verified

### 01. Eucalyptus Health (Juniper, Pilot, Compound, Kin)
- **Website:** https://www.eucalyptus.health
- **What they do:** Group of digital healthcare clinics. Juniper is the women's weight-management brand (medication plus practitioner support plus app), Pilot is men's health, Compound is metabolic, Kin is reproductive health. 550,000+ patients across brands, 100,000+ in Juniper Australia, 400+ employees globally, founded 2019, Sydney.
- **Why they fit the Erakulis ICP:** The Juniper app already connects to Apple, Fitbit and Garmin for activity, heart rate and blood glucose, ships a Bluetooth digital scale with the first medication delivery, and asks patients to record waist measurements manually into a progress graph that the clinical team reviews. That is a connected program with a tape measure at the centre of it. A 2-photo scan replaces the manual step, adds composition (fat versus lean mass, which is the live clinical question in medication-assisted weight loss), and gives the clinical team a repeatable series instead of self-reported numbers.
- **Key metrics:** 100,000+ Australian Juniper members, 90% of patients reported positive weight-loss outcomes (company figure), 200,000+ Juniper patients globally, Good Design Award (gold) for the Juniper app.
- **Data handling:** Australian Privacy Principles plus UK and EU rules through its offshore operations, so GDPR is in scope. Handles prescription and clinical data, so procurement will ask where photos are processed and how long they persist. No public ISO or SOC 2 certification found.
- **Sources:** https://www.eucalyptus.health/blog/wearables, https://www.eucalyptus.health/, https://www.myjuniper.com/program

### 02. Vively (Vively Health Pty Ltd)
- **Website:** https://www.vively.com.au
- **What they do:** Metabolic health platform combining continuous glucose monitoring (14-day sensors), food and lifestyle logging, blood testing (70+ markers), biological age, and dietitian and nurse support. Founded 2021 by Michael Pang and Tim Veron, North Sydney. Also operates a New Zealand site and a practitioner-facing platform.
- **Why they fit:** The app pulls sleep, exercise and stress data from Apple Watch, Garmin, Fitbit and Google Fit, and combines it with glucose and food logs into personalised guidance. Body composition is the only major input missing, and it is the one that explains whether a member's improved glucose curve came with fat loss or muscle loss. Their own content already ranks weight-management apps and argues for connected tracking, so the product thesis needs no education.
- **Key metrics:** 30,000+ members, 4,000+ testing locations, $99 baseline health check, pre-seed of $1.5M (Dec 2022, Archangel Ventures), research program with Digital Health CRC.
- **Data handling:** States it is a general wellness platform and does not provide medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. Handles CGM and pathology data under the Australian Privacy Principles. No public certification found; expect security review before any API work.
- **Sources:** https://www.vively.com.au/, https://www.vively.com.au/cgm-for-fitness, https://www.forbes.com.au/news/innovation/diabetes-is-reversible-vively-raises-1-6-million/

### 03. Everlab

## Coverage analysis

### Segments covered
- **Consumer subscription apps (9 companies).** Strong coverage of the Australian DTC market across the multi-pillar (Centr, Kic), women's fitness (Sweat, Emily Skye FIT, Transform by Fitaz), general transformation (28 by Sam Wood, 12WBT) and weight or metabolic program (The Fast 800, Defeat Diabetes) segments. This is close to exhaustive for apps of meaningful size.
- **Metabolic, longevity and clinical-adjacent platforms (3).** Vively, Everlab and Eucalyptus are the three Australian companies with the deepest connected data loops. Complete for their size band.
- **B2B fitness and health software (4).** Hapana (studio and gym platform), VALD (measurement hardware and apps), Catapult (elite sport), dorsaVi (movement measurement). This is where the reach-per-integration is highest.
- **Corporate wellbeing and insurer-linked wellness (4).** AIA Australia, Springday, Sleepfit, Sonder. Constrained by the prior telehealth campaigns having already worked the health funds.
- **Club and franchise networks with a real digital layer (2).** Fernwood and Fitstop, both admitted as documented exceptions to the gym-chain exclusion.
- **Nutrition data and programs (2).** Xyris and Digital Wellness cover the Australian nutrition-software and licensed-program layer.

### Gaps that remain
1. **No Australian AI food scanner (C1 empty).** Every photo-recognition meal-logging app found in this research is non-Australian (Cal AI, SnapCalorie, Calorie Mama, Welling and similar). Australian food logging runs on barcodes and the Australian food database (Xyris, CalorieKing). So there is no local company to point to as a precedent for AI-driven capture, and no Australian competitor occupying that space either.
2. **No Australian consumer app with phone-camera body composition (C2 empty by design).** The only Australian companies doing image-based body measurement are competitors: Advanced Health Intelligence (Perth) and mPort (Sydney). This is the product opportunity and the competitive risk in the same sentence: Advanced Health Intelligence already sells a smartphone BodyScan into fitness apps, so several targets may have been approached by them already. Worth asking early in each conversation.
3. **No Australian AI assistant in fitness.** Nothing local matches the AI personal assistant part of the Erakulis stack. The closest are Digital Wellness's coach (Hope), Everlab's AI care layer and Perx's engagement algorithms. Expect to explain the concept rather than reference a local peer.
4. **Insurer and health-fund segment is thin by prior coverage, not by market size.** Medibank, Bupa and HCF are the largest wellbeing programs in Australia and all three are already worked. AIA Australia is the main untouched large program; nib was reviewed and left out because no wearable integration or personalised program could be verified from public sources.
5. **Sleep and recovery is a one-company segment.** Sleepfit is the only Australian sleep-health platform found at meaningful scale. Compumedics and similar are diagnostic hardware, which is out of scope.
6. **Men's health and men's fitness apps are absent as standalone targets.** The Australian men's segment sits inside telehealth groups (Pilot within Eucalyptus, Stagger within Midnight Health, Mosh already worked), not in standalone fitness apps.
7. **Youth, teen and over-60s fitness are absent.** No Australian company of relevant size was found in either.
8. **New Zealand is excluded by geography but is the obvious next list.** Femmi (women's running, cycle-aligned adaptive plans) and Les Mills (Auckland, Les Mills+ digital) both fit the product ICP and both fail only the Australia rule. Worth its own campaign rather than being smuggled into this one.
9. **Size data is thin across the private companies.** Employee counts are not published for most of the DTC list, and third-party data providers disagree where they do exist. Rather than pick a number, `companies.csv` records "not disclosed" and puts verifiable member, download or location counts in `key_features`. Sizing for Sales Navigator work will need a separate pass.
