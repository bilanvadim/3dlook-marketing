## Essay (word count target: ≤525)

3DLOOK exists because measuring the human body accurately, at scale, over a phone camera, used to be
impossible. 3DLOOK turns two ordinary smartphone photos into a full 3D body model, 80+ body
measurements and body composition data, in under 45 seconds, at 96-97% accuracy against manual
measurement. I lead the company as CEO, and my job is to keep 3DLOOK focused on the outcomes our
customers need from that technology.

What matters to the businesses we serve is what the technology lets them do. A telehealth provider can
review a patient's body-mass index (BMI) alongside a scan result instead of relying on self-report.
UK Meds, an online pharmacy, uses that same approach for BMI verification inside its prescribing
workflow, where the scan supplies data staff review rather than a decision made automatically. Yazen
built our platform into a weight-loss program and ran 34,000 scans through it in 2025. A uniform
manufacturer gets a garment fit right the first time instead of remaking it: Safariland has used our
platform in custom-fit PPE production for more than five years, and Burlington Medical uses it for
custom radiation aprons. In 2025, our customers ran 112,100 scans across 67 active accounts, and $822K
of our $1.084M in annual recurring revenue now comes from enterprise contracts rather than one-off
deals.

Two products carry that work. FitXpress serves telehealth, weight-loss and GLP-1 programs, online
pharmacies, insurers and employer wellness programs. Mobile Tailor serves apparel and uniform
manufacturers.

The part of this job I didn't expect is how much of running a health-data company is convincing
people, inside and outside it, that trust has to be earned continuously. I talk about this in board
meetings and on stages. I spoke at the GIANT Health Event in London in December about why AI lets care
move from reactive to preventive, and I write regularly about data trust as a board-level
responsibility, not only an engineering checklist. Every scan we process runs under handling aligned
with the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) and the General Data Protection
Regulation (GDPR), and I treat that as a competitive advantage worth explaining publicly, rather than
a line item buried in a footnote.

We are a 28-person team that has raised $16.2M and works with more than 100 companies across health
and apparel. Compared with the industries we sell into, insurers, pharmacies, uniform manufacturers,
hospital systems, we are small. I've learned that a small team earns trust from enterprise buyers by
being more precise about what our technology does and doesn't do than anyone asks us to be. 3DLOOK was
named to Sifted's 2020 Pioneers of the New World and won the IEEE Retail Digital Transformation Grand
Challenge.

Leading this company has taught me that the hardest part of scaling a body-data platform is building
an organization disciplined enough to say no to positioning claims we can't back, in an industry where
overstatement is common and the cost of getting it wrong lands on someone's medical decision or
someone's custom-fit uniform. That discipline is what I'm most proud of building at 3DLOOK, and it's
the standard I hold the team to every day.

## Bio (word count target: ≤125)

Katerina Galich is CEO of 3DLOOK Inc, a body-scanning technology company she leads across two
products: FitXpress, used by telehealth, pharmacy and insurance customers for verified body-mass
index (BMI) and body composition, and Mobile Tailor, used by apparel and uniform manufacturers for
custom-fit measurement. Under her leadership, 3DLOOK has grown to 67 active customers and $1.084M in
annual recurring revenue, with clients including UK Meds, Yazen, Safariland and Burlington Medical.
She speaks publicly on AI in preventive healthcare and on data trust as a board-level responsibility,
including a December talk at the GIANT Health Event in London. 3DLOOK has been named to Sifted's
Pioneers of the New World and won the IEEE Retail Digital Transformation Grand Challenge.
