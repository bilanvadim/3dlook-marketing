# 3DLOOK Health Content Strategy Guidelines (for Content-Writing Agents)

> **Source of truth:** [Content Strategy Guidelines Google Doc](https://docs.google.com/document/d/18twJmZ3wt2MRe9lQbfLhjJ9CwqKT60pzwCJMRpSrfvo/edit)
> **Companion:** [`content-plan.md`](./content-plan.md) — the hub/cluster editorial matrix these rules operate on.
> **Applies to:** all new/refreshed FitXpress health articles (fitness, telehealth, GLP-1, insurance, wellness, bariatrics, clinical trials, occupational health). Voice/claims discipline still comes from `about-me.md`; facts from `brand-assets/product-info/`; the 11 editorial guardrails from `brand-assets/style-guides/editorial-guardrails.md`. This file governs **strategic placement**: where a piece sits in the architecture and what job it does.
> **Last synced from source:** 2026-07-07.

## 1. Core principle

Every article must support the Health content architecture, not exist as an isolated blog post. Before drafting, identify the topic's **strategy row** in `content-plan.md`: main hub topic, cluster section, search intent, action type, execution priority, existing-published-article match, recommendation, cannibalization guardrail, seed-idea source.

**Do not start writing from the title alone.** A title without the strategy row is how we get five articles saying the same thing.

## 2. Hub-and-cluster structure

Each piece plays one role:

- **Main hub** — authority page for a vertical/theme: defines the category, the buyer problem, where FitXpress fits, and routes to supporting articles. Do not create a second broad article if a hub already exists or is planned. (Hubs: Health, Fitness, Telehealth, GLP-1, Insurance/BMI, Wellness, Bariatrics, Clinical Trials, Occupational Health.)
- **Supporting article** — answers one narrower buyer / workflow / comparison / implementation question. Must link back to its hub and to the most relevant FitXpress product or use-case page.
- **Lead magnet / checklist** — sales-enablement asset, not a thin indexed page. If short, embed in a hub, gate it, or expand into a full buyer guide.

## 3. Respect execution priority

- **P0** — strategic backbone (main hubs, cross-cluster trust assets, high-value BOFU). Write/refresh first; must be comprehensive, internally linked, positioning-aligned before scaling supporting content.
- **P1** — strong supporting content targeting a specific workflow/comparison/pain/ICP. Must not repeat hub content — deepen one angle.
- **P2** — conditional. Create only if search demand, sales conversations, campaigns, or internal priorities validate it. Prefer adding a hub section first.

## 4. Follow the action type (treat as a writing instruction)

- **Create net-new** — only when the topic is distinct and no strong existing article owns the intent. Check the published-URL field first; don't repeat its structure/claims.
- **Refresh / expand existing** — do NOT draft a new article. Improve the existing page (sections, examples, internal links, FAQs, CTAs, current positioning).
- **Review / decide** — decide first: new article / hub section / product-page section / lead magnet / deferred idea.
- **Section first** — add as a section inside the hub before any standalone (esp. when it could cannibalize the hub).
- **Lead magnet / sales asset** — checklist/guide/download; not a thin SEO article unless expanded.

## 5. Prevent cannibalization (mandatory)

Before drafting, ask: (1) Does an existing article already answer this? (2) Does the title overlap with a hub? (3) Is this broad enough to be a hub, or should it be a section? (4) Does the recommendation say refresh/section-first/do-not-duplicate? (5) What exact search intent should this page own?

If an existing page owns the main topic, the new article **must have a narrower angle**. Never copy an existing article's structure and reword it. Specific standing rules:
- No second generic "AI in Fitness" / "AI in Telehealth" article — refresh the hub.
- Don't duplicate Online-Pharmacy BMI Verification with a near-identical telehealth BMI article.
- Don't duplicate Visual Progress Tracking for GLP-1, Bariatric Pre-Qualification, or Beyond BMI with broad near-copies.
- No broad occupational-health articles until that hub is live and indexed.
- No per-vertical generic privacy/security articles — use the central Data/Privacy/Security/Regulatory FAQ.

## 6. Use existing published articles correctly

The "URL of already published articles" field means one of three things: **refresh target** (update it, don't create new), **internal-link source** (link naturally if related but different intent), or **cannibalization warning** (narrow the topic or make it a section). Do not rewrite an existing article with slightly different wording.

## 7. Match content depth to intent

- **TOFU / educational** — explain the concept, define terms, introduce the problem. Don't over-sell FitXpress early. (e.g. "Why Weight Alone Is Not Enough.")
- **MOFU** — compare approaches, explain workflows, show operational implications, help buyers evaluate. (e.g. "Smart Scale vs AI Body Scan," "Manual vs Digital Intake.")
- **BOFU** — show FitXpress fit, workflows, outputs, buyer value, implementation, CTA. (e.g. "Remote Body Measurement Workflows for Telehealth Providers.")
- **GEO / SEO comparison** — clear definitions, comparison tables, "which is better for what," use-case fit, limitations, FAQs, short summary blocks. **Never** make comparisons "FitXpress wins everything" — buyers and LLMs distrust that.

## 8. Position FitXpress carefully

FitXpress **is**: a mobile body-scanning solution · structured body-data capture layer · remote intake & documentation layer · workflow standardization tool · progress-tracking / scan-to-scan comparison layer · a tool that supports review, monitoring, documentation, operational efficiency.

FitXpress does **NOT** (never claim): diagnose conditions · make treatment decisions · replace clinicians · replace protocol-defined clinical endpoints · replace DEXA / validated reference methods universally · make underwriting decisions · make hiring decisions · make fitness-for-duty or clearance decisions · guarantee regulatory compliance · detect fraud automatically · serve as a standalone medical authority.

Precise language to use: "supports," "helps standardize," "provides structured records," "can support review," "where the workflow or protocol allows," "intake and documentation layer," "not a replacement for clinician review," "not a standalone decisioning system." **"Supports clinician review" is your friend.**

## 9. Vertical-specific boundaries

- **Fitness** — apps, digital coaching, recomposition, progress visibility, engagement, retention. Don't blur into wellness rewards or GLP-1 clinical workflows.
- **Telehealth** — remote-care workflows, patient experience, documentation, privacy, remote monitoring. Keep separate from GLP-1 eligibility & online-pharmacy compliance unless explicitly the bridge.
- **GLP-1** — medication-supported weight-loss progress, body composition, records, clinic workflows, baseline data. Don't duplicate the GLP-1 market / visual-progress / Beyond-BMI / online-pharmacy-BMI pages.
- **Insurance** — underwriting-support, BMI/build verification, applicant experience, documentation, consent. No automated underwriting, fraud detection, employment screening, or decisioning.
- **Wellness** — engagement, personalization, progress visibility, rewards, coaching. Softer claims; no medical/diagnostic/clearance/clinical-decision language.
- **Bariatrics** — pre-qualification, pre-auth documentation, obesity-care intake, post-op progress, records. Don't duplicate generic GLP-1/telehealth/body-comp pages.
- **Clinical Trials** — protocol-defined anthropometric workflows, hybrid/decentralized trials, sponsor/CRO workflows, site variability, participant burden, structured records, audit readiness. Don't blur into telehealth/bariatric care; don't imply endpoint validation or replacement of protocol-defined reference methods.
- **Occupational Health** — pre-employment/pre-placement/return-to-work intake, fit-for-duty documentation support, rescreens, multi-site consistency, workforce screening vendors, workers'-comp/absence workflows. Don't imply hiring/eligibility/clearance/diagnosis/fitness-for-duty decisioning.

## 10. Central trust assets

- **Accuracy framework** (`mobile-body-scanning-accuracy`) — canonical source for accuracy framing. Always clarify: accuracy for which decision? which reference method? which capture protocol? which population? which workflow? what tolerance? Is the real issue accuracy, repeatability, robustness, validation, or output fit? **Never reduce accuracy to one universal number.**
- **Data, Privacy, Security & Regulatory FAQ** (planned, P0) — canonical source for stored data, photos, 3D mesh, measurements, server geography, retention, deletion, data ownership, HIPAA/GDPR/CCPA/SOC 2/FDA. Vertical pages carry short notes that link here, not duplicate answers. **Compliance claims must be approved by legal, product, and security before publishing.**

## 11. Internal linking (four directions, where relevant)

- **Up** to the hub (every supporting article).
- **Sideways** to related clusters where the reader journey overlaps (e.g. GLP-1 ↔ telehealth/bariatrics/fitness).
- **Down** to BOFU / FitXpress product pages (educational & MOFU route serious buyers).
- **To trust assets** whenever discussing accuracy, privacy, security, FDA/HIPAA/GDPR/CCPA/SOC 2, retention, or deletion.

## 12. Recommended article structure

1. Clear opening stating the buyer problem
2. Short answer / definition section
3. Why this matters now
4. Workflow / use-case explanation
5. Where FitXpress fits
6. What improves operationally
7. What FitXpress does **not** do
8. Comparison table or decision framework (if relevant)
9. Buyer / ICP fit
10. Implementation or evaluation considerations
11. FAQs
12. CTA

For sensitive verticals (telehealth, GLP-1, insurance, bariatrics, clinical trials, occupational health) include a clear **scope note early**.

## 13. Tone and claims discipline

Clear, specific, useful for B2B buyers. Avoid hype: "revolutionary," "game-changing," "transforming everything," "AI-powered future," "unlocking limitless possibilities." Use operational language instead: reduces manual intake · standardizes capture · supports review · creates structured records · improves documentation consistency · reduces rework · supports scan-to-scan comparison · supports remote workflows. If a claim touches compliance, medical use, employment, underwriting, clinical trials, or security — write cautiously and route for expert review. (This complements — does not replace — `about-me.md` voice and `editorial-guardrails.md`.)

## 14. FAQ requirements

Every major article includes FAQs answering real search & procurement questions: What is [concept]? · How does [workflow] work? · Can [body scanning] replace [manual method / DEXA / exam]? · What data is captured? · Is this used for decisioning? · Who reviews the data? · How does this reduce rescreens/rework/site burden? · Where does FitXpress fit in the workflow? · What does FitXpress not do? For GEO/AEO, answers are concise and direct (2–5 sentences).

## 15. Conversion guidance (CTA by intent)

- **TOFU** — soft CTAs ("Learn how FitXpress supports structured body data capture," "Explore how mobile body scanning works").
- **MOFU** — evaluation CTAs ("Compare body data workflows," "See how FitXpress supports remote progress tracking," "Review the buyer checklist").
- **BOFU** — direct CTAs ("Book a FitXpress demo," "Talk to 3DLOOK about your workflow," "Explore FitXpress for telehealth and weight-loss").

Do not force the same CTA into every article — match reader readiness.

## 16. Quality checklist before submission

- [ ] Belongs to the correct main hub
- [ ] Follows the action type
- [ ] Does not duplicate an existing published page
- [ ] Follows the cannibalization guardrail
- [ ] Uses the correct vertical boundary
- [ ] Links to the right hub, BOFU page, and trust assets
- [ ] Includes FAQs
- [ ] Avoids unsupported medical / legal / regulatory / underwriting / employment / clinical-trial claims
- [ ] Has a clear CTA matching intent
- [ ] Answers one distinct search intent
- [ ] Useful for both humans and AI answer systems

The goal is not to publish more — it is to publish content with a clear job, a clear place in the cluster, and a clear path to commercial relevance.
