Guide
3DLOOK Health Content Strategy Guidelines for Content Writing Agents
1. Core principle
Every article must support the Health content architecture, not exist as an isolated blog post.
The goal is to build a structured content system around FitXpress and 3DLOOK’s body-data capabilities across health, fitness, telehealth, GLP-1, insurance, wellness, bariatrics, clinical trials, and occupational health.
Before drafting any article, identify:
* Main hub topic
* Cluster section
* Search intent
* Action type
* Execution priority
* Existing published article match
* Recommendation
* Cannibalization guardrail
* Seed idea source
Do not start writing from the title alone. A title without the strategy row is how we get five articles saying the same thing, which is technically publishing, but spiritually vandalism.

2. Use the hub-and-cluster structure
Each piece must belong to one of these content roles:
Main hub
A hub is the main authority page for a vertical or strategic theme. It should explain the category, define the buyer problem, show where FitXpress fits, and route readers to supporting articles.
Current or planned hub areas include:
* AI Body Data for Health, Fitness, Telehealth, Insurance, Occupational Health, and Clinical Research
* AI in Fitness
* AI in Telehealth
* GLP-1 Market Growth and Patient Progress Tracking
* Insurance Underwriting and BMI Verification
* Wellness Platforms
* Bariatric Pre-Qualification and Patient Progress Tracking
* Hybrid and Decentralized Obesity Trials
* Occupational Health Screening
Do not create a second broad article if a hub already exists or is planned.
Supporting article
A supporting article should answer one narrower buyer question, workflow question, comparison question, or implementation question.
Examples:
* Manual Intake vs Digital Intake in Occupational Health Screening
* What CROs Should Ask Before Using Remote Body Measurement Tools
* Remote Body Measurement Workflows for Telehealth Providers
* Top 7 Remote Body Composition Tools for GLP-1 Clinics
A supporting article must link back to its hub and to the most relevant FitXpress product or use-case page.
Lead magnet / checklist
A checklist or buyer guide should usually be treated as a sales-enablement asset, not a thin indexed blog post.
Use this format for:
* Buyer’s checklists
* Vendor evaluation guides
* Clinical trial checklists
* Occupational health intake checklists
* Insurance BMI verification checklists
If the asset is short, do not publish it as a standalone SEO page. Embed it in a hub, gate it, or expand it into a full buyer guide.

3. Respect execution priority
P0
P0 content is strategic backbone content. It should be written or refreshed first.
P0 content includes main hubs, cross-cluster trust assets, high-value BOFU topics, and pages that support multiple verticals.
Examples:
* Main Health hub
* Body scanning accuracy framework
* Data, Privacy, Security & Regulatory FAQ
* AI in Fitness hub refresh
* AI in Telehealth hub refresh
* GLP-1 hub refresh
* Occupational Health hub
* Clinical Trials hub
P0 articles must be comprehensive, internally linked, and aligned with positioning before supporting content is scaled.
P1
P1 content is strong supporting content. It usually targets a specific workflow, comparison, buyer pain, or ICP.
Examples:
* Remote Body Measurement for Online Fitness Coaching Programs
* Manual Intake vs Digital Intake in Occupational Health Screening
* What CROs Should Ask Before Using Remote Body Measurement Tools
* Top Insurance Tech Companies
* Top Mobile Body Scanning Software for Wellness Apps
P1 articles should not repeat hub content. They must deepen one specific angle.
P2
P2 content is conditional. Create it only if search demand, sales conversations, campaigns, or internal priorities validate it.
Examples:
* Progress visibility as a retention lever
* Some buyer checklists
* Niche implementation articles
* Some “section first, standalone later” topics
For P2 topics, consider adding a section to the hub first before creating a standalone page.

4. Follow the action type
Each topic has an action type. Treat it as a writing instruction.
Create net-new
Write a new article only when the table says the topic is distinct enough and no strong existing article already owns the intent.
Before writing, check the “URL of already published articles” field and avoid repeating their structure or claims.
Refresh / expand existing
Do not draft a new article. Improve the existing page.
Add missing sections, update examples, strengthen internal links, add FAQs, improve CTAs, and align the page with the current FitXpress positioning.
Review / decide
Do not automatically create the article.
First decide whether the topic should be:
* A new article
* A section in the hub
* A section in a product page
* A lead magnet
* A deferred idea
Section first
Add the topic as a section inside the hub before creating a standalone article.
This is especially important for topics that could cannibalize an existing hub.
Lead magnet / sales asset
Write as a checklist, guide, or downloadable asset. Do not publish as a thin SEO article unless expanded into a full buyer guide.

5. Prevent cannibalization
Cannibalization prevention is mandatory.
Before drafting, ask:
1. Does an existing article already answer this same question?
2. Does the title overlap with an existing hub?
3. Is this topic broad enough to be a hub, or should it be a section?
4. Does the recommendation say “refresh,” “section first,” or “do not duplicate”?
5. What exact search intent should this page own?
If an existing page already owns the main topic, the new article must have a narrower angle.
For example:
* Do not create another generic “AI in Fitness” article. Refresh the AI in Fitness hub.
* Do not create another generic “AI in Telehealth” article. Refresh the AI in Telehealth page.
* Do not duplicate Online Pharmacy BMI Verification with a near-identical telehealth BMI article.
* Do not duplicate Visual Progress Tracking for GLP-1 with another generic GLP-1 engagement article.
* Do not duplicate Bariatric Pre-Qualification with another broad bariatric intake article.
* Do not create broad occupational health articles until the Occupational Health hub is live and indexed.
* Do not create separate generic privacy/security articles for every vertical. Use the central Data, Privacy, Security & Regulatory FAQ.
Every article must have one clear job.

6. Use existing published articles correctly
The “URL of already published articles” field tells the agent what already exists.
Use it in three ways:
As the page to refresh
If the strategy says refresh or expand, update that existing article rather than creating a new one.
As a source of internal links
If the existing article is related but not the same intent, link to it naturally.
As a cannibalization warning
If the existing article is too close, narrow the new topic or convert it into a section.
Do not copy the existing article’s structure and rewrite it with slightly different wording. That is not content strategy. That is a plagiarism costume party.

7. Match content depth to intent
TOFU / educational
Use for broad awareness topics.
The article should explain the concept clearly, define terms, and introduce the problem. It should not over-sell FitXpress too early.
Examples:
* AI Fitness Progress Tracking: Why Weight Alone Is Not Enough
* Wellness Progress Tracking: Why Weight Alone Is Not Enough
* Body Composition Tracking for GLP-1 Patients
MOFU
Use for problem-aware and solution-aware readers.
The article should compare approaches, explain workflows, show operational implications, and help buyers evaluate options.
Examples:
* Smart Scale vs AI Body Scan
* Manual Intake vs Digital Intake
* How Mobile Body Scanning Improves Patient Engagement
BOFU
Use for buyer-ready, operational, and commercial content.
The article should show FitXpress fit, workflows, outputs, buyer value, implementation considerations, and CTA.
Examples:
* Remote Body Measurement Workflows for Telehealth Providers
* What CROs Should Ask Before Using Remote Body Measurement Tools
* Return-to-Work Screening Documentation
* How Insurers Can Create More Consistent Body Data Records
GEO / SEO comparison
Use direct, answer-friendly structures:
* Clear definitions
* Comparison tables
* “Which is better for what?”
* Use-case fit
* Limitations
* FAQs
* Short summary blocks
Do not turn comparison articles into “FitXpress wins everything.” Buyers and LLMs both distrust that, as they should.

8. Position FitXpress carefully
FitXpress should be positioned as:
* A mobile body scanning solution
* A structured body-data capture layer
* A remote intake and documentation layer
* A workflow standardization tool
* A progress tracking and scan-to-scan comparison layer
* A tool that supports review, monitoring, documentation, and operational efficiency
Avoid claiming that FitXpress:
* Diagnoses conditions
* Makes treatment decisions
* Replaces clinicians
* Replaces protocol-defined clinical endpoints
* Replaces DEXA or validated reference methods universally
* Makes underwriting decisions
* Makes hiring decisions
* Makes fitness-for-duty or clearance decisions
* Guarantees regulatory compliance
* Detects fraud automatically
* Serves as a standalone medical authority
Use precise language:
* “supports”
* “helps standardize”
* “provides structured records”
* “can support review”
* “where the workflow or protocol allows”
* “intake and documentation layer”
* “not a replacement for clinician review”
* “not a standalone decisioning system”
The phrase “supports clinician review” is your friend. Treat it kindly.

9. Follow vertical-specific boundaries
Fitness
Owns fitness apps, digital coaching, body recomposition, progress visibility, engagement, retention, and app features.
Do not blur too much into wellness rewards or GLP-1 clinical workflows.
Telehealth
Owns remote-care workflows, patient experience, documentation, privacy, and remote monitoring.
Keep telehealth separate from GLP-1 eligibility and online pharmacy compliance unless the article is explicitly about the bridge.
GLP-1
Owns medication-supported weight-loss progress tracking, body composition, patient records, clinic workflows, baseline data, and tools for GLP-1 clinics.
Do not duplicate the GLP-1 market page, visual progress page, Beyond BMI page, or online pharmacy BMI verification article.
Insurance
Owns underwriting-support workflows, BMI/build verification, applicant experience, documentation, and consent.
Do not imply automated underwriting, fraud detection, employment screening, or decisioning.
Wellness
Owns engagement, personalization, progress visibility, rewards, wellness apps, employee wellness, and coaching.
Keep claims softer. Avoid medical, diagnostic, clearance, or clinical decisioning language.
Bariatrics
Owns bariatric pre-qualification, pre-auth documentation, obesity-care intake, post-op progress, and patient records.
Do not duplicate generic GLP-1, telehealth, or body-composition pages.
Clinical Trials
Owns protocol-defined anthropometric measurement workflows, hybrid and decentralized obesity trials, sponsor/CRO workflows, site variability, participant burden, structured records, and audit readiness.
Do not blur into telehealth care delivery or bariatric clinical care. Do not imply endpoint validation or replacement of protocol-defined reference methods.
Occupational Health
Owns pre-employment intake, pre-placement intake, return-to-work intake, fit-for-duty documentation support, rescreens, multi-site consistency, workforce screening vendors, workers’ compensation, and absence workflows.
Do not imply hiring decisions, employment eligibility decisions, clearance decisions, diagnosis, or fitness-for-duty decisioning.

10. Use the central trust assets
Two cross-cluster trust assets should support the whole strategy.
Accuracy framework
Use the mobile body scanning accuracy article as the central source for accuracy framing.
When discussing accuracy, always clarify:
* Accuracy for which decision?
* Compared with which reference method?
* Under which capture protocol?
* For which population?
* For which workflow?
* With what tolerance?
* Is the real issue accuracy, repeatability, robustness, validation, or output fit?
Do not reduce accuracy to one universal number.
Data, Privacy, Security & Regulatory FAQ
Use this planned FAQ as the canonical source for:
* Stored data
* Photos
* 3D mesh
* Measurements
* Server geography
* Retention
* Photo deletion
* User deletion requests
* Deletion endpoints
* Data ownership
* HIPAA
* GDPR
* CCPA
* SOC 2
* FDA approval
* Whether FDA approval is required
Vertical pages may include short privacy notes, but they should link to the central FAQ. Do not create separate generic privacy articles that repeat the same answers.
Compliance claims must be approved by legal, product, and security teams before publishing.

11. Internal linking rules
Every article must include internal links in four directions where relevant:
Link up to the hub
Every supporting article links back to its main hub.
Link sideways to related clusters
Use cross-links when the reader journey naturally overlaps.
Examples:
* GLP-1 article links to telehealth, bariatrics, and fitness where relevant.
* Occupational health article may link to privacy FAQ and accuracy framework, not to unrelated wellness pages.
* Clinical trials article may link to DEXA comparison, body scanning accuracy, and structured body data pages.
Link down to BOFU pages
Educational and MOFU articles should route serious buyers to FitXpress pages.
Link to trust assets
When discussing accuracy, privacy, security, FDA, HIPAA, GDPR, CCPA, SOC 2, data retention, or deletion, link to the relevant central trust asset.

2. Short answer or definition section
3. Why this matters now

5. Where FitXpress fits
6. What improves operationally
7. What FitXpress does not do

13. Required tone and claims discipline
The tone should be clear, specific, and useful for B2B buyers. Avoid hype.
Do not use vague AI language such as:
* “revolutionary”
* “game-changing”
* “transforming everything”
* “AI-powered future”
* “unlocking limitless possibilities”
Use operational language instead:
* reduces manual intake
* standardizes capture
* supports review
* creates structured records
* improves documentation consistency
* reduces rework
* supports scan-to-scan comparison
* helps teams evaluate progress
* supports remote workflows
* improves data availability before review
Do not overstate clinical or regulatory claims. If a claim touches compliance, medical use, employment, underwriting, clinical trials, or security, write cautiously and route for expert review.
________________


14. FAQ requirements
Every major article should include FAQs that answer real search and procurement questions.
Good FAQ types:
* What is [concept]?
* How does [workflow] work?
* Can [body scanning] replace [manual method / DEXA / exam]?
* What data is captured?
* Is this used for decisioning?
* Who reviews the data?
* How does this reduce rescreens / rework / site burden?
* Where does FitXpress fit in the workflow?
* What does FitXpress not do?
For GEO/AEO, answers should be concise and direct, usually 2 to 5 sentences.

15. Conversion guidance
Each article needs a CTA that matches intent.
TOFU
Use soft CTAs:
* Learn how FitXpress supports structured body data capture.
* Explore how mobile body scanning works.
MOFU
Use evaluation CTAs:
* Compare body data workflows.
* See how FitXpress supports remote progress tracking.
* Review the buyer checklist.
BOFU
Use direct CTAs:
* Book a FitXpress demo.
* Talk to 3DLOOK about your workflow.
* Explore FitXpress for telehealth and weight-loss.
* Explore FitXpress for connected and digital fitness.
Do not force the same CTA into every article. Yes, shocking: readers have different levels of readiness.

16. Quality checklist before submission
Before delivering a draft, confirm:
* The article belongs to the correct main hub.
* The article follows the action type.
* The article does not duplicate an existing published page.
* The article follows the cannibalization guardrail.
* The article uses the correct vertical boundary.
* The article links to the right hub, BOFU page, and trust assets.
* The article includes FAQs.
* The article avoids unsupported medical, legal, regulatory, underwriting, employment, or clinical-trial claims.
* The article has a clear CTA.
* The article answers one distinct search intent.
* The article is useful for both humans and AI answer systems.
The goal is not to publish more. The goal is to publish content that has a clear job, a clear place in the cluster, and a clear path to commercial relevance.
