	Relationships 
	Write relationships explicitly. Avoid compressed business or technical phrasing that makes the reader decode the relationship.  
	When one requirement varies based on another factor, use clear constructions such as “depends on,” “varies by,” or “is determined by.” 
	Example:
* Avoid: “The historical records the feature needs scale to what is being compared.”
* Prefer: “The historical records required depend on the type of comparison.”
	Audience’s presumed reaction
	Focus on the concept, not on the audience’s presumed reaction. Define the issue directly and name its components.
	Avoid phrases such as “what trips people up,” “the mistake buyers make,” or “what most teams misunderstand.” 
	Example:
* Avoid: “What trips up most procurement reviews is treating ‘who owns the data’ as one question, when it is really five.”
* Prefer: “‘Data ownership’ involves several distinct rights: individual privacy rights, customer contractual rights, processing rights, intellectual-property rights, and rights to generated outputs.”

	Corrective negation
	Avoid constructions such as “X, not Y” when they may sound corrective, dismissive, or overly instructive. Lead with the recommended approach and explain its purpose or benefit. 
	Always
	Use negation when it communicates a necessary product, clinical, legal, or regulatory boundary. Preferred: “This explanation should be included in the patient-facing flow, where patients can access it when needed. A policy document can provide supplementary detail.” Acceptable exception: “FitXpress supports clinician review; it is not a diagnostic tool.” 
	Contrast using “rather than”
	Avoid using “rather than” to frame one format, capability, workflow, or outcome through corrective contrast with another. State the primary characteristic directly, then explain any limitation or difference in a separate clause or sentence.
	When the contrast may sound dismissive, imply that the alternative is inherently inferior, or require the reader to interpret the intended relationship.
	Use “rather than” when it describes a necessary clinical, legal, regulatory, or product boundary, or a genuine user choice that cannot be stated as clearly without contrast. Avoid: “Results typically arrive as a report rather than a live data feed.” Prefer: “Results typically arrive as a PDF report or portal export. Live data-feed availability depends on the provider and integration.”
	Internal content-cluster labels
	Replace internal planning terms with reader-facing language that identifies the concrete topic, question, workflow, or decision covered by the section.
	Apply when terms such as bridge, hub, pillar, cluster, or supporting content describe the page’s role in the content architecture rather than a concept used by the intended audience.
	Retain a term when it has an established meaning within the relevant industry or accurately describes a visible website feature. Example: replace “The GLP-1 bridge: current BMI, historical BMI, and documentation continuity” with “How does prior GLP-1 treatment affect BMI documentation at bariatric intake?”

	Positioned as
	When describing 3DLOOK, its products, technology, outputs, intended use, or regulatory status
	Only when discussing explicit market positioning, messaging strategy, or competitive positioning
	When defining what the product is, does, supports, replaces, or is intended to be used for
	“Positioned as” suggests that the product’s identity, intended use, or regulatory status depends on external perception rather than on 3DLOOK’s defined product scope and claims. It can also make a factual product boundary sound like a marketing choice.

	Buyer / customer
	All corporate content
	Use buyer only when discussing procurement, buying criteria, or vendor evaluation. Use customer when referring to an established contractual relationship, deployment responsibility, or legal role.
	When either term merely labels the intended audience, assumes an existing commercial relationship, or replaces the specific organization responsible for an action.
	These terms can expose the marketing framework instead of describing the operational reality. “Customer” can also imply that the organization has already selected 3DLOOK.
	Name the actual actor or context: organization, program, provider, clinic, employer, operator, procurement team, decision-maker, or person being screened.
	Body metrics / body measurements
	Whenever describing numerical body-related data or FitXpress outputs 
	Use body metrics as the umbrella category for numerical body-related outputs. Use body measurements specifically for anthropometric dimensions, such as circumferences, lengths, and widths. Describe BMI, basal metabolic rate, and body composition estimates as metrics or calculated/estimated outputs. 
	Using body metrics and body measurements interchangeably; describing BMI, body composition, or basal metabolic rate as body measurements; replacing the approved “80+ body measurements” claim with “80+ body metrics.” 
	The terms represent different levels of classification. Conflating them obscures which outputs are anthropometric dimensions and which are calculated or estimated values. 
	Use the precise category: body measurements, calculated metrics, body composition estimates, or the umbrella term body metrics when referring to multiple output types.
