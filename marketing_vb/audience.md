# audience.md — who 3DLOOK / FitXpress health content is for

> Read before every writing task. This file defines *who* we write for. Voice and claims discipline live in `about-me.md`. Scope: the seven FitXpress **health** segments. Method: apply the shared spine, then the layer for the relevant segment.

## The buyer in one line
B2B decision-makers at digital-health, telehealth, GLP-1, insurance, wellness, fitness, and clinical/care organizations who need **trustworthy body data collected remotely, at scale** — without it becoming a medical, eligibility, or decisioning claim.

## Shared spine — true across every segment

**What they have in common**
- They run remote-first or high-volume workflows where in-person measurement doesn't scale.
- They don't trust self-reported body data (weight, BMI, build): it's inaccurate, outdated, or intentionally misrepresented.
- Their unit economics are defined by CAC pressure plus retention/outcomes — not by one-time acquisition.
- They must show measurable, *defensible* outcomes to third parties: payers, employers, insurers, regulators.
- Compliance and privacy (HIPAA, GDPR, SOC 2) are procurement gates, not nice-to-haves.
- They are wary of hype and of "AI replaces the expert." They want a support layer with clear boundaries, not a black box.
- They read like buyers doing diligence: they want references, disclosed limitations, and workflow fit — not adjectives.

**What they want from us**
Structured, repeatable body data from a smartphone; API/SDK integration with a clear "we provide / you build" boundary; honesty about what it does and doesn't do; and evidence of validation and privacy posture.

**Common objections → our honest answer pattern**
- *"They already self-report weight/BMI."* → Self-report is inconsistent and easy to misrepresent; we add objective, repeatable, structured data before review.
- *"We already use (connected) scales."* → A scale gives one number; we add composition, 80+ measurements, a 3D model, and longitudinal comparison.
- *"We don't want onboarding friction."* → Two photos, guided capture, under a minute.
- *"Our clinicians/underwriters assess manually."* → We standardize intake and cut review burden; the human still decides.
- *"How does this help the business?"* → Better qualification/engagement → conversion, retention, and cleaner outcomes reporting.

**Decision-maker roles (across segments):** Founders/CEOs; CMOs and Medical Directors; Head of Clinical Operations; Head of Member Engagement/Retention; Head of Outcomes/Program Insights; Product Managers/CTOs; Compliance & Risk; Growth/RevOps; Care Coordination.

**Geographies:** US (core), UK, Canada, Germany, UAE, Australia. The UK is compliance-sensitive (tightening scrutiny of remote GLP-1 prescribing).

## Segment layers — what changes per vertical
Each layer: *who · core pain · the hook that lands · what NOT to say.*

### 1. Telehealth & Weight-Loss (GLP-1) — the core segment
- **Who:** virtual-first clinics, GLP-1 programs, metabolic/obesity platforms running longitudinal 30/60/90-day programs; typically 500+ active members with repeat check-ins.
- **Core pain:** members drop off when progress isn't visible; weight alone is not enough; repeat check-ins are a churn risk rather than an engagement engine.
- **Hook:** make body progress *visible* → repeat check-ins → adherence → retention. Repeatability (`< 1 cm`) so small real changes aren't lost in measurement noise. Longitudinal, defensible outcomes for payer/employer partners.
- **Don't:** diagnostic claims; positioning as a DEXA or calibrated-scale replacement; implying eligibility decisioning. Keep separate from UK online-pharmacy BMI compliance unless the piece is explicitly the bridge.

### 2. Weight & BMI Verification (UK online pharmacy / remote prescribing)
- **Who:** GLP-1 telehealth providers, online pharmacies, and remote prescribers operating in the UK regulated market.
- **Core pain:** patients misreport BMI to qualify; manual photo review is subjective and inconsistent; UK regulators are tightening remote GLP-1 eligibility scrutiny.
- **Hook:** objective, defensible BMI/build verification *before* clinician review; audit-ready documentation; anti-spoof controls (live capture, pose validation, clothing detection).
- **Don't:** imply automated eligibility or prescribing decisions; guarantee compliance. Say "supports compliant workflows," never "makes you compliant."

### 3. Connected & Digital Fitness
- **Who:** fitness apps, digital/virtual coaching, connected-fitness platforms, corporate wellness. Subscription retention is the primary KPI.
- **Core pain:** churn without visible progress; personalization limited to surveys/goals; a crowded market competing on UX.
- **Hook:** visible transformation plus body-data personalization → engagement, retention, and premium-tier monetization. Tone is lighter and less clinical here.
- **Don't:** medical, diagnostic, or clearance language. Don't blur into GLP-1 clinical or wellness-rewards workflows.

### 4. Wellness Rewards Verification (employers & insurers)
- **Who:** enterprise employers, health plans, Medicare Advantage providers, wellness/benefits platforms, population-health vendors.
- **Core pain:** manual wellness verification is an administrative burden; self-report is unreliable; hybrid/remote workforces complicate onsite screening; incentive fairness and auditability are at risk.
- **Hook:** remote, standardized, audit-ready verification; fraud-prevention *support*; higher participation; fairer, more consistent incentives across distributed populations.
- **Don't:** medical/diagnostic claims; implying the reward or eligibility decision is automated. Keep claims soft.

### 5. Insurance Underwriting
- **Who:** life and health insurers, insurtech, accelerated/digital underwriting teams, preventive and wellness-linked programs.
- **Core pain:** self-reported build/BMI misrepresentation (the second-largest driver of misclassification in accelerated underwriting, after smoking non-disclosure); in-person exams slow accelerated journeys.
- **Hook:** remote structured body data as *supporting evidence*; faster triage; fraud-prevention support; an auditable per-case trail. Ground it in CDC / Munich Re / Swiss Re / NAIC / LIMRA data.
- **Don't:** automated underwriting, automatic fraud detection, standalone decisioning, or employment screening. Frame as "supporting evidence for underwriter review — not standalone decisioning."

### 6. BCRL / Oncology Survivorship
- **Who:** oncology survivorship programs, breast-cancer aftercare clinics, remote-patient-monitoring providers, cancer-care networks, lymphedema/rehab teams.
- **Core pain:** BCRL is underdiagnosed early; tape measurement is inconsistent and hard to reproduce at home; in-clinic monitoring doesn't scale; long-term self-monitoring adherence is weak.
- **Hook:** remote longitudinal monitoring of subtle volumetric/asymmetry change; reproducible digital body records; engagement through visual 3D comparison. This is the most compliance-sensitive vertical — lead with a clear scope note and disclaimer.
- **Don't:** claim to detect or diagnose BCRL/lymphedema; replace clinical assessment. Say "supports monitoring workflows," never "detects lymphedema."

### 7. Plastic Surgery
- **Who:** aesthetic and plastic-surgery clinics (Turkey, US, UK, Germany, UAE), body-contouring specialists, high international/medical-tourism volume.
- **Core pain:** consultation-to-surgery drop-off from last-minute BMI disqualification; wasted consult slots on unqualified patients; no reliable remote pre-screen.
- **Hook:** remote pre-qualification (BMI, body composition) before travel or consult; fewer no-shows and disqualifications; better input for surgical planning.
- **Don't:** diagnostic or anesthesia-risk claims; replacing surgeon assessment. Frame as support for pre-screening and planning.

## How to use this file
1. Identify the segment first, then apply the shared spine + that segment's layer.
2. Respect vertical boundaries (per content strategy): don't let telehealth bleed into pharmacy compliance, fitness into GLP-1 clinical workflows, or wellness into clinical decisioning.
3. Match depth to funnel stage — TOFU educational, MOFU comparison/workflow, BOFU operational/commercial.
4. When in doubt on a claim, default to the softer, support-layer framing and route for review.
